What you can animate ​

Every hit lands on the real target

Animate a punch, a throw or a fireball once against stand-ins. In game, Motion Warp fits the path between where it really starts and where the target really is, so an arc stays an arc at any range.

Cast by role

Actors are roles. Your game passes in the characters, or a role fills itself with the local player, the nearest player or a tagged object.

Constraints that follow the action

Track To, Damped Track, Lock, Floor, Limit Rotation and more run live in game. A camera aims at whoever plays the target.

Drivers read the live scene

Distance, Speed, Copy and Property drivers set a value from something else: a camera's FieldOfView from how far a character is, a light's Brightness from an attribute your game sets.

Motion without keys

Shake, Wiggle, Oscillate, Flicker, Recoil and other generators move a camera, a joint or a light over a span. They're seeded, so every player sees the same shake.

The whole moment in one scene

A grab and slam, a cutscene or a summon keeps every rig, camera, effect and sound on one Timeline, timed against each other.

VFX and sound on cue

Emit() bursts, trails and lights preview as you scrub. Sound bars show waveforms, and keys snap to the loud hits.

Direct the camera

Camera actors pose like any other actor. Look through one with Q, cut or blend between shots on the Camera track, and key letterbox bars, blur and fades on the screen.

Any property of any instance

Key colours, gradients, sequences, attributes and text from the Inspector, with an editor for each type.

Curves and Ease

Bezier keys and tangents, a Channels view, and one Ease curve for everything selected, with saved presets.

Layers

Override and Additive layers over the whole scene, each with a weight curve. Your game can change a layer's weight while it plays.

Versions and undo

Every edit is one Studio undo step. Saved versions let you preview, compare, restore or branch an earlier scene.

Posing that leaves your joints alone

The gizmo never rewrites your joints. You get pins, context IK, joint limits, mirror posing, onion skin and motion trails.

An MIT runtime

Games play scenes straight from their data, with no upload. PlayForAll keeps every player in sync, late joiners included.

Native export

A scene that only moves one rig's joints exports as a KeyframeSequence or CurveAnimation and saves to Roblox from inside the plugin.

Who it's for ​

Combat and ability devs

Grabs and projectiles that land on the real target, with VFX and a hit frame that fires on the server.

Cutscene makers

Several actors, cameras and sound, played for everyone at once.

Pro animators

The curves, tangents, onion skin and trails you know from Blender or Maya, inside Studio.

Beginners

It looks like Studio. Every button has a tooltip, and a disabled button says why.

Play it in three lines ​

luau
local AcesMotion = require(game.ReplicatedStorage.AcesMotion)

local track = AcesMotion.Play("Slam", { Attacker = player.Character, Target = enemy })
track.MarkerReached:Connect(function(marker) if marker == "Hit" then dealDamage(enemy) end end)

The runtime is a small, MIT licensed ModuleScript the plugin installs for you. PlayForAll plays a scene for every player in sync with the server clock, late joiners included, while markers and events fire in your server code.
