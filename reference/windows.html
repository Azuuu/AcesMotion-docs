Pointer position matters

While the pointer is over the Timeline or the Hub (or the Cast tab popped into its own window), or one of them has focus, Studio's own selection is set aside. Studio's Delete, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+D then cannot touch your rigs. It comes back when the pointer leaves.

What action code sees ​

  • Action code gets the scene's time on its context: context.Time (scene seconds), context.Frame (scene frame), context.Fps, and context.TimeScale.
  • context.TimeScale is how fast the scene plays: SetSpeed in games, the Timeline's playback speed in Studio, negative backwards.
  • These sit beside context.Seconds (time into the block). They are current before Run and on every Scrub, in Studio and in games.
  • Hovering a block shows its module's Description first (Event: what it sends), so developers can explain their actions to each other.
  • Each param's Description becomes its tooltip in the Inspector.
  • A param a module gained after its blocks were made arrives with its Default (in Studio and in games).

Editing action code

Each preview run loads the action fresh in Studio: its current code and its own child modules. Edits apply on the next run, and module-level variables start clean.

Other modules it requires (a sibling, a shared util) are loaded once, like in a game. An edit to one of those applies after the plugin reloads (Studio reloads it on the next start, or reinstall the plugin).

Renaming or moving an action module keeps its blocks. Each module gets a permanent id (AcesMotion_ActionId attribute) that its blocks remember, in Studio and in games.

Ruler ​

The 52 px strip over the lanes. It shows frame or second labels, the playhead chip (the frame number, or "0.70s"), markers at the top, the loop region bar, the export region bracket, and a handle at the scene end.

Scrubbing and panning ​

  • Press and drag anywhere on the ruler that is not a marker, region or the scene end to scrub.
  • The playhead snaps to markers, keys, regions and sound peaks (when those snap targets are on), and otherwise lands on whole frames.
  • Hold Alt to flip Snap for the scrub. Whole frames hold unless Snap > Frames is off.
  • Dragging over a sound plays short audio snippets (Settings > Audio).
  • Middle drag, or Space + drag, on the ruler pans time.

In Seconds mode the ruler labels as densely as in frames: the smallest of 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10 s (and up) whose labels stay at least 56 px apart, so a 2 s scene at a wide zoom reads 0.1s, 0.2s, 0.3s... Labels drop trailing zeros (0.1s, 0.25s, 1s). Ticks subdivide the labels, a faint lane line marks every tenth of a second, and a stronger line every half second (or every label, when labels are wider apart). The Graph's grid follows the same marks.

Several markers ​

  • Ctrl+click or Shift+click markers (on the ruler or on actor rows) to pick them together.
  • Dragging one moves them all by the same amount.
  • Del or the menu's Delete N markers removes them all. Colour and Hold set them all. Each is one undo step.
  • A plain click on a picked marker keeps the others picked so you can drag them. A click on another marker picks just that one.
  • Box-select markers: Shift-drag along the ruler's empty strip picks every marker in the span (Ctrl-drag adds to the ones already picked). The chip shows how many. A plain drag still scrubs.

Actor markers ​

Right click an actor row's header (at the playhead) or its empty lane (at the pointer) > Add actor marker at {time}.

  • It sits on that actor's row, not the ruler: a small flag in the actor's colour at the top of the row with a stem down it. Several on one frame line up side by side.
  • Click selects it (the Inspector's Marker panel edits it). Drag moves it (snaps; one undo step). Right click opens the marker menu.
  • A per-rig export (a native Roblox animation, or Split runtime scenes) takes the scene's markers plus that actor's own, so each animation can have its own keyframe markers.
  • AcesMotion playback fires them all.
  • Removing the actor puts its markers back on the ruler.

Hit-stop holds ​

  • A marker with a Hold draws a bar after its flag as long as the hold, and a faint band across the lanes.
  • An actor marker's hold is a thin bar under its flag on the actor's row.
  • Right click a marker > Hold for Off, 2, 4, 6 or 8 frames (seconds with the Seconds ruler; the current one ticked) or Custom... (the marker panel's Hold field).

Rotations ​

  • Rotations keep their axis and their turns. A joint or root keyed after turning past 90 degrees about one axis keeps that turn on that axis. It never becomes a half turn on the other two, which rolled the rig between keys.
  • A turn past 180 degrees or past a whole turn keeps going the way it was turned.
  • Bakes, merges, imports, exports and a rotation order change keep each curve continuous the same way.

Joints turn the short way between keys. When a joint's (or a root's) X, Y and Z rotation keys sit on the same frames, the rotation between them takes the shortest turn, the way Roblox's Animation Editor blends poses. A torso no longer lifts or wobbles mid-move when an angle passes 90 degrees.

  • It stays smooth through each key, and the angle that changes most sets the timing, so eases, tangents and overshoot still shape it.
  • The path through a key follows its tangents. With Auto (the default) it goes flat at a key where the motion turns back, so an arm or leg in a run cycle stops at its key pose instead of swinging past it. Elsewhere it bends only as far as it can without overshooting. Linear and Flat keys don't bend it at all. Smooth on all three angles keeps the free curve, which can swing past.
  • Each angle follows its own curve where one has a key the others don't, on Constant keys, on a turn of nearly 180 degrees, and on a spin. A spin is one angle turning past 180 degrees while the other two barely move. Keys whose angles only look far apart (a turn or a flip apart, but nearly the same pose) take the short way.
  • Camera roots use Each angle on its own, so a camera never rolls.

See where the short turn is off. A joint row shows a warning icon when some of its keys turn each angle on its own instead. Its tooltip lists where and why. Click it for the fixes and the Between keys choices. The same fixes sit at the bottom of the Between keys submenu (row and key menus) and the Inspector's Between keys dropdown, whose tooltips also say where it's off:

  • Align keys keys every angle wherever one of them has a key, at its own curve's value, so the poses don't move and the short turn works across them. One undo step.
  • Unwind runs the Euler filter on the tracks with a spin, so they turn the short way.

Switch a track with Between keys (row menu, key menu, Rotation submenu, or the Inspector's Key panel > Track): Shortest turn or Each angle on its own. With several rows selected, the row menu sets them all in one undo step.

  • Euler filter (key menu, row menu, Split's Curves menu) moves rotation keys by whole turns so none flips the long way round. Where all three angles have a key, it can also switch that pose to its other set of angles when that set is nearer the key before. The status line says how many keys moved, or that none needed it.
  • Rotation (row menu > Rotation) converts a CFrame track to any Euler order (XYZ, XZY, YXZ, YZX, ZXY, ZYX) or Quaternion. Every keyed pose stays.
  • A Quaternion rotation key is one key to every tool (its four channels move together).
  • An Euler track's Rotation submenu also holds Between keys: Shortest turn or Each angle on its own.

Custom actions ​

Errors never stop the scene

Lock ​

On a joint row (a hand, a foot, any joint, creatures too). It holds its part where it was at the lock's first frame while the limb above it bends to keep it there: planted feet while the hips move, a hand on a wall or a rail.

  • A lock with no Spot yet gets the spot it holds the first time you pose the rig (its hips, say), so posing at the lock's first frame never drags the foot along.
  • Target (an actor, a part, a point): it sticks to the target instead and follows it.
    • Picking or changing the target keeps the part where it is and stores its grip on the target.
    • Posing the target moves the part with it. Posing the part moves only its grip (keyed with Auto-key), never the target.

A lock is the base of its part's stack

Every other constraint on that row (Floor, Limit Location, Copy Location, the aims) applies on top of where the lock holds the part, whatever their order on the lane, and the limb bends to reach the result. So a Floor under a planted foot still lifts it. An actor-wide Floor that bends limbs lifts a locked foot too.

Adding a lock (each is one undo step, with a toast and its Undo):

  • Select keys on a hand or foot row and right click > Lock: one lock per row, from its first selected key to its last. With keys on several rows of one actor, its hands and feet get one each.
  • Pick a part in the viewport and press Shift+L (or right click > Lock): a lock from the playhead to that row's next key, or a second when it has none.
  • Pins > Turn pins into locks makes each pin a lock from the playhead and clears the pins. An anchored pin's lock targets what it was anchored to.
  • Add constraint > Lock adds one on a joint row too.

The lock's marker: while the playhead is inside a lock (or its block is selected), a sphere marks the spot it holds, with a line to its target when it has one.

Posing a locked part while the playhead is inside its lock works like posing any part (IK bends the limb), and the lock goes with it, the way an IK control is keyed in other tools.

  • With Auto-key (or K) the lock's Spot is keyed at that frame. You can keep one lock over the whole timeline and key the foot wherever it should be, planted from key to key and moving between them.
  • The first Spot key also keys the lock's first frame where it held, so the frames before stay put.
  • With Auto-key off it moves the whole lock (or keys it, once its Spot has keys).
  • Posing the hips or any other joint never moves a locked part.
  • The Spot's keys show as one Spot row under the lock's block (expand its lane), and move, copy and delete together.
  • The locked joint's own keys carry the Spot key at the same time with them. Copy a planted foot's keys and paste them later and the foot stands in the same spot.
  • Paste keys mirrored leaves the copied Spot out (a spot in the world isn't the partner's) and keys the partner's lock where the mirrored pose puts the part instead. Pasting keys from an unlocked joint onto a locked one moves its lock the same way.

Every other way a pose reaches a locked part moves its lock the same way:

  • Paste pose, Paste mirrored (the pose), Flip pose, Reset to rest and the pose library put the lock where the new pose puts the part (keyed with Auto-key).
  • Shift+K keys the Spot like K.
  • The tween slider tweens the Spot with the joint.
  • Spread and dragging keys in the curve editor keep the Spot key with its joint's key (the curve editor moves it in time only).
  • One undo step; Esc cancels.

Motion Warp ​

Motion Warp bends an actor's path so it lands on the real target, wherever that is: a fireball that still hits a unit three times further away, a coin flying into a moving player's backpack, a camera that flies in to a boss wherever it spawned.

You animate the trip once against the stand-ins. In a game the same path is re-fitted between where it really starts and where the target really is, so an arc stays an arc at any range.

Adding one ​

  • A spawned actor that appears at a socket starts from that socket.
  • If the span has no root keys yet it keys the two ends (where it starts and the target), so it flies at once.

On several rows: with several root or joint rows selected (or parts picked), Add Motion Warp and the Library's warp presets add one to each.

  • The target is picked once for all of them, as one undo step.
  • Rows that can't take a warp are skipped, and the toast says how many.

  • The Ease window opens on the warp's trip ("0.30 s to 0.80 s"). Its presets and curve set the warp's own ease, one undo step.
  • It re-times the trip: the path stays the same, the speed along it follows the curve.
  • It works on top of any timing your root keys give, and with no root keys too.
  • Presets keep it.

How it reads your keys ​

  • When you pick its target (or use Capture line) it remembers the line from where it starts to the target as they stand then.
  • Each frame of your keys keeps its share of the trip and its distance off that line, so the timing and easing you keyed stay.
  • Move the target's stand-in by hand afterwards to see what a game with the target somewhere else plays.
  • No root keys yet: a warp whose actor isn't keyed to travel over its span (no root keys, or keyed to stay put) walks the line evenly over the span instead, anything its keys do riding on top. So it flies, a Path shape shows and the arc diamond appears before you key anything.

Export

A Roblox animation plays it as it is for the stand-ins. The export report lists it among what needs the runtime.

Audio lanes ​

Audio lanes hold the scene's own sounds: music, voice-over, ambience, anything that belongs to no actor.

Rows ​

  • They sit at the bottom of the rows, after the last actor, under an Audio row (speaker, "3 lanes").
  • The Audio row's main keys stand for the clips starting there, like an actor row's.
  • Each lane (sound, its name, its volume "80%") has its own Mute, Lock and volume.
  • A muted lane draws at half brightness and is silent.

Clips ​

Clips look and work exactly like a sound's Play() keys and bars:

  • The tick at the start, the bar with its waveform.
  • Drag to move (Ctrl copies, Alt flips snapping).
  • Trim both ends; fade in and out with their shapes; loop.
  • Ctrl stretch for speed.
  • Overlapping clips stack in up to four sub-lanes.
  • Audio scrubbing and peak snapping.

A bar's label starts after its fade in, so the fade line never crosses it. A 3D clip shows a target icon before its name. A clip with no sound yet is a "No sound" placeholder.

Selecting: clips select like keys (box select, Shift and Ctrl, the Audio row's main keys, Select all clips in lane).

  • A box over keys and clips takes both, so a retime, nudge, copy, paste or delete moves them together.
  • Status bar: "1 clip selected on Music".

Layers ​

Scene layers sit on top of Base (each actor's own keys), scene-wide, bottom to top.

  • A layer holds keyed rows only: joints, the root and properties.
  • Blocks, sounds, spawns, attach keys and call keys stay on Base.
  • The actor's generators, constraints, drivers and attachments run on the blended pose.

Posing saves what you see ​

  • On an Additive layer a key holds the difference from the layers below.
  • On an Override layer, the key that makes the blend land where you posed.
  • Layers above the one you key are taken into account too, on Base and on a layer: the key is what makes the whole stack show your pose.
  • An Override layer at full weight above hides the row, and the status line says the key won't show.
  • At weight 0 a layer can't show anything, so the key holds the pose at full weight and the status line says so.

Clamp ​

In Target, for colours. On by default.

  • On keeps each colour channel (and HSV's value) between 0 and 1.
  • Off lets them go past 1, for properties that take brighter than white (lights, Highlights).
  • A colour's Result preview (its colour strip) shows what comes out, clamped or not.
  • Its Shape shows the wave on each driven channel, like any other value.

Part colours and grey hues

A Part's Color wraps round past 1, so keep Clamp on there.

Snap to Loop ​

In Timing, for Shake, Wiggle, Oscillate, Flicker, Spin and Cycle, and custom generators that name a LoopParam. It makes the generator loop seamlessly.

  • Its speed (Period, Frequency, Rate or Speed; a Pattern's step rate, a Scroll's speed) snaps to the nearest value where whole repeats fit the block, so its end meets its start.
  • Type 1.1 s on a 3 s block and it becomes 1 s (3 waves). 1.3 s becomes 1.5 s (2 waves).
  • Dragging the field or the preview steps through the loopable values live.
  • Resizing the block re-snaps it from your value, so it stays close. 1 s on a block stretched to 3.2 s plays as 1.067 s, and back to exactly 1 s at 4 s.
  • The field always shows what plays.
  • Phase and every other setting stay free.
  • Noise (Shake, Wiggle, a random Flicker) loops too.
  • A Pattern snaps to whole passes of its steps, a Scroll to whole laps, a Cycle to whole passes of its list.
  • The block stays where it is. Right-click it and choose Fit to playback range (the loop region, else the whole scene) to loop it with playback.
  • The note under the toggle says how many repeats fit, and warns when the block doesn't cover the playback range.
  • While it's on, the speed can't be keyed, and a keyed speed can't snap.

What can't loop

A Shake that dies away (set Decay to 0), a spinning number, or a Cycle with Hold last. Recoil and Typewriter play once.

  • Its OK keeps it, Cancel drops it, and its own undo steps back.

Generators ​

Built in starts with the Screen effects group: Fade to black, Fade from black, Flash white, Letterbox and Vignette pulse. These go on the scene's Screen row whatever is selected.

Then come Shake, Wiggle, Oscillate, Flicker / Strobe / Pulse, Spin, Recoil, Overshoot / Settle, Stepped, Hold / Fade, Cycle / Flipbook and Typewriter, each with its description.

  • Click one to open its presets, built in then yours, each with its settings in a line: "Amplitude 0.40, 6 Hz, no decay".

  • Click to pick. Double click (or Add to selected rows) adds it to the Timeline's selected rows at the playhead, spanning their selected keys.

  • Right click one of your presets: Add to selected rows, Rename (in place) or Delete.
  • Export presets... and Import presets sit above the button.
  • The search filters generators and presets.

  • Look at player aims at the local player's head, fading out between 5 and 15 studs.

  • My presets lists the ones you saved from a constraint's block menu or its Inspector panel ("Damped Track: Nearest player · Head, fade 8 to 24 studs").

  • Right click one of your presets: Add to selected rows, Rename (in place) or Delete.

Preset files ​

Preset files share presets with your team.

Export presets is a floating window. It lists:

Shortcuts ​

Every AcesMotion shortcut, read live from the plugin, grouped by where it works (Anywhere in AcesMotion, Timeline, Viewport, Inspector, ...). Each row shows its current key ("No key" when it has none) and its name. Hover a row for its description.

Search shortcuts is the box at the top. It filters the table as you type:

  • Each word must match.
  • Letters in order inside the name are enough: "tgak" finds Toggle auto-key.
  • Plain text in the key ("shift+b") or the description matches too.
  • Groups with no match hide. "No shortcuts match ..." shows when nothing does.

Every shortcut can be rebound right in the table. Click its key, then press the new one with any modifiers. Esc cancels.

  • A key another shortcut already uses in the same place is refused: "X is taken by {name}".

Saving and undo ​

How scenes are saved, versioned, undone, recovered and shared in Team Create.

Saving ​

  • Every edit is written into the place as you work.
  • Each scene is a folder (default ReplicatedStorage.AcesMotionScenes) holding compact binary chunks in attributes, one per actor, so an edit rewrites only what it touched.
  • View state is per user, not in the place: zoom, scroll, expanded rows, compact rows, view mode, split height, row heights. The 50 most recent scenes are remembered.

Saving the place while a scene is posed

It saves the posed part positions (Roblox's own Animation Editor has the same catch). Joints snap them back in a running game. Closing the scene puts everything back at rest.

Versions ​

Versions show in the History tab.

  • Save (Scene menu, or Alt+Shift+S while an AcesMotion window has focus) keeps the scene as it is now as a version.
  • Versions live in ServerStorage.AcesMotionHistory: saved with the place, shared in Team Create, never sent to players.
  • Each version stores only the chunks that changed.
  • Automatic versions are saved as you work (Settings > Versions).
  • Preview, Compare, Restore and Branch work from the History tab, the Inspector and the Scene menu.

Unsaved dot: an accent dot after the scene name while the scene has changes no version you saved holds. It shows in the Timeline's Scene field, the Hub's scene bar, Open scene and the Scenes list.

  • Automatic versions don't clear it. Undoing back to the saved state does.
  • Closing a scene with the dot showing never asks. A toast says "Closed with unsaved changes" with Save version.

Version rules: a scene remembers the version that saved it.

  • A scene saved by a newer AcesMotion opens read-only with a banner.
  • An older scene is migrated on load, with a one-time confirm before its first save in the new format: "Save in the new scene format. Older AcesMotion versions can't open it after."

Undo ​

  • Every edit is one named Studio undo step (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y, or Edit > Undo): key edits, poses, pins, marker and region edits, imports, window OK.
  • A pose drag that keys is one step.
  • The edit-mode preview rests the stand-ins around every recording, so no step carries a pose. After an undo the stand-ins sit at rest for a moment, then re-pose.
  • Undo brings the selection back with the edit. Undoing a move selects the same keys (blocks, camera switch keys, attach keys) where they were.
  • Undoing a delete selects what was deleted. Redo brings back what was selected when you undid.
  • Changing the selection is not an undo step of its own.
Version kindIn undo
A version you saveIts own undo step ("Save version v4"), so Ctrl+Z takes it back.
An automatic versionJoins the step before it. It costs no extra Ctrl+Z, and undoing that step removes it too (redo brings both back).
RestoreOne step. The scene before it is always kept as an automatic version, even with automatic versions off.

Team Create ​

Scenes are ordinary attribute changes, so several people can edit one scene at once. A teammate's edit shows up in your open scene within about half a second. When you both change the same actor at the same moment, your edit is applied again on top of theirs, so neither is lost. See Working together for what you see of each other and what happens when edits meet.

Crash recovery ​

  • The open scene is mirrored into plugin settings about 2 seconds after each edit, undo, redo or teammate's change. So Restore never brings back edits you undid.
  • Each scene keeps its own copy, so editing another scene after a crash never overwrites the crashed one's.
  • Closing the scene (or Studio) cleanly drops its copy.

If Studio closed without that (a crash) and the place was not saved:

  • When the plugin starts, Output says "AcesMotion kept a recovered copy of Fight Scene. Open it from the Hub's Scenes tab."
  • The Scenes tab shows a banner above the list ("Fight Scene has a recovered copy from 5 minutes ago (and 1 more).") with Open (opens the newest one's scene) and Discard.
  • Opening the scene shows a Timeline banner: "A recovered copy saved 5 minutes ago has changes the place doesn't."

One error never breaks the whole plugin

Each distinct error is reported once: a warning with its stack in Output and one line in the status bar. The failing step is skipped (a failing edit changes nothing, a failing drag always lets go) and the rest keeps working.

What a teammate has picked shows in their colour:

  • Keys: a ring around each key, outside your own selection look. A key two teammates picked gets two rings.

Who is editing a field ​

Dragging the same keys ​

While a teammate drags keys, the rows they are moving show a dashed box in their colour around the keys, and a lock icon at the right of the row's name ("Maya is dragging keys here"). The status bar says "Maya is dragging keys on Head, LeftFoot".

The lock never stops you. Start dragging those keys yourself and a card opens by the pointer, "Maya is dragging these keys. You can still move them. Whoever lets go last wins.", then closes after a few seconds or when your drag ends.

Follow ​

Following a teammate puts your camera and playhead on theirs.

  • Start it by clicking their avatar, from the teammate menu, or with the Follow button in In this place.
  • Your camera eases onto theirs and stays with it. Your playhead moves with theirs.
  • The joints they pick glow wider in their colour.
  • When they play, you play too, from their frame: preview sounds, effects and action blocks run as in your own play. If you drift more than a frame and a half from them, you jump back in step. When they stop, you stop on their frame.
  • Following someone in another scene opens that scene first.

While you follow, the viewport has a frame in their colour with a tab at the top ("Following Maya", "Move the camera to stop", a Stop button). The status bar starts with a Following Maya pill ("Following Maya · Playing" while they play) with its own Stop button, and your time chip on the ruler gets a ring in their colour.

Anything you do yourself stops following, then does what you asked:

  • moving the camera;
  • scrubbing or moving the playhead;
  • changing the selection;
  • pressing Play, pause or Stop;
  • looking through a camera;
  • closing the scene.

A toast says so ("Stopped following Maya", with what you did, such as "You moved the camera.") and has a Follow button to pick up again. If they leave the scene you get "Maya left the scene". Following someone with no scene open only tells you so ("Maya has no scene open").

Who is viewing you ​

While teammates follow you, a small chip sits in the viewport's bottom-left corner, above the tool indicator: up to three of their avatars and "Maya is viewing", "Maya and Leo are viewing" or "Maya and 2 others are viewing". The first name is whoever started following first.

  • Hover it for everyone following you, with their @username.
  • Right-click it for Hide viewing indicator. View > Viewing indicator and Settings > Collaboration > Show who is viewing bring it back.
  • It hides while Show collaborators is off.

Edits that meet ​

Nothing is locked, and you never wait for a teammate. Each actor's keys, and the scene's own settings, are saved separately, so edits to different actors never touch each other.

When you and a teammate change the same actor at the same moment, AcesMotion takes their change and applies yours again on top, inside the same undo step, so both changes stay. If the same actor keeps changing under you three times in a row, your edit is dropped with a toast naming it, for example 'Couldn't save "Move keys"' with "Hero keeps changing. Run it again."

A few edits can't be applied again on a newer scene, because they replace a whole list or a whole scene: restoring a version, changing the frame rate, importing a clip, baking keys, mirror rules and swatches. If one of these meets a teammate's change, yours is refused and theirs stays, with a toast like '"Restore v4" didn't save' and "Maya changed Hero at the same time. Nothing changed; run it again."

Undo is yours. Ctrl+Z only takes back your own steps. If undoing one would also wipe a change a teammate made on top of it, you get "Undo removed Maya's change" with Put back Maya's change, which puts their part back and keeps your undo. Putting it back is its own undo step.

Whole-scene changes ask first. Changing the frame rate, restoring a version, or updating an older scene to the new format while others are in the scene opens a confirm that names them, for example "Change the frame rate for everyone?" with their names as chips and Change to 30 fps. Alone, these run straight away.

When someone moves scenes between folders in the Scenes tab, everyone else gets "Maya moved 12 scenes" ("Into Combat / Swords.") with Show, which picks them in the Scenes tab.
