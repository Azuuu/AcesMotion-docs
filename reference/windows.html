Windows ​

Every AcesMotion window, control and menu, with a picture of each part.

Timeline: layout, widths and the empty state ​

  • With no scene open the Timeline shows Open a scene: the place's scenes in the same list as the Scenes tab (search, the 40 px rows with "60 fps | 2.00 s | 3 actors", hover Rename / Duplicate / Delete, double click renames, New scene and Import at the bottom), centred, 440 px wide, with a Settings gear beside its title. Click a scene to open it right there. With no scenes in the place it shows "No scene open" with the AcesMotion logo and two buttons: Open Hub and Settings.
  • With a scene open the Timeline is: the toolbar (36 px per line: 72 px in Two rows, 108 only under 744 px), optional banners, the track column beside the stage (ruler over the lanes), and a 22 px status bar. The banners and lanes move down with the toolbar's height.
  • The track column is 296 px wide (200 px in the narrow layout, window under 1100 px, where rows are also 20 px tall instead of 24).
  • Toolbar groups: the toolbar is eleven groups with a thin divider between groups on a line: Menus (Scene, Edit, View, Pose), Transport, Time, Rate (frame rate, speed), Posing (Move & rotate | Resize, FK | IK), Posing fields (Chain, Space, the Resize: Each field while Resize is the tool, Pivot), Pins and mirror (Pin, Mirror posing, Flip pose), Keying (Auto-key, Snap), Display (Onion skin, Trajectory with Timing, Look through camera), View mode (Dope sheet | Curves | Split) and Ruler units (Frames | Seconds). The View groups sit at the right edge of their line.
  • Ruler units set every time field: Frames or Seconds also decides how the Inspector and the Hub show and take times: a block's Start and Length, fades, a spawned actor's Appears at, Disappears at and Linger for, a constraint's Delay and Look ahead, a driver's Smooth length, a generator's Period, Kick and Delay. What is stored doesn't change.
  • Toolbar layout (Settings > Look > Toolbar layout, View > Toolbar layout, or the last item of the More menu in One row; saved per user):
    • Two rows (default): row 1 holds Menus, Transport, Time, Rate and, at the right, the View groups; row 2 holds the tool groups. Under 1343 px row 1 switches to compact labels (Scene field 120 px, no dimmed other-unit readout, Time field 64 px, "Dope" instead of "Dope sheet"). Each row stays on one line: a row that still does not fit moves its own groups into More until it does (row 1: Rate, then Ruler units, then View mode; row 2: Posing fields, Tween, Display, Keying, Pins and mirror, Posing), so the toolbar keeps the lanes their room. Everything shows from 1185 px; at 1000 px only Posing fields and Ruler units are in More. Only under 744 px, where Menus, Transport, Time and More alone don't fit, does row 1 take a second line. Two rows never merges into one line, however wide the window.
    • One row: one 36 px line. When it does not fit, compact labels come first (from 2152 px), then groups move into the More menu one at a time, in this order: Posing fields (under 2152), Rate (1847), Display (1686), Keying (1507), Pins and mirror (1402), Posing (1271), Ruler units (1068), View mode (911). Menus, Transport and Time always stay. Under 736 px, Time and More drop to a second line. With the Resize field showing, everything fits from 2418 px (compact from 2272). At 1720 px Posing fields and Rate are in More.
    • The plan comes from the real control widths, so the toolbar reflows at once as the window is resized (no animation), when the Resize tool turns on or off, and when Split turns on or off.
  • Banners stack under the toolbar: read-only scene, "Update runtime", "Update available", a recovered copy ("A recovered copy saved ... has changes the place doesn't."), and "{stand-in} was deleted. Its keys are kept." (see Status bar and banners).
  • Pointer position matters: while the pointer is over the Timeline, the Hub (or the Cast tab popped into its own window) or one of them has focus, Studio's own selection is set aside, so Studio's Delete, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+D cannot touch your rigs. It comes back when the pointer leaves.

Timeline: toolbar controls (left to right) ​

  • Scene field (shows the scene name, or "No scene"): opens the Scene menu (see Timeline menus). Rename... turns the field into a text box (Enter keeps the name, clicking away cancels). An accent dot after the name shows while the scene has changes no version you saved holds; the tooltip says since which version.
  • Edit, View and Pose fields: open their menus (see Timeline menus). They always stay on the toolbar.
  • Go to start (Home): jumps to frame 0, or to the loop region's start when there is one.
  • Previous key (Up) and Next key (Down): jump the playhead to the previous or next key of the whole scene (greyed with "No keys before the playhead." / "No keys after the playhead.").
  • Previous frame (Left) and Next frame (Right): step one frame. Stepping, jumping and going to start/end all stop playback.
  • Play / Pause (Space): plays from the playhead (from the start when the playhead is at or past the end of the range). Disabled with "Add an actor first." in a scene with no actors.
  • Go to end (End): jumps to the last frame, or the loop region's end.
  • Loop (on by default): playback repeats the loop region, or the whole scene when there is no loop region. Off: playback stops at the end.
  • Toggle loop, Toggle snapping, Toggle trajectory, Hide overlays, Show other layers, Layer above, Layer below and Add layer have shortcut ids with no key by default: bind them in Settings > Shortcuts, and the buttons' tooltips show the key.
  • Ping-pong (off by default): plays forward, then backward, between the ends of the range.
  • Time field: shows the playhead as "f42" (frames) or "0.70 s" (seconds) following the ruler, with the other unit dimmed beside it. Click to type a time and press Enter: 42 or f42 is frame 42, 0.7s is 0.7 seconds, and with the ruler in seconds a bare 0.7 is seconds (and f42 still means frame 42). Bad text puts the old value back.
  • Frame rate field (e.g. "60 fps"): menu of 24, 25, 30, 50, 60 and 120 fps. Changing it never shifts keys (time is stored in 6000 ticks per second).
  • Playback speed field (e.g. "1x"): menu of 0.1x, 0.25x, 0.5x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x and 4x. It changes the preview only, not the scene.
  • Auto-key (record icon, off by default, Ctrl+Shift+K): writes keys when you pose or edit something. While it's on, the button and the status bar's "Auto-key on" turn record red, so it never looks like Snap. Its chevron opens Auto-key options (Auto-key, Key frame 0 when it's missing, Posing settings...).
  • Onion skin (Ctrl+Shift+O, on by default) with a chevron for the Onion menu.
  • Snap (magnet icon, on by default) with a chevron for the Snap menu. Hold Alt while dragging to flip it for that drag: with Snap on, Alt skips the targets; with Snap off, Alt snaps to them. Frame snapping (Snap menu > Frames) holds either way, so with Snap off a dragged key still lands on a whole frame.
  • Trajectory (on by default) with a chevron for the Trajectory menu.
  • Timing (stopwatch icon, off by default, J holds it on): lets you slide a motion trail's key dots along the path to retime keys.
  • Pin (pin icon, P): pins or unpins the picked parts. A badge shows how many pins exist and the button is lit while any exist. Disabled with "Pick a part first, then click to pin it. Or hold {key} and click a part." when nothing is picked. Its chevron opens Pins options.
  • Mirror posing (Shift+M, off by default) with a chevron for Mirror options. Flip pose (Shift+F): mirrors the pose onto the other side in place.
  • Look through camera (camera icon, Q): the viewport sees through the scene's live camera (see Viewport: looking through a camera). Lit while look-through is on, also while looking away. Disabled with "Add a camera actor in the Hub first." when the scene has no usable camera actor; its chevron (always enabled) opens the camera menu: Look through camera, Follow camera track, Align camera to view, one item per camera actor (ticked: the one looked through; picking one turns Follow off, looks through it and snaps a looked-away view back), Add camera at view, Show frustums (All cameras, Selected only, Off), Show camera switch lines, When you move the view (Look away until scrubbed or played, Exit look-through, Stay on the camera), Reset roll when focusing (see Viewport: camera actors, F), Camera settings... (opens Settings > Camera). With no camera actor the list reads "No camera actors" and the first three items are greyed. When Display is in the More menu, the pair is there (Look through camera, and a Camera submenu with the same items). While piloting the camera (once Pilot camera is built), the button carries a small amber "P" badge at its top right.
  • Move & rotate and Resize (the gizmo tool switch, T switches): Move & rotate shows the rotate rings and move arrows together; Resize shows face and corner handles for parts and models. The chevron after Resize opens Resize options (Each, Group, Corner handles, Posing settings...).
  • FK | IK switch (FK by default) with a chevron for IK options.
  • Chain field ("Chain: Auto", "Chain: 3 joints"): how many joints an IK drag moves. Menu: Auto (to a branch, at most 3), 1 to 8 joints, Shorter (Ctrl+[), Longer (Ctrl+]).
  • Space field (Local, Parent or World; R cycles it): the axes the gizmo uses. Disabled while Resize is the tool ("Resize works on the part's own axes.").
  • Resize: Each / Group field (only while Resize is the tool; it widens Posing fields, which may then wrap or move into More): how several picked actors are resized.
  • Pivot field ("Pivot: Joint" or "Pivot: Custom"): menu with the Snap pivot checkbox and a note "Hold over a part to place". Disabled while Resize is the tool.
  • Dope sheet | Curves | Split switch: see Curve editor. Tab cycles it.
  • Frames | Seconds switch: the ruler and every time label (time field, chips, status text) follow it.
  • "..." (More tools): shown only when a group has moved into it (One row, or Two rows under 1185 px) or in the Split view. Its menu lists only the groups that moved, in toolbar order, each under its name as a dimmed heading: Rate (Frame rate: 60 fps, Playback speed: 1x), Posing (Move & rotate, Resize, FK / IK), Posing fields (Chain, Space, Resize while Resize is the tool, Pivot), Pins and mirror (Pins, Mirror, Flip pose), Keying (Auto-key, Snap), Display (Onion skin, Trajectory, Timing, Look through camera, Camera), View mode (Dope sheet, Curves, Split), Ruler units (Frames, Seconds). In One row it ends with Toolbar layout (Two rows, One row); in Split, Curves (split view) follows. The button is lit while its menu is open. A small accent dot on the button means a tool tucked inside is switched on: Onion skin, Trajectory, Timing or look-through (Display), Auto-key (Keying; Snap is on by default, so it doesn't light the dot), pins or Mirror posing (Pins and mirror), or a custom pivot (Posing fields).

Timeline: menus ​

  • Scene menu: New scene; Open scene (a list of every scene with the open one ticked and an accent dot on scenes with unsaved changes, then "Browse in the Hub"); Save (Alt+Shift+S while an AcesMotion window has focus or the pointer is over the Timeline: saves a version of the scene with an automatic name such as "v4"; see Saving, versions and undo); Save as... (a small card asks for the copy's name, filled with "{name} copy" and selected; a taken name is refused in the card; Enter saves, Esc cancels; the copy opens and you keep working in it, and a toast offers Open original); Versions (the newest 15 versions with their times, automatic ones dimmed; picking one previews it; then Version history..., which opens the Hub's History tab); Rename...; Duplicate; Import... (opens the Import panel); Export... (opens the Export panel); Close scene; Delete scene... (red, asks to confirm, Ctrl+Z brings it back); Settings.
  • Find a command (F3, rebindable, or the top of the Edit menu): a search over every AcesMotion command opens over the window you're working in (the Timeline, Inspector or Hub, or the viewport). Type part of the name ("tgak" finds Toggle auto-key), its key or what it does; the best matches come first. Each row shows where it works and its key ("No key" for one you haven't bound). Arrows move, Enter runs it, Esc or a click outside closes; the highlighted command's description shows at the bottom. With nothing typed, your recently run commands come first. Hold keys (Space to pan and the like) aren't listed.
  • Edit menu: Find a command... (F3); Undo and Redo (named after Studio's next step, e.g. "Undo Resize"; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y are Studio's keys); Copy; Paste; Paste keys mirrored; Duplicate keys; Delete keys (red); Select all keys; Clear key selection; Nudge keys (1 frame left, 1 frame right, 10 frames left, 10 frames right); Key selected; Key whole actor. Items grey out when they cannot apply. Each item is enabled for what it acts on: Copy keys with keys selected; Duplicate and Delete with keys, attach keys, blocks or Camera switch keys; Nudge with keys, attach keys or blocks.
  • Menus and tooltips name their keys (and show a key you bind later): the View menu's Zoom submenu (Zoom in =, Zoom out -, Frame selected F, Frame all A); the Edit menu's Nudge keys shows both keys of each way ([ / Shift+Left); the ruler's Loop in / out (Shift+I / Shift+O); an action track's Rename track (F2); every "... settings..." item (Open settings, once bound); Look through camera's tooltip names the focus key (F); key tooltips say Ctrl+drag copies, Shift click adds and Ctrl click toggles.
  • View menu: Hide overlays (hides joint dots, bones, the motion trail, constraint and driver guides, attach lines and camera frustums at once; the gizmo and part highlights stay; Hide while playing, on by default and also in Settings > Gizmos, hides all of that plus the gizmo, the Resize handles, part highlights and pin markers while the scene plays, so you watch the animation alone, and brings them back when it stops; bindable as "Hide overlays" in Settings > Shortcuts); Joint dots; Dots and bones: selected only (only on the selected actors and those with a picked joint; also in Settings > Gizmos); Bones (Show bones, then Diamonds, Wire, Sticks); Part highlights; Show attach lines; Gizmo (Rotate rings, Move arrows, Long arrows, Plane handles, Corner handles, Separate move and rotate, the handle choice, Small / Medium / Large, Snap pivot); Space (Local, Parent, World); Scene origin; Onion skin (the Onion menu); Trajectory (the Trajectory menu); Show other layers (the toolbar toggle); Effect preview; Camera (the toolbar's camera menu, the same items; the only place Show camera switch lines sits in the View menu); Row stripes (on/off and 2, 4, 6, 8, 12%); Unkeyed joints (on/off); Sound rows (Compact, Expanded); Waveforms; Toolbar layout (Two rows, One row); Windows (Hub, Inspector, Settings, Reset window layout); Keyboard shortcuts (opens Settings on its Shortcuts page: every key, live).
    • Gizmo > handle choice: with Separate move and rotate off it reads "Move & rotate handles" / "Resize handles". With it on it reads "Rotate handles" / "Move handles" / "Resize handles" and T cycles all three.
    • Gizmo > Small / Medium / Large set the gizmo size to 80, 100 or 125 percent.
  • Pose menu: Copy pose (Shift+C); Paste pose (Shift+V); Paste mirrored (Shift+N); Flip pose (Shift+F); Select mirror (menu only); Reset to rest (Shift+R); Attach to... (Shift+G); Detach (Shift+Alt+G; greyed with the reason when nothing picked is attached, or on its rig, at the playhead); Back to rig (Shift+Alt+A, snaps a rig joint back into its place); Back to rig where it is (no default key: rejoins keeping its place); Pick welded parts (on / off, also in Settings > Posing); Mirror posing (Shift+M); Mirror rules... (opens Settings > Posing).
  • Greyed menu items that have a reason show it in the status bar for 3 seconds when clicked (Pilot camera in the camera menu; Flip pose and Look through camera in the "..." menu).
  • Tool dropdowns (a 14 px chevron after the tool's button; the same items sit under View and in the "..." menu):
    • Auto-key options: Auto-key; Key frame 0 when it's missing; Posing settings...
    • Snap options: Snap; Frames; Keys and markers; Region edges, start and end; Waveform peaks; Snap distance (4, 6, 8, 12 or 16 px).
    • Onion options: Onion skin; Ghosts at keys; Ghosts every N frames; Past ghosts; Future ghosts; Before (1 to 5); After (1 to 5); Step (1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15 or 30 frames, greyed unless ghosts are every N frames); Selected actors only; Limbs only; Onion settings...
    • Trajectory options: Show trajectory; Frame ticks; Key dots; Line thickness (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 px); Point size (75, 100, 140, 200, 300%); Range (Visible range, Loop region, Around the playhead ±N); Frame ticks: every frame, 2, 5 or 10 frames; Edit with (Gizmo, Drag); Timing mode; Trajectory settings...
    • Pins options: Pin or unpin picked parts; Unanchor picked pins; Turn pins into locks; Clear all pins; a note on the pin key; Posing settings...
    • Mirror options: Mirror posing; Flip pose; Mirror plane (X, Y or Z, saved in the scene); Mirror rules...
    • IK options: FK; IK; IK chain; a note on the context IK key; Posing settings...
    • Resize options: Each; Group; Corner handles; Posing settings...
  • Row filter menu (funnel icon in the track column header): All rows, Muted rows only, Locked rows only, then Unkeyed joints (on/off) and Stack rows (on/off).
  • Action menus (see Timeline: action tracks and blocks): the actor row's Add action track; an action track's menu (right click its header or an empty spot of its lane): Add block (Event; the custom actions by title, a module that failed to load greyed as "Name (could not load)", one that returns neither a function nor an action table as "Name (not an action, see Output)"; "No custom actions yet" when there are none; Use selected module as an action (tags the ModuleScripts selected in the Explorer as custom actions, one undo step); New custom action... (makes a ModuleScript tagged AcesMotionAction with a typed, commented template inside the instance selected in the Explorer, else ReplicatedStorage, adds its block at the clicked frame and opens the script; one undo step)), Rename track, Mute / Unmute, Remove action track (at once; the status bar says "Removed Attacks and its 4 blocks. Ctrl+Z brings it back."); a block's menu: Resolve target (only on a red block whose module is missing or broken: pick a tagged action module, use the module selected in the Explorer, or recreate one under the old name), Duplicate (Shift+D; on a stack row of generators, constraints or drivers the copy goes in the sub-lane below at the same time, elsewhere right after the selection), Delete from timeline, Delete with its module (also removes the module from the place; asks first and says how many other blocks use it; one Ctrl+Z brings both back), Open script, Select in Explorer (selects the module in the Explorer at once; Studio's selection is set aside again the next time the pointer enters the Timeline or you click in it, so Delete there can't remove the module by surprise), Show in Inspector (opens the Inspector on the selected blocks). Double click a custom block to open its module (Open script brings its tab to the front when it is already open behind another). Action code gets the scene's time on its context: context.Time (scene seconds), context.Frame (scene frame), context.Fps, context.TimeScale (how fast the scene plays: SetSpeed in games, the Timeline's playback speed in Studio, negative backwards), beside context.Seconds (time into the block), current before Run and on every Scrub, in Studio and in games. Hovering a block shows its module's Description first (Event: what it sends), so developers can explain their actions to each other; each param's Description becomes its tooltip in the Inspector. A param a module gained after its blocks were made arrives with its Default (in Studio and in games). Each preview run loads the action fresh in Studio: its current code and its own child modules, so edits apply on the next run and module-level variables start clean. Other modules it requires (a sibling, a shared util) are loaded once, like in a game: an edit to one of those applies after the plugin reloads (Studio reloads it on the next start, or reinstall the plugin). Renaming or moving an action module keeps its blocks: each module gets a permanent id (AcesMotion_ActionId attribute) that its blocks remember, in Studio and in games.

Timeline: ruler, markers, regions and scene length ​

  • The ruler is 52 px tall. It shows frame or second labels, the playhead chip (the frame number, or "0.70s"), markers at the top, the loop region bar, the export region bracket, and a handle at the scene end.
  • Scrub: press and drag anywhere on the ruler that is not a marker, region or the scene end. The playhead snaps to markers, keys, regions and sound peaks (when those snap targets are on) and otherwise lands on whole frames. Hold Alt to flip Snap for the scrub (whole frames hold unless Snap > Frames is off). Dragging over a sound plays short audio snippets (Settings > Audio).
  • Markers: right click the ruler > Add marker at {time} adds one named "Marker 1", "Marker 2"... in the next colour. Several markers can sit on the same frame: their flags line up side by side to the right of the shared stem, each still dragged, renamed and right clicked on its own. Drag its flag or stem to move it (snaps; the chip shows its name and the shift). Double click its flag to rename it in place (Enter keeps). Right click it for the marker menu: Rename; Colour (Coral, Amber, Green, Teal, Sky, Indigo, Violet, Pink); Move playhead here; Delete marker. A dashed line of the marker's colour runs down the lanes, drawn above the grid so it shows even on a frame or second line.
  • Loop region (an accent bar above the ruler labels): created with Shift+I (loop in at the playhead), Shift+O (loop out), or the ruler menu. Drag the bar to move it, drag either end to resize. Hovering an end lights that handle (brighter, 2 px wider, with a soft halo) so it's clear a drag resizes; the export bracket's ends do the same. Right click selected keys > Set loop region to selection / Set export region to selection sets either region from the first to the last selected key. Playback and Home/End use it.
  • Export region (a bracket under the loop bar): set from the ruler menu ("Set export region to the loop region"); drag the bracket or its ends. Export > Range can use it.
  • Scene end: drag the handle at the scene length on the ruler to change the scene length (one undo step, snaps like everything else). Shortening never deletes keys.
  • Ruler menu (right click empty ruler): Add marker at {time}; Loop (Loop in at {time}, Loop out at {time}, Clear loop region); Export region (Set to the loop region, Clear export region); Time (Insert 1 frame, Insert 1 second, Remove 1 frame and Remove 1 second at {time}, and, when a loop region exists, "Remove the loop region's time ({from} to {to})"). Insert and remove shift every key after that time.
  • Actor markers: right click an actor row's header (at the playhead) or its empty lane (at the pointer) > Add actor marker at {time}. It sits on that actor's row, not the ruler: a small flag in the actor's colour at the top of the row with a stem down it (several on one frame line up side by side). Click selects it (the Inspector's Marker panel edits it), drag moves it (snaps; one undo step), right click opens the marker menu. A per-rig export (a native Roblox animation, or Split runtime scenes) takes the scene's markers plus that actor's own, so each animation can have its own keyframe markers; AcesMotion playback fires them all. Removing the actor puts its markers back on the ruler.
  • Hit-stop holds: a marker with a Hold draws a bar after its flag as long as the hold, and a faint band across the lanes; an actor marker's hold is a thin bar under its flag on the actor's row. Right click a marker > Hold for Off, 2, 4, 6 or 8 frames (seconds with the Seconds ruler; the current one ticked) or Custom... (the marker panel's Hold field).
  • In Seconds mode the ruler puts a tick and a faint lane line every 0.1 s and labels every 0.5 s at the default zoom (labels every 0.1 s when zoomed in, ticks every 0.2 s and labels every 1 s when zoomed out).
  • Middle drag, or Space + drag, on the ruler pans time.

Timeline: track column (row headers) ​

  • A 52 px header holds Search rows (filters rows by name), the row filter button and a + button that opens the Add menu, the same menu as the Cast tab's +: Add actor from selection, Add selected template as a spawned actor, then Add camera at view and the Game target submenu. An item that can't run now is greyed with a short reason on its right ("Select a part, rig or camera", "Select one template"); the button itself is only disabled with no scene open or a read-only scene. In the dope sheet and Split it also holds the Ease and Channels tabs (see Curve editor) and the Ease button (opens the Ease window for the selected keys; disabled with "Select keys first.").
  • Rows, top to bottom: Actor rows (tinted wash, a 3 px stripe in the actor's colour, expanded by default) > Instance rows (one per animated object inside the actor: class icon, name, how many of its properties are animated) > Track rows (the actor's root, one per joint with a bone icon, one per animated property, Emit() / Play() call rows, attribute rows named like @Power) > Channel rows (X, Y, Z, RotX... with a colour chip and the value at the playhead). Action rows come last under their actor (a bolt icon, the track's name, its block count on the right). Tracks are collapsed by default. A property row is named by the property alone and shows its value at the playhead ("On", "Disc", "45"); a chip names a joint's rotation mode ("Euler XYZ", "Quaternion") or a track's type.
  • Game target badge: an actor row whose role the game fills in shows a dashed badge after its name: Local player, Nearest player, Player camera or Tag: Enemy (its tag; "Tag: none" until one is typed). Hover it to see who fills the role in a game. The name shortens first when the column is narrow; a muted actor's badge fades with its row.
  • New keys come into view: an edit that adds keys (a new track, or more keys on one: the Inspector's diamonds, auto-key, K, a call key such as Emit()) or action blocks opens the collapsed actor and object rows they sit under, so the new key shows at once. Undo and redo don't change rows.
  • Mute or lock several rows at once: select rows (Ctrl or Shift click), then click the eye or lock of one of them (or Mute / Lock in its menu): every selected row gets the same state, one undo step ("Mute 4 rows").
  • Click a header: selects that row (and selects its actor for the Inspector and the viewport). Ctrl+click adds or removes one row. Shift+click selects every row from the last plain click. Ctrl+Shift+click adds that range.
  • Double click a header: keys the row at the playhead (like K). On a call row (Emit(), Play()) it copies the arguments of the key before the playhead; nothing happens where a key already exists.
  • The chevron expands or collapses a row.
  • On hover or selection each actor, root, joint, property and action row shows an eye button (Mute row: skips it in preview, playback and export; shown always while muted, drawn at 50%) a solo button between them, and a lock button (Lock row: stops edits to it). Channel and instance rows have none.
  • Solo (the S button, or Solo in a row's menu) plays only the soloed rows while you work: in the viewport, playback, sounds, effects, onion skin and the motion trail. Solos add up; Alt+click solos only that row; with several rows selected, clicking one's S solos them all. A soloed actor row plays its whole actor, a soloed track just that track (with the generator, constraint and driver lanes on it, and a soloed lane keeps its track). Rows left out fade like muted rows but stay editable. Solo is never saved: the game and Export always play the whole scene, and closing the scene clears it. Camera actors and the Camera row are never left out, so the view you look through keeps working. The status bar shows Solo: N rows while any row is soloed; click it (or Clear all solos in a row's menu) to clear them.
  • A joint row whose part is pinned shows a pin icon (Pinned: click to unpin it).
  • Right click a header: Expand/Collapse; Key this row; Show in Inspector; Remove from timeline (property, attribute and call tracks, a joint or the root that has keys or generators, constraints or drivers on it, or every animated property of an object row; a track goes with its Generators lane and constraint Stack, and when lanes go the status bar says Ctrl+Z brings them back; with several rows selected and the clicked one among them it removes them all as one undo step, and says "Remove N tracks from timeline"; actor, root, joint and channel rows stay); Mute/Unmute; Solo/Unsolo (and Clear all solos while any are on); Lock/Unlock; Reset row height; Show waveforms (sound rows only, a checkbox); Euler filter; Before / after (what the curves do before the first key and after the last: Hold, Cycle, or Cycle with offset, where each repeat starts where the last ended so a walk keeps walking; with several rows selected it sets them all, one undo step); Rotation (a CFrame track's Euler order or Quaternion). An actor row's menu groups its own items: Add (Action track, which adds "Actions", then "Actions 2"..., and expands the actor; Constraint; Motion Warp; Generator; Driver; Camera shake on a camera; Actor marker at {time}, the playhead), Attach (Attach to..., Detach, for its root at the playhead), for a spawned actor Spawn (Appear at {time}, Disappear at {time}, Stay until the end, Linger after disappearing, Select template), Bake stack when it has one, then Mute, Lock, Reset row height, Euler filter, Rotation, Convert to spawned actor / Convert to placed actor, and ends with Remove actor from scene (red; asks first when the actor has keys; Ctrl+Z brings it back), so the Cast tab isn't needed to remove one.
  • Reorder actors: drag an actor row's header up or down (past 4 px an accent line shows where it lands); the selected actor rows go together when the dragged one is among them. One undo step. It reorders the cast too (the Cast tab follows), unless Timeline keeps its own order is on (the actor row's menu, or a cast row's menu in the Cast tab; saved with the scene): then dragging here reorders only the Timeline and the Cast tab keeps its order; turning it off shows the cast order again.
  • Colour blend (a colour track's row menu, and the Inspector's menu on a colour property with keys): how the colour goes from key to key. OKLab (even, natural steps: red to green no longer goes through a muddy brown), OKLCH (the hue turns the short way round the colour wheel, keeping colours vivid), RGB (Roblox's plain mix). The keys' eases and tangents still shape the timing. New colour tracks start with Settings > Timeline > Colour blend (OKLab); tracks made before stay RGB. Games play it the same way.
  • Row-height grips: rows that stack bars (sound Play() rows and Emit() rows) show a 5 px grip on the top and bottom edges of their header (an accent line and the tooltip "Row height" on hover). Drag to make the row taller or shorter (0 to 240 px extra, shared by its lanes, saved with the scene's view); double click the grip or use the row menu's Reset row height to put it back. Plain property rows have no grip.
  • The mouse wheel over the headers scrolls rows. A scene with no actors shows "No actors yet" ("Rows appear here once the scene has a cast.").
  • In Split the headers end at the splitter and the shown curves are listed below it (colour chip, name, value at the playhead; highlighted while they have selected keys).

Timeline: dope sheet rows and keys ​

  • Key shapes tell the interpolation to the next key: Constant is a square, Linear a diamond, Cubic a rounded diamond, Eased a circle, Bezier a hexagon. A main key (an amber down triangle) stands for every key under it at that time (on actor and object rows, and on tracks whose channels differ); an actor row's also stands for its attach keys there. Bars (action blocks, constraints, generators, drivers) never make main keys: each is selected and moved on its own row. Keys of values that hold (booleans, enums, text, textures) are squares. A call key is a tick with a flag.
  • Rotation keys turn the short way: every new rotation key (auto-key, K, the gizmo, piloting, F, paste) is moved by whole turns to sit nearest the key before it, so a camera or joint turning past 180 degrees never spins the long way between two keys. Keys made before this fix: select them and right click > Euler filter.
  • Key types (set from the key menu > Key type): Key is solid, Breakdown is smaller and hollow, Hold is solid with a bar. They are labels for you to see and select by: a key's type never changes how its curve plays (a Hold key that should keep its value uses Constant interpolation).
  • Key states: default, hover (halo), selected (accent with a bright ring), mixed (main key with only some children selected: an accent ring and a small accent triangle inside). Muted rows draw at 50%.
  • Bars for values that hold (under their keys): a boolean is an 8 px green bar while it is on (nothing is drawn while it is off; its key square still shows where it turns off); an enum or text is a 16 px violet segment labelled with the value, two shades taking turns; a texture is the same with a 14 px thumbnail and the asset's catalog name. A bar's tooltip reads like "Disc from f24 to f48". The key menu greys Interpolation and Tangents on keys that cannot blend.
  • Joint rows follow the rig (parents before children: LowerTorso, the legs, UpperTorso, Head, the arms). A part's animated properties sit right under the joint that moves it: a keyed Head joint and a keyed or generated Color on the Head part show as the Head joint row, then (after its channels, when expanded) the Head part row one step in with Color and its generators under it, so a joint and everything on its part read together. Anything inside the part (a face decal) goes there too; parts no joint moves keep their own rows after the joints. A rig's root row is always first, even before it has keys (where the whole rig stands: double click it, or K, to key it). Stack rows (on by default; the stack button at the right of the rows header, the rows filter menu, View > Stack rows, Settings > Timeline, a bindable shortcut) shows the generator, constraint and driver lanes under each row; off, they hide under their rows so only keys show and a box select takes keys alone, while the blocks keep playing. Each row with a stack also has its own stack button beside Mute (on hover, and kept in view, tinted, while its stack is hidden): it overrides Stack rows for that row, saved with the scene's view: with Stack rows on, Hide stack folds one row's lanes under it; with Stack rows off, Show stack brings one row's lanes back. Unkeyed joints (off by default; the rows filter menu, View > Unkeyed joints, Settings > Timeline) lists every joint of a rig as a row, keyed or not, from the moment the actor is added. A row with no keys yet has a dimmed name and an empty lane, with no channels, Mute or Lock until it's keyed; picking its joint in the viewport highlights it, and a double click on its header (or K) keys it. Only the All rows filter shows them. A double click on a rig's actor row keys every joint, so they all get rows.
  • Thousands of keys stay smooth: keys are drawn into a few images rather than one object each, so panning, scrolling, zooming and dragging stay fast with thousands of keys on screen and selected. A drag slides the carried keys as one layer and leaves faded copies where they started; the hovered key shows its halo and tooltip as before.
  • Row stripes: every other row and lane is a shade lighter (View > Row stripes, Settings > Timeline).
  • The lanes show a grid line at each label, the area past the scene end shaded, the loop region washed, and a line per marker.
  • A scroll thumb on the right of the lanes drags to scroll rows.
  • An empty scene shows a card in the lanes: "Add an actor from the selection" with Add actor (opens the Add menu, as the + button) and Open Hub buttons.
  • Keying (see also the Inspector's diamonds): K keys the selected rows at the playhead (a channel row keys its whole track). With no row selected, K keys the picked joints and any posed (unkeyed) tracks, else the selected actors. Shift+K, a double click on an actor's row header, or K with it selected keys the actor whole: every joint of a rig, keyed or not, never its root. Any other actor (a model, part, camera) keys its root. A rig's root also keys from its own row; property rows key from their own rows. K / Shift+K / double click never key a call on an actor or object row (the call would fire); only the call row itself does.
  • Key frame 0 when it's missing (default on): keying past frame 0 on a track with no frame 0 key also keys frame 0 with the value from before animating, so the motion starts where the stand-in was. Calls never get a frame 0 key.

Timeline: selecting, moving and editing keys ​

  • Rotations keep their axis and their turns: a joint or root keyed after turning past 90 degrees about one axis keeps that turn on that axis (it never becomes a half turn on the other two, which rolled the rig between keys), and a turn past 180 degrees or past a whole turn keeps going the way it was turned. Bakes, merges, imports, exports and a rotation order change keep each curve continuous the same way.

  • Click a key: selects it. Shift+click adds, Ctrl+click toggles. Clicking a main key selects everything under it at that time; double clicking a main key takes every key under it (an actor's main key then includes every object's keys). Bars never join main keys: a main key leaves the blocks starting at its time alone, and a box over only the actor row takes no blocks. Keys and blocks selected together (Shift+click a block after selecting keys) still drag, nudge, duplicate and delete as one undo step ("Move 6 keys and 1 block").

  • Drag a key past 3 px: moves the selected keys (snapping to frames, keys, markers, regions, sound and effect bar ends, and waveform peaks). A chip shows the shift ("+5 f", "snap: Hit marker"), ghosts show where the keys were with a dotted accent trail to each key, and the snap line turns into a 2 px accent line when snapped (amber for a waveform peak). Alt flips snapping. Ctrl+drag moves a copy. One undo step per drag. Keys never go before frame 0.

  • Two call keys that would land on the same frame with different arguments keep both (the second goes to the next free tick, so they stack as two bars); identical ones merge.

  • Box select: drag on empty lane. Shift or Ctrl adds to the selection. The status bar counts keys and channels as you drag, and the keys light up in the Timeline as the box grows; the Inspector, the motion trail and the curve editor follow when you let go, so boxing thousands of keys stays smooth. A box covers keys and blocks alike: on an action row it takes the blocks it touches. A box that starts on the Camera row selects switch keys while it stays there, and one that starts on an action row's empty space selects blocks while it stays in that row; once either reaches other rows it selects the keys and blocks of every row it covers (switch keys never mix with keys or blocks: the last selection wins).

  • Double click empty lane space on a row: selects every key of that row (Shift or Ctrl adds).

  • Scale handles: with two or more different times selected, an outlined box with a bar handle on each end appears (16 px out from the keys). Drag a handle to retime the selection around the other end (chip "Scale 120% | pivot: f10"). Shift pivots around the playhead.

  • Spread: select a column of keys (one time, two or more joints) and a hollow handle appears 12 px right of it with a dashed line through the column and a mode chip under it. Drag sideways to offset each joint's keys in time: Random (anything from 0 to the range), Alternate (every other joint by the range), Staircase (evenly down the rows; drag left and the bottom row leads). Whole frames; Alt exact ticks. Keys of one joint move together. The chip reads "Spread 10 f | Staircase"; click the chip or use the key menu's Spread submenu to change the mode and Reroll random. After a spread the same keys stay a column, so a new drag replaces the offsets.

  • Spread whole actors: a column of main keys on two or more actors (every keyed row of each actor at that time, for example clicking the main keys) spreads the actors instead, with the same handle, modes and chip: each actor's keys, blocks, attach keys, spawn time and actor markers from that time move by its offset, one undo step ("Spread 3 actors"). Blocks that start at a spread joint column's time on that joint's row move with its keys too.

  • Key menu (right click a key): Ease... (E); Interpolation (Constant, Linear, Cubic, Eased, Bezier); Key type (Key, Breakdown, Hold); Tangents (Auto, Smooth, Flat, Linear, Broken) (each choice is also a command, "Interpolation: Linear", "Key type: Breakdown", "Tangents: Flat": bind one in Settings > Shortcuts, or find it with F3; a bound key shows beside it in this menu); Euler filter; Spread; Copy; Paste; Paste mirrored; Paste to another actor (a list of actors); Duplicate; Lock (a lock between the selected keys on each hand or foot row); Group keys (Ungroup when a group is selected); Set region to selection (Loop region, Export region); Delete N keys; and Reset speed when a selected sound key was stretched.

  • Copy / Paste (Shift+C, Shift+V): copies keys, action and generator blocks, Camera switch keys and attach keys together (the status bar says what it copied), each kept as it was, so a paste still works after the originals are deleted. Paste goes at the playhead with the same spacing, as one undo step, and selects the copies. With another single actor selected, keys, blocks and attach keys paste onto that actor: blocks onto its action track of the same name (a new one when it has none), attach keys onto the same subject. Paste follows the selection: select where it should go after copying and paste lands there, at the playhead. A selected block sends copied blocks to its lane (a constraint, generator or driver block onto that row's lane; a Lock onto another joint drops its held spot and holds where its new part is), a selected row sends them to that row's lane (made when it has none), and keys copied from one row go onto a selected row or a selected key's row of the same kind (a toast says why when it can't: a constraint needs a root or joint row, a Lock a joint's, keys a row of the same value type). Whichever you selected last counts. Right after copying, the copied items are still selected, so pasting puts the copy back on its own row. A switch or attach key landing on a frame already taken moves the pasted group on a frame at a time. Paste mirrored (Shift+N) sends a joint's keys to its mirror partner with the flipped channels negated, mirrors root keys across the scene's mirror plane, and says in the status line how many keys it left out (a joint whose rest frame does not mirror its partner's exactly).

  • Duplicate (Shift+D) puts a copy right after the selection (one frame past the last key). Delete (Del) removes the selected keys. Nudge: [ and ] move the selected keys (and any selected blocks) one frame, Shift+[ and Shift+] ten frames. Shift+Left and Shift+Right nudge one frame too.

  • Key groups: select keys on a row (two times or more), right click > Group keys. On the actor's row the main keys over a group's span become one amber bar too (overlapping groups make one), standing for everything under it. On that track's row and its channel rows the keys become one group key bar: a flat-topped hexagon as tall as a key, its 45 degree points reaching 6 px past the first key and the last (never narrower than 24 px, centred on its keys when they sit closer), with "6 keys" inside when the bar has room for the text and 12 px each side. It turns the accent colour when all its keys are selected, keeps its colour with an accent outline when only some are, and glows while hovered. Click the bar selects all its keys; drag moves them as one (snapping, Ctrl copies, the scale handles retime it); Del deletes them. Right click > Ungroup shows them one by one again. Actor rows still show their main keys. A group is editor only: the keys play the same.

  • Stacked keys: a crowd of keys on a row (four or more in a run) sitting almost on top of each other at the current zoom (less than a quarter of a key's width from the one before) draws as one bar; fewer, or further apart, stay as their own keys; bars that would overlap each other, or the keys between them, merge into one bar, and zoomed far out a key group touching them joins in too (closer in it stays its own bar with its count and Ungroup), the same group key bar a key group draws, from the first of them to the last (at least 24 px wide and centred on its keys, so even two keys a frame apart read as a bar), with no count inside. It is drawn selected when every key in it is, and in its own colour with an accent outline when only some are. On an actor row a stack of main keys is the amber main key colour. Hovering it says how many keys it holds and where, for example "12 keys, f10 to f14". Clicking it selects every key in it (Shift adds them, Ctrl toggles them, a press on one only partly selected takes them all), dragging it moves them all, and box select catches them as usual. Zoom in and the bar splits into its keys. Actor rows' main keys stack the same way. Unlike a key group it is only how the row is drawn: nothing is saved, and the key menu offers no Ungroup for it.

  • Big selections: with more than 500 keys selected, dragging them, scaling them with the box handles or spreading them moves only the keys drawn in the Timeline while you drag (snapping and the chip still work); the scene and the viewport catch up once, when you let go, still as one undo step. The status bar says "Preview resumes on release." At 500 keys or fewer the scene and the viewport follow the drag live. Blocks work the same way: moving more than 500 blocks slides them on the Timeline and applies the move when you let go. So does the curve editor: the keys and the curves you're moving reshape as you drag, the tangent handles hide until you let go, and the rest catches up on release.

  • Cancel a drag: Esc or a right click while dragging anything in the Timeline (keys, blocks, grips, switch and attach keys, markers, regions, the playhead, a box select, the graph's keys and handles, ease handles) ends it with nothing changed: the scene, the selection and the playhead go back to how they were when the drag began, no undo step is made, and the status bar says "Drag cancelled."

  • Esc clears the key selection. Select all keys has no default key (Edit menu).

  • Edit > Select keys: Before the playhead, After the playhead, At the playhead (the whole column on that frame), and by key type: Keys, Breakdowns, Holds. Edit > Invert key selection selects every key that isn't selected. Each has a shortcut id with no default key, to bind in File > Customize Shortcuts.

  • Euler filter (key menu, row menu, Split's Curves menu): moves rotation keys by whole turns so none flips the long way round; the status line says how many keys moved, or that none needed it.

  • Rotation (row menu > Rotation): converts a CFrame track to any Euler order (XYZ, XZY, YXZ, YZX, ZXY, ZYX) or Quaternion; every keyed pose stays. A Quaternion rotation key is one key to every tool (its four channels move together).

  • Ease window: E, the Ease button in the track column header, or the key menu's Ease... opens a floating window for the selected keys (it opens only when they have a next key to ease toward; otherwise the status bar says why). It stays open and follows the selection, and Studio remembers where it sits. From the top: Ease with the range and a + that saves the shared curve as a preset; the Curve section (the canvas with its two handles and X1 Y1 X2 Y2; dragging a handle reshapes the curve, the fields and the scene live, one undo step on release), which folds and stays folded (also Settings > Curves > Show the curve in the Ease window); Recent, the last 8 presets applied anywhere (this window, the Library, the Ease view), newest first, shown once you have applied one; then a search and every preset, your saved ones first. One click on a card applies it, right click a saved one to delete it (it asks first: a deleted preset can't be undone). With no segment selected everything greys out under "Select keys to ease them." Esc, its close button or E again closes it.

Timeline: call rows, sound bars and effect bars ​

  • Calls are methods keyed as events: a sound's Play() and Stop(), an emitter's Emit() and Clear(), a Trail's Clear(), a VideoFrame's Play() and Pause(). They run while the Timeline plays (not while scrubbing) and in games. Each key is a tick; Emit() ticks show "×5" and a sound's Play() shows its bar.
  • A call row's value slot shows "×5" (a key at the playhead), "N playing" (sound copies sounding there), a burst's life ("0.4 to 1.2 s") or "Loading".
  • Sound bars (Play() rows): each key is a bar from its flag to where the sound ends, with the sound's name ("Roar ×3"), a speed chip when the speed is not 1, a waveform, and up to 4 stacked sub-lanes for overlaps. A later Stop() key cuts the bar. View > Sound rows chooses Compact (24 px plus 20 per extra lane) or Expanded (40 plus 34, label above the waveform).
    • Drag the left edge (below the flag): trims the start; the key moves and the audio stays where it was. Drag the right edge: trims the end (clamped to the sound's length unless it loops); double click it to clear the end trim. Ctrl+drag the right edge: stretches the playback speed (0.25x to 4x, pitch follows; chip like "1.25×"). Right click a stretched key > Reset speed.
    • Fade grips appear on hover or when selected: drag to fade in or out (a fade draws as one curve in its shape, from the bar's bottom at silence to its top at full volume, the part under it shaded; the waveform tapers; the two fades cannot cross). Right click a fade for Fade shape (Linear, Equal power, Slow start, S-curve) and Remove fade in / out.
    • A looped Sound shows a loop highlight, a loop icon and a handle at each end of its loop; drag them to move the loop (it needs a valid pair).
    • Dragging an edge, a fade, or the playhead over a bar plays short audio snippets (Settings > Audio). The snippet ends where the playhead's frame ends, so a hit sounds when the playhead reaches it on the waveform.
    • The flag and the body of a bar act as the key (select, move, right click). Right clicking anywhere on a bar (its edges too) opens the key menu led by Start at f42 and End at f42 (the playhead, in the ruler's units): each does exactly what dragging that edge to the playhead does (Trim start / Trim end), one undo step, for that bar. Greyed with a short reason on the right when the playhead is out of that edge's reach ("The playhead is past its end", "It starts there"); a bar a Stop() key cuts has no end ("A Stop() key ends it"). After them, Fit scene length to this sound makes the scene end where the sound ends (grows or shrinks it, rounded up to a frame; greyed for a looped sound with no end, a bar a Stop() key cuts, or when it already fits), so a long sound plays to its end.
    • Changing a sound's SoundId (or Looped, PlaybackSpeed, its regions), or an emitter's Lifetime, Rate, TimeScale or Enabled, in the Properties window or anywhere else, re-measures its bars and waveform at once. Properties that have their own track are read from the track instead.
    • Waveforms can be turned off in View > Waveforms, Settings > Audio > Show waveforms, and a sound row's menu (fades, loops and peak snapping keep working).
    • Snap to waveform peaks: keys, the playhead, markers and regions snap to a sound's loud hits ("snap: Roar peak", amber snap line). Toggle: Snap menu / Settings > Timeline > Waveform peaks. Peak sensitivity (Settings > Audio: Low, Medium or High, Medium): Low snaps only to the loudest hits, High to quieter hits closer together too.
  • Effect bars (emitters): each Emit() key shows a lime burst bar, solid to the particle's minimum life and fading to its maximum life, with "×20" on it, in sub-lanes like Compact sounds. A Clear() key or the scene end cuts a bar square. An emitter that is Enabled with Rate above 0 shows a thinner stream band on its object row.
    • Hover a bar: a lime outline, a round knob (the min life, a line through the bar) and an end grip (the max life). Drag the end grip or the knob: the viewport updates live and the readout says "Max life 1.20 s" / "Min life 0.40 s". A burst's grips set that key's own life; a stream's grips set the emitter's Lifetime property (keyed when Lifetime is animated and Auto-key is on). Double click a grip to go back to the emitter's Lifetime.
    • Keys snap to a bar's ends ("SlashEmitter max life"); the playhead and markers do too.
    • Right click a burst bar (its grips too): the key menu leads with End at f42, the max life grip dragged to the playhead (one undo step "Set max life on SlashEmitter.Emit()"); greyed "The playhead is before its min life" or "A Clear() key cuts it". A burst starts at its key, so there is no Start at.
  • Lanes you can arrange (sound bars, burst bars and action blocks): drag a bar's body up or down past half a lane and it moves to the lane under the pointer (at most one lane past the row's last; still 4 lanes at most). Moving sideways in the same drag still moves it in time (Alt flips snapping); the chip adds "| lane 3". The bar keeps that lane; if another bar already holds that lane where it is, it stacks as usual instead. Only the bar you drag changes lane. One Ctrl+Z step ("Move to lane 2"). Right click it > Reset lane lets it stack on its own again.

Timeline: action tracks and blocks ​

  • An actor can have any number of action tracks (actor row menu > Add action track). A track holds blocks: an instant (an 18 px chip centred on its frame, its title beside it) or a block with a duration (a bar from its start to its end, icon and title). Blocks are in the actor's colour; the Event block shows a flag and reads "Event: Hit" (its name), a custom action shows its module's Icon image or a bolt and its Name (else the module name).
  • Custom actions are the ModuleScripts tagged AcesMotionAction, wherever you keep them (keep them where the server and clients can require them, such as ReplicatedStorage). A module returns the contract table, or just a function for a quick instant action (Name, Icon, HasDuration, Params, Run, optional Scrub and Recast; an optional Description on the module and on each param, the tooltip of its blocks and of that param in the Inspector). They load in Studio while Settings > Actions > Run custom actions in Studio is on, and reload when a module is edited, added or removed. Errors in Run, its cleanup, Scrub and Recast are caught and warned in Output (a Scrub error once per block, since it runs every step), in Studio and in games, so a broken action never stops the scene.
  • Add a block: right click an empty spot of the lane (or the track's header) > Add block: Event (an instant), or a custom action (a 0.5 s bar for a module with HasDuration, clamped to the scene end, else an instant), at the clicked frame (the playhead from the header). Its parameters start from each param's Default.
  • Overlapping blocks stack in up to 4 sub-lanes (row 24, 44, 64, 84 px; compact rows 20, 36, 52, 68, blocks 14 high). Empty sub-lanes close up: a row is as tall as the lanes its blocks use, so a lone block sits on the first lane whatever lane it was moved to (the order top to bottom is kept). Drag a block or chip up or down to put it in another lane (Ctrl+drag puts the copy there); the block menu's Reset lane undoes that (see Lanes you can arrange).
  • Select: click a block (keys let go; selecting keys lets blocks go); Shift+click adds; Ctrl+click toggles; drag on an empty spot of an action lane box-selects blocks; double click an empty spot selects every block of the track; click an empty spot clears; Esc clears.
  • Move: drag a block's body: every selected block moves by the same time on its own track (the earliest stops at frame 0); the originals stay as faint copies joined by a dotted line, the chip reads "+12 f" or "−12 f | snap: Toggle Trail end". Ctrl+drag copies instead. One undo step ("Move 1 block on Brute.Actions").
  • Trim: a bar shows a grip at each end on hover or selection; drag one to change the start (the end stays) or the end (at least one frame long); a "Length 60 f" read-out (ruler units) follows. One undo step ("Trim the end of Toggle Trail"). The end stops at the scene end. A block that runs past the scene end (the scene was shortened) draws cut off there and keeps its end grip at that edge, so it can be dragged back.
  • Start at / End at (the block menu, under Reset lane, for blocks with a duration): Start at f42 / End at f42 put that edge at the playhead (ruler units), exactly as dragging the grip there. With several blocks selected it trims every one the playhead suits, one undo step ("Start 3 blocks at f42"); greyed with a short reason on the right when none can ("The playhead is past its end", "It ends there"). Instants have no edges, so a selection of only instants leaves the items out.
  • Snapping: block drags snap to frames, keys, markers, region edges, the playhead and the edges of other blocks and bars (a bar's start or end, whichever is near); keys, markers, the playhead and the other bars snap to blocks ("Toggle Trail start", "Toggle Trail end", "Event: Hit"). Alt flips snapping.
  • Shift+D duplicates the selected blocks right after the selection (one frame past its last frame) and selects the copies; Del (while the Timeline has focus) or the block menu's Delete deletes them.
  • A block whose module is missing or failed to load keeps its place and data and draws dashed red on a red tint with a warning icon; its tooltip says why ("Module missing. Put ScreenPulse in the AcesMotionActions folder." / "Could not load. ..."). It still moves, trims, duplicates and deletes. It never shows while custom actions are off or before the modules finish loading.
  • A muted track draws at 50% (its blocks don't run); a locked track or actor, a muted track and a read-only scene refuse edits (the status bar says why, "Unlock Brute or this track to edit it."); selecting still works.
  • Rename: the track menu's Rename track (or F2 with the track's header selected) edits the name in the header; Enter keeps it, clicking away cancels.
  • In Studio (the preview): duration blocks run their module as the playhead enters them and clean up as it leaves, playing or scrubbing; instants (Event, one shot custom actions) fire only while playing, or also while scrubbing forward with Settings > Actions > Fire instant actions while scrubbing. An Event shows "Event: Hit" in the status bar. Every block is cleaned up before each undo step and when the scene closes.
  • Recasting while a block runs (a game's Track:SetCast, or in Studio a spawned actor appearing or a stand-in deleted or restored): a running block whose params use a changed role (an Actor param naming it, an ActorPart or Joint param starting with "Role/"; a module without Params counts as using every role) calls its module's Recast(context, params, changedRoles) and keeps running; a module without Recast is restarted (cleanup, then Run against the new actor).
  • Hover a block, a grip, a track's name or its count for its tooltip (when it runs, how to edit it, or why it can't be).

Timeline: spawned actors ​

  • A spawned actor (an actor that appears and disappears during the scene: a model, NPC, house, car or effect) acts like any actor in Studio. While the scene is open its preview copy (a copy of its template, never saved) is its stand-in: click it in the viewport, pose it, key it, see its properties in the Inspector. In a game nothing exists before it appears: the runtime clones the template then and removes it after.
  • Its template is the object it's made from. Adding one from an object in Workspace (+ > Add selected template as a spawned actor, or Convert to spawned actor) moves that object to ReplicatedStorage.AcesMotionTemplates, so a game never shows it early (one undo step). Templates carry the AcesMotionTemplate tag and a permanent id: rename or move them anywhere and the actor still finds them.
  • The Spawn row ("Lifetime", first under the actor; the actor row keeps its main keys): one bar like an action block, pressing it shows the actor's Spawn section in the Inspector, from where it appears to where it disappears, or to the scene end (then it has no end grip). Hover lights its grips. Drag the body to move both ends, drag a grip to move one (snaps; Alt skips; one undo step). Its label shows the role and "f40 to f88" when there's room. Right click the bar or the row: Appear at, Disappear at, Stay until the end, Linger after disappearing, Select template, Add actor marker, Convert to placed actor.
  • Hide outside its time (default): in Studio the copy hides while the playhead is outside its time, as in a game. Always show keeps it visible. Settings > Timeline > Spawned actors, View > Spawned actors, and the Inspector's Spawn section.
  • Inspector > Spawn section (on top when its copy is selected, like the Camera section): Template (Use selection, Select template), Appears at, Disappears at (Stays to the end), When it goes (Instant | Linger), Lifetime (seconds it lingers, or stays after the scene ends), Socket, Parent to, Preview, and Save look to template: writes the copy's unkeyed look (sizes, colours, any property) into the template (one undo step; keyed values aren't baked in; parts keep the template's Anchored and Massless).

Timeline: the Camera track ​

  • The Camera track is one row pinned at the top of the dope sheet (and of Split's dope rows), 30 px high (26 in compact rows); it never scrolls, the other rows scroll under it. It only shows once the scene has a camera actor (or switch keys); add one with the + menu > Add camera at view. Its header: the camera icon, "Camera", how many switch keys it holds, and Mute and Lock on hover (always shown while set). Click the header to select the row; right click it for Select all switch keys, Mute (Unmute) and Lock (Unlock). Muting draws the track at 50% and the first unmuted camera in the cast is live instead (in games too); a locked or muted track and a read-only scene refuse every edit and say why in the status bar.
  • A switch key makes a camera actor live from its frame. Each draws as a shot bar (the action bar look in the camera's tint): the key is the solid chip with the camera icon at the bar's left end, the bar runs to the next key (1 px short) or the scene end, or ends early at the key's Until, with the camera's name (hidden under 60 px, cut with "..." when long). The shot under the playhead has a stronger outline and the Live pill at its right end (hidden under 116 px).
  • Before the first key no camera is live, and after a shot's Until up to the next key neither: the lane is empty there; Studio keeps your own view and games give the player's camera back. With no keys, one dashed implied bar reads "Wide is live. Right click to switch cameras." (the first camera in the cast); it also covers frame 0 up to the first key when no key has a usable camera or the track is muted (then that first camera is live the whole scene).
  • Shot length: each shot has the action blocks' end grip at its right end (on hover and selection). Drag it to cut the shot short: it sets the key's Until, snapping like other edges (Alt skips), with the guide and a "Length 40 f" read-out under the row; at least one frame after the key; drag it onto the next key (the last shot: the scene end) to run to it again. One undo step ("Trim the end of CloseUp", "Run CloseUp to the next switch"). Moving or duplicating a key carries its Until; inserting or removing time shifts it. A key whose camera actor is gone draws dashed red with a warning icon, "Missing camera"; a key naming a muted camera draws at 50%. Both are skipped: the camera before them stays live. Keys past the scene end aren't drawn.
  • A cut-short shot keeps its last frame: the camera is still live on the frame its end grip sits on (inclusive), so you can pilot it and set a last key there; no camera is live after it until the next key.
  • Shot start: a start grip at its left end too (the action blocks' start grip). Drag it to start the shot later or earlier while its end stays put (a shot that runs to the next key keeps running to it); it stops one frame after the previous key and one frame before the shot's end, snaps (Alt skips) and reads "Length 1.20 s". One undo step "Trim the start of CloseUp".
  • No camera actor: the lane reads "No camera actor in this scene." with an Add camera at view button.
  • Camera switch lines: every key that works drops a dashed vertical line in its camera's tint from the Camera row down through all the rows (like marker lines, which draw over them); a dragged key's line follows it. Off with Show camera switch lines (the camera menu, View > Camera, Settings > Camera).
  • Select: click a shot (keys, blocks and markers let go; the Inspector shows the Camera switch panel, also when the shot was already selected); Shift+click adds; Ctrl+click toggles; drag on an empty spot of the lane box-selects the keys whose left ends it touches (down into the rows, it selects their keys and blocks instead, see Box select); double click an empty spot selects every key; Esc clears.
  • Move: drag a shot: the selected keys move in time only (the neighbouring bars resize live, the earliest stops at frame 0); a frame another key holds is skipped (keys never share a frame); the old keys stay as faint chips joined by a dotted line; the chip reads "+2 f | snap: Hit marker". Ctrl+drag copies instead. One undo step ("Move 1 switch key", "Duplicate 1 switch key").
  • Snapping: switch key drags snap to frames, keys, markers, region edges, the playhead and every bar's and block's edges; keys, blocks, bars, markers, regions, the playhead and the scene end snap to switch keys ("Switch to Wide"). Alt flips snapping. Switch keys never join an actor's main keys.
  • Shift+D copies the selected keys one frame past the last one (spacing and cameras kept, on past any taken frame) and selects the copies; Del (Timeline focused) deletes them; the shot before them grows back, and with no keys left the track is implied again.
  • Lane menu (right click an empty spot): Switch to (the camera actors, the one live at that frame ticked; picking one adds a key at the clicked frame, or changes the key already there: "Switch to CloseUp at f48", "Switch key to Top"; greyed with no camera actor) and Add camera at view (a new camera actor at Studio's view and a key naming it at the clicked frame, one undo step). Key menu (right click a shot, which selects it): Switch to (changes the selected keys' camera), Switch at f84 (the playhead: a new switch key there naming the camera you pick, or on a key already there, its camera changes; puts a camera live again after a shot ends), Start at f42 (moves that shot's key to the playhead, its end staying put; greyed "Another switch key is there" or "The playhead is past its end") and End at f42 (its Until at the playhead, or back to the next key when the playhead is on it), each one undo step and for the clicked shot only, Extend to the next switch (Extend to the scene end on the last shot; ticked while the shot already runs there, enabled once its end was cut short: drops the cut so the shot runs to the next switch again and follows it when keys move), then Duplicate (Shift+D), Delete, Show in Inspector.
  • Add camera at view (anywhere: the + menu, the Cast tab, the Camera row) also adds a switch key for the new camera: at the scene's start when it's the first switch, else at the playhead (the lane menu's at the clicked frame), so its shot shows at once. One undo step.
  • Virtual cameras: a camera made with Add camera at view lives only in the scene's data, never as a Camera in your place. While its scene is open the editor shows it with a temporary Camera in Workspace (named after it, never saved) that you pick, pose, key and look through like before; closing the scene removes it, and in a game the camera actor drives the player's camera. A camera actor you made from a Camera of your own keeps that one.
  • Remove the Camera row: right click the Camera row's header > Remove camera track removes every switch key and every camera actor (with their keys), so the row goes; the Camera objects stay in the place. One undo step.
  • Camera in game: press the Camera row's header and the Inspector shows how a game's camera goes into the scene's shots and back: Blend in (time and ease: Smooth, Linear, Ease in, Ease out) from the player's view into the first shot, Blend out back at the end, and When stopped early (Like the end, Snap back, or Blend back over its own time and ease) for a scene the game stops with Stop(). Times follow the ruler; 0 cuts. Saved with the scene; a game's play options override each. One undo step per change.
  • The status bar says "1 switch key selected on Camera" ("... Its camera is missing." for a missing one), "Moving 1 switch key on Camera. Alt flips snapping.", "Copying ...".
  • Tooltips: a shot "Switch to CloseUp: Live from f40 to f84. Drag to move, drag its start or end to trim it. Right click for Switch to, Start at, End at, Duplicate and Delete." (a cut-short shot adds "No camera is live from there to f84."); the end grip "Shot end"; a missing one and a muted camera's one say why they're skipped; the implied bar, the Live pill, the header name and its count each have theirs.
  • [ ] (and Shift with them, 10 frames) nudge the selected switch keys, on past any frame another key holds; one undo step.

Timeline: screen effects ​

  • Screen effects are one Screen row per scene, added from the + menu, the Cast tab's + menu or the Camera row's header menu (Add screen effects; greyed with the reason once the scene has them, or while it is read only). Its row sits at the top of the actors, right under the Camera row, and it keeps nothing in your place: the editor previews it on a temporary object while the scene is open.
  • What it holds, each keyed like any property (its rows show once keyed; the Inspector lists them all): letterbox bars (Bars top and Bars bottom, each a % of the screen's height from 0 to 50, so a bar takes the same share on every screen; Bars colour), Vignette (0 to 1) and its colour, Blur (0 to 56), Fade (0 to 1: 1 covers the screen) and Fade colour (black, white or any colour), colour correction (Brightness, Contrast and Saturation from -1 to 1, Tint) and bloom (Intensity, Size, Threshold). Everything starts neutral, and colour correction, blur and bloom add on top of the game's own Lighting effects.
  • Depth of field is per camera: a camera actor has Focus distance (studs), Focus on (an actor or part: focus follows it, measured from the camera every frame), In-focus radius, Near blur and Far blur, keyed on the camera, so each shot keeps its own lens.
  • Quick actions (the Screen row's header and lane menus, and the Camera row's Screen effects submenu, at the playhead): Fade out here (fades to the fade colour over a second and stays faded to the scene's end), Fade in here (starts covered and clears over a second), Flash here, Bars in and Bars out (keys sliding both bars over half a second), and Bars preset (keys both bars to 1.85:1, 2:1, 2.39:1 or 2.76:1 on a 16:9 screen).
  • Reset to default (right click a value's row in the Inspector) puts a screen effect or lens value back to its neutral default.
  • In the Inspector the Screen row shows Screen effects (chip "Scene"): a section per group (Bars, Vignette, Blur, Fade, Colour, Bloom), each value a slider (Brightness, Contrast and Saturation fill from 0 in the middle) or a colour swatch, with its key diamond; drag a slider (one undo step on release, Esc puts it back) or click its number to type. A camera actor shows a Lens section: Focus distance and In-focus radius in studs, Focus on (pick an actor or part, or Use selection; while set, Focus distance is greyed: Focus on decides it), Near blur and Far blur, and the note "Depth of field is off while Near and Far blur are both 0." while both are 0. The Timeline names these rows "Top bar", "Fade", "Near blur" and so on.
  • Hold / Fade is a generator that holds a number at its Value, easing in and out by its fades: a Fade block is a Hold on Fade. Between Hold blocks a value with no keys goes back to its rest.
  • Library presets: the Generators tab's Screen effects group (Fade to black, held to the scene's end; Fade from black; Flash white; Letterbox, both bars in and out again: stretch it over the shot; Vignette pulse) goes on the Screen row's own rows at the playhead whatever is selected, and adds the screen effects first when the scene has none.
  • In the viewport: the effects and the live camera's lens show while you look through a camera, as players see them. Show screen effects (the camera menu, View > Camera, Settings > Camera): While looking through (the default), Always or Never. They draw over the whole viewport.
  • In games the runtime draws them on each player's screen while a play drives the player's camera, and they ease in and out with the camera's Blend in and Blend out. Screen = false in the play options plays the camera without them; with Camera = "Manual" nothing is drawn and GetCamera() returns the values (Screen, Lens) for your own camera code. A native export (Save to Roblox) can't hold them.
  • The Screen row can't be soloed, spawned or made a game target (it is the view, not the performance).

Timeline: attached rows (Attach, Detach, Back to rig) ​

  • Attached rows: every object with attach keys gets a row under its actor, after the Spawn row: "Attached · Root" for the actor itself, "Attached · Sword" for a joint or bone. The icon says what is attached: a link for the actor, a target for a rig joint, a bone for a skinned-mesh bone. Mute (the attachment is off) and lock (its keys stay put) work as on other rows, several selected rows at once; search and the Muted / Locked filters include them.
  • Keys: Attach (two linked rings), Detach (two rings apart), Back to rig (a return arrow).
  • Bands: from an Attach key to the next key (or the scene end) a bar in the target actor's colour, labelled with the target ("Defender · LeftHand"); on a short bar the actor's name drops, then the text shortens, then goes. A missing target is a red dashed bar ("LeftHand missing"), a target that depends on the object an amber one ("Loop, ignored"). After a Detach key a grey dashed "Detached" bar runs to the next key. A Back to rig key snapping back into place with a blend draws a ramp as long as the blend.
  • Gestures: click a key, bar or ramp to select its key and show the Attach panel (Shift adds, Ctrl toggles). Drag to move the selected attach keys, Ctrl+drag to copy, Alt flips snapping; a frame another key of the same object holds is skipped. Double-click an empty spot of the row to select all its keys; drag on empty lane to box-select (attach keys are taken when the box holds no other keys or blocks). Keys, markers and bars snap to attach keys ("Sword key").
  • Keys on the keyboard: Del deletes the selected attach keys, Shift+D copies them one frame after the last, [ ] and Shift+Left / Shift+Right nudge them, Esc clears them; the Edit menu's Duplicate, Delete and Nudge act on them too.
  • Group keys: the actor row's main keys stand for its attach keys too, so clicking, dragging (Ctrl copies), nudging, Shift+D and Del on a main key move them with the actor's other keys in one undo step; a box over keyed rows and Attached rows takes both. Dragging an attach key that's selected along with keys moves them all.
  • Menus: right-click a row's lane: Attach here to..., Detach here and, on a joint's row, Back to rig here (snaps into place) and Back to rig here (where it is) (greyed with the reason where they can't apply), plus Select target on a bar and Delete key on a key. Right-click the row's name: Mute, Lock, Remove attachment (asks first with more than one key). The actor's name menu and its row's lane menu offer Attach to... and Detach for the whole actor at that frame (an actor never goes back to a rig).
  • Status bar: "1 attach key selected on Sword", "3 attach keys selected", "Moving 1 attach key on Cup. Alt flips snapping."

Timeline: generators ​

  • Generators drive a value live over a span, on top of its keys: a camera keyed from A to B shakes while it travels. Right click a track's (or a channel's) empty lane space > Add generator > Shake, Wiggle, Oscillate, Flicker / Strobe / Pulse, Spin, Recoil, Overshoot / Settle, Stepped, Hold / Fade, Cycle / Flipbook or Typewriter (then your custom generators, Use selected module as a generator and New custom generator...): a Generators lane appears right under that row, holding the block. A new block spans the keys selected on that row, else a second from where you clicked. Right click a lane's empty space to add another there. Add generator is also in a row header's right-click menu (a joint, root, property or channel row; an actor row adds it on its root; when that row is one of the selected rows, every selected row gets one, one undo step) and in the Inspector's property row menu (every selected object's property, at the playhead). Overlapping blocks stack in sub-lanes; lanes apply in order, sub-lanes top to bottom.
  • What they drive: numbers (FieldOfView, Brightness...), Vector3, Vector2, UDim and UDim2 (a GUI's Position or Size shakes), colours (per channel), NumberRanges and NumberSequences / ColorSequences (particles, beams, trails), an actor's root and joints (position and rotation axes), and on/off values (Flicker / Strobe / Pulse switch Enabled). Cycle / Flipbook steps through a list of any values (Materials and other enums, image ids, text) and Typewriter types out text. Generators that can't drive a row's value are greyed with the reason (enums, text and assets stay keys only). A property with no keys can take one too: its row appears for the lane.
  • Blocks are drawn in the generator magenta with its icon, its name and preset ("Shake · Handheld") and its blend chip ("×" for Multiply, "Replace"; Add shows none). Drag to move (Ctrl copies), drag an edge to trim, drag a top corner grip to fade it in or out (the fades draw like a sound's: one curve in its shape, the part under it shaded). They move with keys in a box select that covers their lane.
  • Block menu: Blend (Add, Multiply, Replace), Fade in shape, Fade out shape, Fit to playback range (moves the selected generator blocks onto the playback range: the loop region, else the whole scene; for Snap to Loop), Reseed, Save as preset... (keeps its settings as one of your presets and shows it in the Library's Generators tab with its name ready to type), Bake to keys (writes its result as a key every frame on the track it drives and removes the block, one undo step; other blocks on the lane stay live), Duplicate, Delete.
  • Settings rows: click a Generators lane's arrow to open it: under each block, a header row (the block's name) then one row per number setting and Influence, drawing that setting's keys. The value column reads the setting at the playhead. Setting keys select like any key: click one to put the playhead on it and show its block in the Inspector scrolled to that setting, Shift + click to add more, drag a box over setting rows (with other rows too), then drag, nudge or delete them together (snaps like keys, Alt skips); right click for Delete key. A box or a click on one block's setting keys shows that block in the Inspector at that setting. Click a settings row's name to show its block in the Inspector at that setting. Moving a block (or changing its Start) takes its setting keys with it; copies get their own.
  • Custom generators are the ModuleScripts tagged AcesMotionGenerator, wherever you keep them (somewhere the server and clients can require, such as ReplicatedStorage). They appear in the Add generator menu after the built-ins (one that fails to load is greyed with "(could not load)"). Use selected module as a generator tags the ModuleScripts selected in the Explorer; New custom generator... makes a typed, commented template inside the Explorer selection (else ReplicatedStorage), adds its first block on that row and opens the script. A module returns a table with Offset(seconds, duration, channel, settings, noise, kind) (kind: "Number", "Vector3", "CFrame"... optional to read) (how far to move one channel: studs and degrees for a CFrame's six channels) and optional Description, Kinds, Blend, Params (Number, Integer, Boolean, Enum: shown and keyable in the Inspector) and DefaultAxes. Keep Offset pure (use noise for randomness) so every player sees the same thing. Blocks keep the module's id (its AcesMotion_GeneratorId attribute; an older module's AcesMotion_ActionId still works and is copied over the next time the plugin touches it), so renaming or moving it keeps them working. Editing a tagged module reloads it and the preview follows (another module it requires applies after the plugin reloads).
  • A missing custom generator: its blocks draw failed (with why) once the modules are loaded; they keep their settings and work again when the module is back. Resolve target (block menu and Inspector) points them at another tagged module, the one selected in the Explorer, or a fresh template under the old name.
  • Camera actors: the actor row's menu has Add camera shake: a Shake on its root with the Handheld preset.
  • Hold / Fade holds a number at its Value, easing in and out by the block's fades (the screen effects' fades and letterbox bars). Generators drive attributes too.
  • Blends: Add puts the generator's offsets on top of the keys (a CFrame in the actor's own axes, or the world's with Space); Multiply scales the keyed value (a flicker on a keyed brightness; Amplitude reads as a fraction); Replace takes over inside the block from the value at its start. On/off values Replace (the pattern) or Multiply (on only where both are on).
  • Random generators are seeded: the same Seed plays the same in Studio, exports and games; Reseed rolls a new one.

Timeline: constraints ​

  • Constraints drive an actor's root or a rig joint from other things, on top of its keys: aim a camera at a character, copy a prop's rotation, keep a head inside its limits. Right click a root or joint row's header, its lane's empty space, or a Stack lane > Add constraint (right-click a selected row: with several rows selected, the pick goes on every root and joint row among them in one undo step, a target picked once for all, rows that can't take it skipped and counted): Track (Track To, Damped Track, Locked Track), Copy (Copy Location, Copy Rotation, Copy Transforms), Limit (Limit Location, Limit Rotation, Limit Distance), Relationship (Floor, Lock on a joint row, and Child Of, which starts Attach to, Shift+G), Motion (Delay, Face Motion, Horizon Lock), then Custom (your own constraint modules, see below).
  • Space and Relative to (Limit Rotation, Limit Location, Copy Location, Copy Rotation, Copy Transforms, Horizon Lock): World measures on the world's axes; Local measures from what Relative to picks: Parent (what it hangs from: the part above a joint; for an actor's root, the scene origin), Rest pose (where it rests as built, on the part's own axes, whatever its rig's joint offsets: 0 is its rest pose; for a root, the scene origin) or Object (an actor or part you pick, with the same list, viewport pick and Use selection as a target; it can't be the subject or what it carries). The Relative to dropdown shows only on Local. Limit Rotation starts on Local relative to its Rest pose, the others on World. An Object that's missing (none picked, its role not in the scene, its part gone) draws the block failed as "Relative to missing" and it does nothing until it's back. The Limit Location box and Limit Rotation arcs in the viewport draw in that frame. Horizon Lock on Local keeps level with what it's relative to (a camera level with a ship's tilting deck).
  • Floor's Contact: Point (the default) keeps the subject's pivot above the floor; Bounding box keeps the bottom of its bounding box there instead, as it's turned now, so a crate sits on the floor rather than sinking to its middle, and a tipped one rests on its lowest corner. An actor's root uses its whole model's box in the pose at that frame (a rig's parts where their joints put them, so scrubbing and playing never measure the pose shown before), a joint the part it moves; a bone has no box, so it uses its pivot. Offset raises or lowers that contact point, and the viewport guide's plane marks where it stops. Saved presets keep the choice, and the runtime plays it the same way.
  • Floor for the whole actor: on an actor's root row, a Floor's Parts chooses what it holds above the floor: Root (the default, just the root), All parts (every part and bone the rig moves, plus anything it holds), Feet, Hands, Limbs (every arm and leg part) or Picked (tick the parts in the rig's joint tree; the list stays by name if you swap to a rig with the same names). Feet, Hands and Limbs know R15 and R6 names, and bones by the words in their names (foot, toe, ankle; hand, wrist, finger). Resolve by picks how: Lift actor raises the whole actor just enough that the lowest part touches, the pose unchanged; Bend limbs bends a sinking hand or foot up through its arm or leg instead, while the torso or head still lift the actor. Contact (Point or Bounding box) counts for each part. A locked part is never lifted with the actor (lifting can't move it): when it would sink, the Floor pushes it up through its limb instead, whatever Resolve says, so dragging a locked foot down stops it on the floor (with Bounding box, its whole box). Root with Bounding box does this for every locked part. When a set finds nothing on the rig, the Inspector says so ("No feet found on this rig. Pick parts instead.") and the block shows its failed look. In the viewport, a dot marks each part it's holding up and the status line says what it did ("Floor | All parts: lifted 0.4 studs by LeftFoot"). The Library's Feet on the floor preset sets Feet and Bend limbs.
  • Lock (a joint row: a hand, a foot, any joint, creatures too) holds its part where it was at the lock's first frame while the limb above it bends to keep it there (posing the rig, its hips say, first gives a lock with no Spot yet the spot it holds, so posing at the lock's first frame never drags the foot along): planted feet while the hips move, a hand on a wall or a rail. With a Target (an actor, a part, a point) it sticks to the target instead and follows it: picking or changing the target keeps the part where it is and stores its grip on the target, so posing the target moves the part with it, and posing the part moves only its grip (keyed with Auto-key), never the target. Holds: Spot and angle (the default) keeps the part as it was; Spot only keeps where it is and lets it turn with its keys, so a planted foot can roll onto its toes. Chain length: 0 is Auto (the limb, up to the torso); 1 to 8 bends that many joints up, on through the hips or chest for a far reach. The knee or elbow keeps bending the way its keys bend it. A limb that is one joint (an R6 arm or leg) turns at the hip or shoulder to keep its far end on the spot, starting from its held angle; with no knee it can't change its length, so when the hips dip the foot stays on the spot's line and the out-of-reach note shows the difference. Out of reach, the limb points straight at the spot and stops; the viewport's marker turns red with a red line across the gap, and the Inspector says how far short it stops. Weight, fades, the Distance fade and keyable settings work as on every constraint, and locks play the same way in games. A lock is the base of its part's stack: every other constraint on that row (Floor, Limit Location, Copy Location, the aims) applies on top of where the lock holds the part, whatever their order on the lane, and the limb bends to reach the result, so a Floor under a planted foot still lifts it. An actor-wide Floor that bends limbs lifts a locked foot too. A native export bakes them into the keys.
  • Adding a lock: select keys on a hand or foot row and right click > Lock (one lock per row, from its first selected key to its last; with keys on several rows of one actor, its hands and feet get one each); pick a part in the viewport and press Shift+L (or right click > Lock): a lock from the playhead to that row's next key, or a second when it has none; Pins > Turn pins into locks makes each pin a lock from the playhead (an anchored pin's lock targets what it was anchored to) and clears the pins. Each is one undo step, with a toast and its Undo. Add constraint > Lock adds one on a joint row too.
  • The lock's marker: while the playhead is inside a lock (or its block is selected), a sphere marks the spot it holds, with a line to its target when it has one. Posing a locked part while the playhead is inside its lock works like posing any part (IK bends the limb), and the lock goes with it, the way an IK control is keyed in other tools: with Auto-key (or K) the lock's Spot is keyed at that frame, so you can keep one lock over the whole timeline and key the foot wherever it should be, planted from key to key and moving between them. The first Spot key also keys the lock's first frame where it held, so the frames before stay put. With Auto-key off it moves the whole lock (or keys it, once its Spot has keys). Posing the hips or any other joint never moves a locked part. The Spot's keys show as one Spot row under the lock's block (expand its lane), and move, copy and delete together. The locked joint's own keys carry the Spot key at the same time with them: copy a planted foot's keys and paste them later and the foot stands in the same spot (Paste mirrored leaves the Spot out). Every other way a pose reaches a locked part moves its lock the same way: Paste pose, Paste mirrored (the pose), Flip pose, Reset to rest and the pose library put the lock where the new pose puts the part (keyed with Auto-key); Shift+K keys the Spot like K; the tween slider tweens the Spot with the joint; Spread and dragging keys in the curve editor keep the Spot key with its joint's key (the curve editor moves it in time only). One undo step; Esc cancels.
  • A lock holds through its last frame: a foot locked up to f30 is still held at f30.
  • Limit Rotation: a limited axis can turn all the way round before it stops, so a head limited to Y -45 to 45 never looks behind: past a limit it stays at the nearer end, and switches sides only when the target passes straight behind.
  • Motion constraints: Delay copies its target from a few frames earlier (Delay in frames, keyable; Position, Rotation, and Offset to trail from where it is): tails, capes, a second dancer echoing the first. Face Motion points along its own path and leans into turns (Aim and Up axis, Bank 0 to 1, keyable; Look ahead in frames): vehicles, birds, fly-through cameras. Horizon Lock keeps the horizon level under a shake or on a vehicle (Roll on, Pitch off by default). None of them need a target. A property row shows Add constraint greyed: constraints drive positions and rotations.
  • A new constraint's span: the keys selected on that row (first to last); else, on a camera, its current shot (from the switch that made it live to the next one); else from where you clicked (or the playhead) to the scene end.
  • Its target: something selected in the Explorer that's part of the cast (and isn't the subject, or carried by it) becomes the target at once. Otherwise you pick it in the viewport: what you hover highlights in its actor's colour with a dashed line from the subject and a chip ("Cam tracks r2 · Head"); click to pick, Esc adds it without a target (pick one in the Inspector later). Floor and Limit Location work without one.
  • Blocks sit on the same lanes as generators, drawn in the constraint mint with their own icon and their target in the label ("Track To · r2 · Head"). A row whose lane holds a constraint labels it Stack. A new constraint goes on the top sub-lane, above the generators it overlaps, so a camera's shake rides on top of its tracking.
  • One stack per row, in order: a row's generator and constraint blocks apply top to bottom (lanes, then sub-lanes, then start), each on the result of the ones above; moving a block to another sub-lane changes the result. An attached subject runs its attachment first.
  • States: "No target" when it has none yet (it does nothing); a missing target (its role isn't in the scene or the part is gone) draws failed as "Head missing" and does nothing until it's back; a target that depends on what it drives is a loop, drawn "Loop, ignored". A muted block draws faded and does nothing.
  • Block menu: Select target (selects it in the Explorer; for a Point target, Move point moves it with the gizmo), Mute / Unmute, Fade in shape, Fade out shape, Save as preset... (saves its settings, blend, fades and Distance fade as one of your presets and opens it in the Library to name it), Bake to keys, Duplicate, Delete. Drag a top corner grip to fade it in or out; Influence and every number setting are keyable and get their own setting rows under the block, like a generator's.
  • Bake to keys writes the result as a key every frame over the block and removes it, together with every block above it on the row that overlaps it ("Bake to keys (with Track To above)"): the blocks below stay live on the new keys. One undo step. The row header's Bake stack bakes the whole stack.
  • Custom constraints are the ModuleScripts tagged AcesMotionConstraint, wherever you keep them (somewhere the server and clients can require, such as ReplicatedStorage). They appear in the Add constraint menu's Custom group with the constraint icon (one that fails to load is greyed with "(could not load)" and why). Use selected module as a constraint tags the ModuleScripts selected in the Explorer; New custom constraint... makes a typed, commented template inside the Explorer selection (else ReplicatedStorage), adds its first block on that row (its target is picked in the Inspector) and opens the script. A module returns a table with Solve(world, context): where the subject is now (after its keys and the blocks above it) in, where it should be at full strength out. context has Targets (each Target setting's world now), Settings, Time and Duration (seconds into the block, its length), Parent (what it hangs from; a root: the scene origin), Origin (the scene origin), Fps, Rest (the subject from its keys alone), Sample(name, seconds) (a target at another time) and Self(seconds) (the subject at another time). Optional: Description, Drives ({ Position = true, Rotation = true }: those gizmo parts grey out while posing it) and Params: Target (a target the user picks, Optional = true when it works without one), Number (keyable unless Keyable = false), Integer, Boolean, Enum (Options) and Axis (one of the six axes). Influence, fades, the Distance fade and the stack are applied for you. Keep Solve pure (no memory between frames) so every player sees the same thing and scrubbing, SetTime and Bake work. Blocks keep the module's id (its AcesMotion_ConstraintId attribute; an older module's AcesMotion_ActionId still works and is copied over the next time the plugin touches it), so renaming or moving it keeps them working; editing a tagged module reloads it and the preview follows (another module it requires applies after the plugin reloads).
  • A missing custom constraint: its blocks draw failed (with why) once the modules are loaded, keep their settings and work again when the module is back. Resolve target (first in the block menu) points them at another tagged constraint module, the one selected in the Explorer, or a fresh template under the old name; Bake to keys waits until it's back. With Run custom constraints in Studio off, a custom block reads "Hover Bob · off", does nothing in Studio, and its Inspector says why.
  • Baking something live asks first: when a baked constraint follows a target the game fills in (the local player, the nearest player, the camera, a tagged object) or fades by distance, a card at the pointer says what baking freezes ("Cam follows the local player (Hero). Baking freezes it to the stand-in.") with Cancel and Bake anyway. Esc or a click outside cancels.
  • Export bakes a rig's constrained joints and root into its Roblox animation through the whole stack. Constraints on other actors play with the runtime: the export lists "constraints" under what needs it.

Timeline: drivers ​

  • Drivers set a value from something else: a camera's FieldOfView from how far a character is, a light's Brightness from a game-set attribute, a joint's turn from another object's height. Right click a row's header or its lane's empty space > Add driver (with several rows selected, as Add constraint: every selected row, one undo step, the source picked once): Copy (copies one property's whole value), Distance (how far apart two things are), Axis (one axis of an object's position or rotation), Speed (how fast something moves or turns) and Property (a property or attribute), each with its own icon. They go on any row a generator takes: a property, a root or joint, or one channel of one (a Vector3's Y, a joint's Rot Z: expand the row). Distance, Axis, Speed and Property give a number, so on a whole Vector3 or CFrame they're greyed with why; Copy takes a value of the same type. Enums, text and assets stay keys only.
  • A new driver covers the whole scene (it describes a rule); the keys selected on that row set its span instead. Its source comes from the Explorer selection when something there is in the cast, else you pick it in the viewport (Esc leaves it for the Inspector). Copy and Property then ask for their property in the Inspector.
  • Copy on a joint or root copies either a pose or a placement. Pick a rig part a joint moves (copy the Head onto the RightUpperArm) and it starts on Pose (Transform): the arm turns as the neck turns, from its own place. Placement (CFrame) (what a plain part or a model starts on) puts the joint's part where the source is in the world, worked out like the Copy Transforms constraint, so it never flies off the rig. On a root, a pose copies in the scene origin's space and a placement is the source's world. Blend: Replace blends to it; Add and Multiply compose it (a pose on top of the joint's own pose, a placement as an offset in the world).
  • Copy one axis: on a channel row (Position X / Y / Z, Rotation X / Y / Z of a joint or root, expand the row) or a number property (FieldOfView, Brightness), a Copy of a pose or a placement copies one axis of it: the matching axis by default (Same axis: Rot Z from Rot Z; Position Y on a number property), or any axis you pick (the Head's Rotation Y onto the arm's Rotation Z). A placement reads its axis in World or Local from what Relative to picks, as the Axis driver does. Replace, Add and Multiply work on the number.
  • Copy some axes: a Copy onto a whole joint or root row has Position and Rotation rows of X / Y / Z checkboxes, all on to start. An axis you untick keeps the value under the driver (its keys, or what the stack below gives); the ticked ones come from the source (copy only the Head's Y turn, or only its height). A pose uses the joint's own axes (its Transform's offsets and X, Y, Z angles); a placement uses World, or Local from what Relative to picks, worked out like the Copy Location and Copy Rotation constraints. Replace, Add and Multiply act on the ticked axes only.
  • Blocks sit on the same lanes as generators and constraints, drawn in tangerine with the driver icon, labelled "Distance · r2 > FieldOfView" (what it reads, then what it drives; Copy reads "Copy · Lamp.Brightness"). A lane holding a driver reads Stack. Add shows a "+" chip, Multiply a "×" chip; Replace (the default) shows none. A short bar drops the source, then shortens the name, then keeps only the icon. A muted driver draws faded.
  • States: "Distance · No source" until a source is picked; the failed look (dashed red, a warning) with "r2 missing" or "Lamp.Power missing" when its source is gone (the keys play as if there were no driver); amber dashes and "Loop, ignored" when its source depends on what it drives.
  • Open its lane for the driver's settings rows: In Low, In High, Out Low and Out High can be keyed, and Influence.
  • Right click a driver for Select source (selects what it reads in the Explorer), Mute, Blend (Replace, Add, Multiply), the fade shapes, Save as preset... (keeps its settings, blend and fades as one of your presets and opens it in the Library's Drivers tab to name it), Bake to keys (with the blocks above it; a driver that reads something live asks first), Duplicate and Delete. Drivers drag, trim and fade like every block, and a row with a driver gets Bake stack in its header menu.
  • Adding a driver changes nothing yet: Out Low and Out High both start at the value the row has at the playhead (the graph is flat there), a whole colour's ramp starts as that one colour, and In Low to In High starts at 0 to the source's value now (0 to 10 when it has none yet). Then set Out Low and Out High to what it should drive between. An Add driver starts at 0 and a Multiply at 1 for the same reason. A preset brings its own numbers.
  • Custom drivers are the ModuleScripts tagged AcesMotionDriver, wherever you keep them (somewhere the server and clients can require, such as ReplicatedStorage). They appear in the Add driver menu's Custom group with the driver icon (one that fails to load is greyed with "It errors" and why). Use selected module as a driver tags the ModuleScript selected in the Explorer and adds it on that row; New custom driver... makes a typed, commented template inside the Explorer selection (else ReplicatedStorage), adds it on that row over the whole scene and opens the script. A module returns a table with Drive(context): the value at full strength out (a number for a number row or one channel, else a whole value of the row's type; return context.Current to change nothing). context has Settings, Time and Duration (seconds into the block, its length), Fps, Current (the value under the driver) and Channel, Read(name, seconds?) (a Target setting's world, or a Property setting's value), Distance(from, to?, seconds?) (to left out: from the driven object) and Speed(name, turning?). Optional: Description and Params: Target (a thing you pick, Optional = true when it works without one), Property (an object you pick plus one of its properties or attributes), Number (keyable unless Keyable = false), Integer, Boolean and Enum (Options); Target and Property take a Label. Blend, Influence and fades are applied for you, and while a required Target or Property is unset the block changes nothing (Drive isn't called). Keep Drive pure (no memory between frames) so every player sees the same thing and scrubbing, SetTime and Bake work. Blocks keep the module's id (its AcesMotion_DriverId attribute; an older module's AcesMotion_ActionId still works and is copied over the next time the plugin touches it), so renaming or moving it keeps them working; editing a tagged module reloads it.
  • A missing custom driver: its blocks draw failed ("Spring Follow · Module missing" look, with why) once the modules are loaded, keep their settings and work again when the module is back. Resolve source (first in the block menu) points them at another tagged driver module, the one selected in the Explorer, or a fresh template under the old name; Select source and Bake to keys wait until it's back. With Run custom drivers in Studio off, a custom block reads "Spring Follow · off", changes nothing in Studio, and its tooltip and Inspector say why.

Timeline: Motion Warp ​

  • Motion Warp bends an actor's path so it lands on the real target, wherever that is: a fireball that still hits a unit three times further away, a coin flying into a moving player's backpack, a camera that flies in to a boss wherever it spawned. You animate the trip once against the stand-ins; in a game the same path is re-fitted between where it really starts and where the target really is, so an arc stays an arc at any range.
  • Add it from an actor's or root row's menu, or the root lane's empty space: Add Motion Warp (after Add constraint). It covers the root keys selected on that row, else the next half second. Its target comes from the Explorer selection when something there is in the cast, else you click it in the viewport (Esc leaves it for the Inspector). A spawned actor that appears at a socket starts from that socket. If the span has no root keys yet it keys the two ends (where it starts and the target), so it flies at once. All one undo step.
  • The block sits on the root's Stack lane with constraints and generators, in orchid with the Motion Warp icon, labelled "Motion Warp · Caster/RightHand > Target/UpperTorso" (where it starts, then what it lands on; with no start, the actor's own name: "Motion Warp · Orb > Point"). It moves, trims and fades like every block. Open its lane for its settings rows: Weight, Arc and Arc turn can be keyed.
  • Right click it for Select target (Move point for a Point), Capture line, Bake shape to keys, Mute, the fade shapes, Save as preset..., Bake to keys (the warp's whole result, for the stand-ins, the block removed), Duplicate and Delete.
  • Ease the trip: select a Motion Warp bar (no keys selected) and press E, or right click it > Ease...: the Ease window opens on the warp's trip ("0.30 s to 0.80 s") and its presets and curve set the warp's own ease, one undo step. It re-times the trip (the path stays the same, the speed along it follows the curve), on top of any timing your root keys give, and works with no root keys too. Presets keep it.
  • No root keys yet: a warp whose actor isn't keyed to travel over its span (no root keys, or keyed to stay put) walks the line evenly over the span instead, anything its keys do riding on top, so it flies, a Path shape shows and the arc diamond appears before you key anything.
  • How it reads your keys: when you pick its target (or use Capture line) it remembers the line from where it starts to the target as they stand then. Each frame of your keys keeps its share of the trip and its distance off that line, so the timing and easing you keyed stay. Move the target's stand-in by hand afterwards to see what a game with the target somewhere else plays; Capture line makes where things stand now the line again.
  • Export: a Roblox animation plays it as it is for the stand-ins; the export report lists it among what needs the runtime.
  • On a joint: Add Motion Warp on a rig joint's row (or its lane) bends Joints instead: the chain up from that joint reaches so the joint lands on the target (a claw, a bite, a tentacle), the block sitting on that joint's Stack lane. Changing Bends in the Inspector moves the block between the joint's row and the root's, keeping its timing, targets, settings and keys (one undo step).
  • By speed: a warp timed By speed whose trip plays longer for the stand-ins as they stand draws the extra time as a faded, dashed extension after its bar, labelled "plays 1.2 s here" (the whole trip's play time). Items after it stay drawn at their keyed times; Play in Studio plays the longer trip, as a game does.
  • Repeated actors: a spawned actor set to Repeat (Inspector > Repeat) shows the repeat icon on its row instead of the spawn icon.

Timeline: audio lanes (scene sounds) ​

  • Audio lanes hold the scene's own sounds: music, voice-over, ambience, anything that belongs to no actor. They sit at the bottom of the rows, after the last actor, under an Audio row (speaker, "3 lanes") whose main keys stand for the clips starting there, like an actor row's. Each lane (sound, its name, its volume "80%") has its own Mute, Lock and volume; a muted lane draws at half brightness and is silent.
  • Clips look and work exactly like a sound's Play() keys and bars: the tick at the start, the bar with its waveform, drag to move (Ctrl copies, Alt flips snapping), trim both ends, fade in and out with their shapes, loop, Ctrl stretch for speed, overlapping clips stacking in up to four sub-lanes, audio scrubbing and peak snapping. A bar's label starts after its fade in so the fade line never crosses it. A 3D clip shows a target icon before its name; a clip with no sound yet is a "No sound" placeholder.
  • Selecting: clips select like keys (box select, Shift and Ctrl, the Audio row's main keys, Select all clips in lane); a box over keys and clips takes both, so a retime, nudge, copy, paste or delete moves them together. "1 clip selected on Music".
  • Adding: the + Add menu (Timeline and Hub) has Add audio lane (named in place) and Add selected sound (the Sound or AudioPlayer selected in the Explorer, at the playhead, on the selected lane, else the first, else a new "Audio" lane). Right click a lane's empty space for Add sound here... (an empty clip whose panel opens), Add selected sound here, Paste and Select all clips in lane.
  • From the Explorer, asked each time: Use as template plays that Sound itself with its own volume and effects (it is tagged as an AcesMotion template where it is, so it can live anywhere) or Copy its id takes its id, volume, speed and loop (the Sound isn't needed afterwards). Esc cancels.
  • Menus: the Audio row (Add lane, Mute all audio, Lock all audio); a lane's header (Rename, Mute, Lock, Remove lane). Rename types in place: Enter keeps it, clicking away cancels.
  • Playback: clips play in the preview while the Timeline plays, start partway when playback begins inside them, follow loops and jumps, and stop with playback. 2D clips play flat; a 3D clip plays from its part in the cast (Settings > Audio > Play sounds in 2D while animating flattens it in the editor; games still play it in 3D).

Timeline: layers ​

  • Scene layers sit on top of Base (each actor's own keys), scene-wide, bottom to top. A layer holds keyed rows only: joints, the root and properties. Blocks, sounds, spawns, attach keys and call keys stay on Base, and the actor's generators, constraints, drivers and attachments run on the blended pose.
  • Override blends from the layers below toward the layer's keys by its weight; Additive adds the layer's keys on top as offsets, scaled by its weight (above 1 exaggerates). A layer's Weight is a curve: no keys is 1.
  • One layer at a time: the Timeline shows the active layer, with the same rows as Base. New keys go to it. On a layer a Weight row sits on top, keyed like any property row (its value at the playhead on the right). It stays pinned under the Camera row while the other rows scroll under it, with a shadow once they do. Rows that live on Base (action blocks, generators, constraints, spawn and attach rows) hide on a layer.
  • Posing saves what you see: on an Additive layer a key holds the difference from the layers below; on an Override layer the key that makes the blend land where you posed. Layers above the one you key are taken into account too, on Base and on a layer: the key is what makes the whole stack show your pose (an Override layer at full weight above hides the row, and the status line says the key won't show). At weight 0 a layer can't show anything, so the key holds the pose at full weight and the status line says so. Posing a joint the layer's mask leaves out adds it to the mask in the same undo step ("LeftUpperArm added to Polish's mask").
  • Layer picker (toolbar, after the Scene field): "Base", or the active layer's name and mode on an amber field, so you never key the wrong layer by mistake. Its menu lists the layers topmost first and Base last (a check on the active one, its mode on the right), then Add layer (just above the active one, which it becomes), Add layer for selected (its mask starts at the picked joints, else the selected actors; greyed with "Select an actor or a joint first." when nothing is), and Layers pane.... The status bar's Layer item (amber on a layer) opens the same menu.
  • Show other layers (the toggle after the picker, View > Show other layers, Settings > Timeline; off by default): the other layers' keys show as faint dots behind the active layer's (Base's own keys when a layer is active), and on a layer Base's blocks, spawns and attach spans come back as plain dimmed shapes, no labels or colours, that can't be picked.
  • Merge down folds a layer into the one below (Base for the lowest), Bake to Base writes what every layer shows into Base and removes the layers (muted ones drop out); both sample every frame and thin the keys, so the pose stays the same. One undo step each.
  • Mute takes a layer out; Solo plays Base plus the soloed layers only.
  • Shortcuts (no keys by default; bind them in Settings > Shortcuts or run them from F3): Layer above, Layer below, Add layer, Show other layers.

Timeline: curve editor (Dope sheet, Curves, Split, Channels, Ease) ​

  • Generators in the Channels view: where generator blocks drive a channel, its result (keys plus generators) draws as a faded curve over its keys, so you see the shake on top of the keyed move.

  • The Dope sheet | Curves | Split switch picks the layout. Tab cycles Dope sheet > Ease view > Channels view > Dope sheet; in Split, Tab swaps the lower part between Ease and Channels. The view is remembered per user and per scene.

  • In the dope sheet and Split, the Ease | Channels tabs under the search box open that view under the dope rows (Split) and close it again when clicked while open. In Curves they swap the stage's view.

  • Channels view: the 32 px curve toolbar in a row of its own above the ruler (level with the search, filter and + row; the Ease | Channels tabs sit beside the ruler), a 44 px value axis, grid, scene end, loop wash and marker lines, the curves of the channels under the selected rows (else the channels of the last key selection made in the dope sheet; X red, Y green, Z blue; rotations in degrees), faded curves before the first key and after the last (Settings > Curves > Show infinity), keys, tangent handles on selected keys, and the playhead.

    • Ctrl+drag a key copies the selected keys (the originals stay), as in the dope sheet. Right click empty graph: Frame selected (F), Frame all (A), Select all keys, Clear key selection, Paste, Normalise, Isolate, Before / after (for the selected keys' curves, else every shown curve; the faded curve before the first key and after the last shows the result).

Inspector: property panel ​

  • While the Timeline plays or scrubs, the Inspector's values and key diamonds follow the playhead up to 20 times a second (so it stays light), and show the exact frame as soon as the playhead stops.

  • Scrolled down, every Inspector panel shows a soft shade under its header (or under a pinned generator preview), so it's clear where the settings pass underneath.

  • The properties come first. The Game target, Attached and Stack sections sit at the end of the list, and the panel scrolls to them when you pick a rig or model as a whole (its actor row) or an Attached row. A joint (its row, its keys or a click in the viewport) shows its part's own properties (Color, Size...) from the top. The root (its row, its keys or the root picked in the viewport) shows the model's root part: the part it moves by, else its PrimaryPart, else its HumanoidRootPart (a model with none shows the model). An actor that is a single part shows that part's properties from its actor row and its root too. A property row (or keys of one property) scrolls to that property; anything else (the Explorer, the object list) starts at the top. Closing a block opened from the Stack brings you back to the Stack.

  • Repeat (a spawned actor's preview copy, first in the list): Off plays one copy. Per target plays one copy for each item of a list the game casts on the Over role (by default the role its Motion Warp lands on: Cast = { Targets = { a, b, c } }), each landing on its own item, its actor markers carrying Target (that item) in their payload; an empty list plays none. Preview targets is what Studio previews it on (Remove per row, Add selection adds the Explorer selection; empty: the role's own stand-in), saved with the scene. Count plays that many copies, each copy's landing Point turned by the Spread: Around axis (Axis Up, Right or Forward, and Angle: 360 spreads them all round, less fans them from the keyed one) or Random (within Angle, the same every play for a Seed; the button rerolls it). Stagger (in the ruler's units) starts each copy that much after the one before. In Studio the other copies preview as faded clones landing where they would; clicking one picks the keyed copy, the only one you pose and key. Every change is one undo step.

  • The Inspector tab shows the properties of the picked instance inside an actor. What is picked follows your last selection: a Timeline actor row (the actor's model, or the part itself when the actor is one part), a joint row (its part, or its Bone), the root row (the model's root part, such as HumanoidRootPart), a property row (that property's instance, the row highlighted and scrolled to), a Timeline key selection (the keys' object; a joint shows its part), a Studio selection inside an actor, a click in the viewport (an actor's root, or a joint's part), or the panel's own picker. Nothing picked: "Nothing selected" ("Select a key, block, marker, row or actor to edit it here.").

  • Game target section (an actor's own model picked; not cameras or spawned actors; at the end of the list; folds with its header): Filled in game by, a dropdown with The game's cast (the default: the game passes who plays the role), Local player (each player's own character), Nearest player (the character of the player nearest the scene, picked again as players move), Player camera and Tagged object (an object in Workspace with a CollectionService tag). Tagged object adds Tag (type it, then Enter or click away) and Pick: Nearest (the tagged object nearest the scene) | First (the first one found). Under them: "In Studio it previews from its stand-in. In a game the runtime fills it in." (a Player camera previews from Studio's camera). Each change is one undo step; greyed on a locked actor or a read-only scene. Constraints can aim at these actors like any other; keying one drives the player's own character during the scene.

  • Header: class icon, the instance's name and class chip. Picker (a 24 px field with the path inside the actor): opens a popover tree of the actor's instances with a search (names and class names; parents of a match stay), an animated-property count per instance, class icons; Enter picks the first match, Esc or a click outside closes it.

  • Search properties searches the whole actor: this instance's matches first, then "Elsewhere on {actor}" with a Show arrow that jumps to that instance. The box shows the row count or "N matches"; Esc or x clears it.

  • Animated only (the key button beside the search): lists only the properties with keys, on every instance of the actor (like a search for "animated"), and works together with the search text. The box says "N animated"; with none, Show all properties turns it off. It stays on until you turn it off.

  • Picking and copying property values: a property row lights up while the pointer is over it. Click a row to pick it (it stays highlighted; Ctrl or Shift click adds or removes more). Shift+C copies the value of the row under the pointer, else the picked one; Shift+V pastes onto the row under the pointer, else onto every picked row. Only a value of the same type pastes (an enum onto the same enum). The status bar says what was copied. The row menu's Copy and Paste show the same keys.

  • Add driver and Paste as driver (a property row's right-click menu): Add driver offers Copy, Distance, Axis, Speed and Property on that property, no Timeline row needed. Paste as driver turns the last copied property (Shift+C) into a Copy driver on this one over the whole scene ("Paste as driver (Lamp.Brightness)"), one undo step; greyed with why until something is copied, or when the copied value's type doesn't match ("A Color3 can't drive a Number.").

  • Properties are listed by Studio category, A to Z inside each, as sections that fold (remembered per class). Every Inspector panel remembers the sections you fold: blocks, generators, constraints, switch keys and attach keys keep them closed until you open them again. A Calls section comes first on an object that has methods; a Model's Scale is in Transform; an Attributes section comes last.

  • Rows hide what cannot be animated or keyed: read-only and deprecated properties, BrickColor, Font and PhysicalProperties values, assembly and movement velocities of parts. On a part inside a rig, Position and Rotation are not listed (the joints move it) and an info card says to pose the part in the viewport. An instance with nothing to animate says "Nothing to animate here".

  • Editors by type:

    • Number (ints step by 1): a field that scrubs and types. Vector2, Vector3, UDim, UDim2: one field per part. NumberRange: one field ("2", "1 to 5"; type 0, 1 5 or 1-5); dragging moves both ends; the chevron ("Split into Min and Max") splits it into Min and Max, and the chevron on the split lines ("Join into one field") joins it back.
    • Bool: a toggle. Enum: a menu with the current item ticked. Text: a text box (Enter keeps, Esc cancels).
    • Color3: a swatch and hex (type a hex or R, G, B); click the swatch to open the colour picker window. NumberSequence and ColorSequence: a preview; click to open the sequence or gradient editor window.
    • Asset (textures, images, sounds): a thumbnail, an id field ("Paste 123456 or rbxassetid://123456"; a Decal id is refused with "Not an image: a Decal": paste its image's id), a name line ("used by N keys") and a palette of the track's images then the actor's; click a swatch to swap.
    • CFrame properties: a read-only position (they are posed in the viewport). CFrame attributes: position and rotation fields (degrees).
    • Sequence rows also have a one-value control before the preview: a number box for a NumberSequence (shows the value when flat, "..." otherwise; typing or dragging makes the whole sequence that number) and a colour swatch for a ColorSequence (OK makes the whole gradient that colour).

  • Number fields everywhere scrub: press and drag left/right past 3 px; Shift is 10x finer; Ctrl rounds to whole numbers; Esc puts the value back; releasing commits once. A click without a drag types, and typing arithmetic works on the value shown: +5, *2, /2, +=5, -=3 (a plain -3 is minus three; a Mixed field refuses it). While you drag, the field shows the number as it changes and a left-right arrow in place of its unit; a generator setting updates its bar and the viewport live too.

  • Key diamond (20 px, right of each row): hollow = not animated (click animates: adds the track and keys the current value); bottom half filled = animated, no key on this frame (click keys it); filled = keyed here (click removes this key; the track stays); orange filled = changed, not keyed yet (click keys it). K over a row keys it (hover key, Inspector). Tooltips read "Animate this property", "Key this property", "Remove this key", "Key this change".

  • The edit rule: a property with a track and Auto-key on is keyed at the playhead (one undo step). With a track and Auto-key off it becomes a temporary pose (diamond turns orange; moving the playhead drops it). No track: the instance itself changes for good (one undo step). Editing a tracked property in Studio's own Properties window (or the command bar) records the same way (a key with Auto-key on, a Changed pose with it off); undo, redo and playback are not recorded.

  • Row menu (right click anywhere on the row, fields and swatches included): Key at playhead (K); Remove from timeline; Copy; Paste (same type only; not on a viewport-posed CFrame); Reset to default (the class's default; Model Scale 1); Reset to value before animating; Remove attribute (attribute rows; asks first if it has keys).

  • Calls section: key a method with its diamond. Emit() has a Count field (1 to 50) and a Life line (blank reads "Life Emitter's"; type 0.4 1.2 or 0.6; dragging moves both ends). A sound's Play() has Count and Stop at (where in the sound to stop, in the ruler's units: frames or seconds, like the audio clip's; empty plays to the end). Stop(), Clear() and Pause() show "Runs at each key". A sound with no SoundId shows "{Name} has no SoundId yet. Its keys play once it has one."

  • Attributes: the + on the Attributes header opens the add card (name, type: Number, Bool, Text, Vector2, Vector3, Color3, UDim, UDim2, NumberRange, NumberSequence, ColorSequence, CFrame, Instance; Roblox's name rules are checked as you type; Enter adds, Esc closes). Attributes animate like properties (rows named @Name). An Instance attribute (a reference to any instance in the place) shows that instance's name with its parent in front: click it to select it in the Explorer, Use selection under it takes the one instance selected in the Explorer, right click Clear empties it (the attribute stays). It has no key diamond: a reference can't be animated. A reference to something deleted shows red with the reason.

  • Multi-edit: select keys on several objects, several Timeline rows, or several instances in Studio (up to 500) and the panel shows "ParticleEmitter ×3" (or "Mixed classes"). Values that differ read Mixed (per part in vectors and ranges). Typing sets only the part you changed on every object; dragging moves each object by the same amount; one undo step. The diamond shows a filled and a hollow diamond when only some are keyed here (tooltip "2 of 3 objects have a key here"); a click keys the rest, a second removes them all. Mixed classes list only shared properties ("Showing the 22 properties both share.") or "Nothing in common" with a Pick one button. Locked actors are skipped and named in the status line ("Brute is locked, 2 of 3 objects changed."). The picker lists each object with its actor and picks one.

  • Object list (the Inspector's object dropdown): opens with every branch expanded (Settings > Look > Object list starts expanded, on). Right click the list: Start expanded, Expand all, Collapse all.

  • Group picker (several objects shown): its search box filters by name, actor or class; Up and Down move the highlight, Enter picks the highlighted object, Esc closes.

  • A read-only scene shows "Read-only scene. Update the plugin to edit it."

Editor windows (colour picker, sequence editor, gradient editor) ​

Floating windows opened from the Inspector. Each is one of a pool of 6 per kind (AcesMotion_ColorPicker_1 to _6, the same for the sequence and gradient editors), so Studio remembers each slot's place and size. They never reopen with Studio.

  • Opening: click a Color3 swatch, or a NumberSequence / ColorSequence preview, in the Inspector. One window per property (and per gradient stop): clicking it again leaves the open window as it is. Several windows can be open at once, each with its own OK and Cancel. A 7th of one kind is refused with "Close an editor window first". The title reads "AcesMotion · SlashEmitter.Color".
  • Footer: Undo and Redo icon buttons (the window's own changes), Cancel (puts back the value from before the window opened), OK (keeps it: keyed at the playhead with Auto-key on; one undo step). Enter = OK (unless a text field is being typed in), Esc = Cancel, Studio's close button = Cancel. A window also closes as Cancel when its object is gone, the scene closes or turns read-only, its parent window closes, or the plugin unloads.
  • Live preview: every change shows on the model as it happens (and again after the playhead moves). Opening a window alone does not mark the property changed.
  • Undo inside a window: every settled change (a drag let go, a typed value, a preset, a saved item) is a Studio step "Edit {property}"; Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y and the footer buttons step through them. After OK one step remains (the commit); after Cancel none.
  • Colour picker: a hue ring with the saturation and brightness square inside; Old / New swatches (click Old to go back to the starting colour); a hex field (6 digits, with or without #); R, G, B fields (0 to 255, scrub or type). Typing a value and pressing Enter keeps it and the window stays open; Enter again is OK.
  • Sequence editor (NumberSequence): header chip "4 of 20 points" (narrow: "4 / 20"; the "Range" label hides under 417 px), Presets, Range Min and Max (scrub or type; Max above Min), Fit (sets Min and Max to just hold every point and its envelope, with a little room, rounded to tidy numbers). The canvas shows the grid, the envelope band, the curve and round points (the two ends are squares, pinned at time 0 and 1). Drag a point (the ends move up and down only; Shift snaps time to 0.05). Click the graph to add a point and keep dragging it (a hollow dot shows where; near the line it lands on the line); right click a point or press Del / Backspace to remove it. Select a point to see two envelope bars above and below it: drag them to widen or narrow the random spread. Fields for the selected point: Time (0 to 1), Value, Envelope, Delete. Values are never clamped: typing outside the range grows it. 20 points at most.
  • Gradient editor (ColorSequence): a bar with stops under it. Click the bar to add a stop (the colour already there, up to 20); drag a stop along the bar (the ends stay at 0 and 1); right click or Del removes one (not the ends; 2 at least); double click a stop opens its own colour picker window (titled "..., stop 2"), which previews into the gradient; its OK keeps the colour, its Cancel puts the stop back, and closing the gradient window closes it as Cancel. Fields: Time, the stop's colour, Add stop (halfway to the next), Delete stop.
    • Blend (the header's dropdown): OKLab blends between your stops in even, natural steps, OKLCH turns the hue round the colour wheel keeping colours vivid, RGB is Roblox's plain mix. Roblox draws a gradient straight between its stops, so OKLab and OKLCH add stops in between for you (20 in all) and keep them up to date as you edit; the editor shows and edits only your stops, and the bar shows the whole gradient as games draw it. Reopening a gradient finds its added stops again and picks its blend; a gradient with none opens with Settings > Timeline > Colour blend.
  • Presets: a Presets button in the sequence editor (between the chip and Range) and the gradient editor (right end of the header) opens a scrolling menu of thumbnails (Esc, Up, Down, Home, End, Enter work while open). Sequence presets are laid on the current Range: Basic (Constant, Rise, Fall, Ease in, Ease out, Smooth, Hump, Dip), Particles (Pop in, Grow and fade, Shrink out, Burst, Flicker, Puff, Sparkle), UI (Fade left, Fade right, Fade both edges, Shine band, Soft edge). Constant flattens at the selected point's value; Sparkle rolls new envelopes each time. Gradient presets replace every stop (a stop's open picker closes as Cancel first): Ramps (White to black, Black to white, Red to yellow, Blue to cyan) and Looks (Fire, Ice, Rainbow, Sunset).
  • Saved strip (under the fields in all three windows): colours, gradients and sequences kept in the scene, shared by every window of that kind and every property. + saves the pending value as "Colour 3" (lowest free name; a duplicate pulses and the chip says "Already saved"); click a saved item to apply it (a saved sequence grows the Range to fit); the current item is ringed; right click, or F2 / Del over a swatch, renames (up to 24 characters; a name another swatch has, in any case, turns the field red) or deletes. Capacity: 64 colours, 32 gradients, 32 sequences. Save, rename and delete are one undo step each. The ... button beside + has Export presets... (this kind picked first) and Import presets (preset files, shared with the Library).
  • Window sizes: colour picker 300 x 468 (minimum 260 x 428), sequence editor 420 x 540 (360 x 460; the minimum also grows a size Studio remembered), gradient editor 420 x 350 (340 x 310). A slot Studio already remembers keeps its old size.

Saving, versions, undo, recovery and Team Create ​

  • Every edit is written into the place as you work: each scene is a folder (default ReplicatedStorage.AcesMotionScenes) holding compact binary chunks in attributes, one per actor, so an edit rewrites only what it touched.
  • Versions (the History tab): Save (Scene menu, Alt+Shift+S while an AcesMotion window has focus) keeps the scene as it is now as a version. Versions live in ServerStorage.AcesMotionHistory: saved with the place, shared in Team Create, never sent to players, and each one stores only the chunks that changed. Automatic versions are saved as you work (Settings > Versions). Preview, Compare, Restore and Branch work from the History tab, the Inspector and the Scene menu.
  • Unsaved dot: an accent dot after the scene name (the Timeline's Scene field, the Hub's scene bar, Open scene and the Scenes list) while the scene has changes no version you saved holds. Automatic versions don't clear it; undoing back to the saved state does. Closing a scene with the dot showing never asks: a toast says "Closed with unsaved changes" with Save version.
  • Versions and undo: a version you save is its own undo step ("Save version v4"), so Ctrl+Z takes it back. An automatic version joins the step before it: it costs no extra Ctrl+Z, and undoing that step removes it too (redo brings both back). Restore is one step, and the scene before it is always kept as an automatic version, even with automatic versions off.
  • Every edit is one named Studio undo step (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y, or Edit > Undo): key edits, poses, pins, marker and region edits, imports, window OK. A pose drag that keys is one step. The edit-mode preview rests the stand-ins around every recording so no step carries a pose; after an undo the stand-ins sit at rest for a moment, then re-pose.
  • Undo brings the selection back with the edit: undoing a move selects the same keys (blocks, camera switch keys, attach keys) where they were, undoing a delete selects what was deleted, and redo brings back what was selected when you undid. Changing the selection is not an undo step of its own.
  • Saving the place while a scene is posed saves the posed part positions (Roblox's own Animation Editor has the same catch); joints snap them back in a running game. Closing the scene puts everything back at rest.
  • Team Create: scenes are ordinary attribute changes, so edits to different actors do not clash; a teammate's edit reloads your open scene.
  • Crash recovery: the open scene is mirrored into plugin settings about 2 seconds after each edit, undo, redo or teammate's change (so Restore never brings back edits you undid). Each scene keeps its own copy, so editing another scene after a crash never overwrites the crashed one's. Closing the scene (or Studio) cleanly drops its copy. If Studio closed without that (a crash) and the place was not saved:
    • When the plugin starts, Output says "AcesMotion kept a recovered copy of Fight Scene. Open it from the Hub's Scenes tab.", and the Scenes tab shows a banner above the list ("Fight Scene has a recovered copy from 5 minutes ago (and 1 more).") with Open (opens the newest one's scene) and Discard.
    • Opening the scene shows a Timeline banner "A recovered copy saved 5 minutes ago has changes the place doesn't." with Restore (writes the copy into the scene as one undo step, so Ctrl+Z goes back to the place's copy; greyed with the reason on a read-only scene), Discard (keeps the place's copy and deletes the recovered one) and an X that only hides the banner (the copy stays, and the Scenes tab still offers it).
    • Copies are kept until you restore or discard them, up to 20 (the oldest goes first); with Settings > Storage > Delete old recovery copies on (the default), copies older than 14 days (Keep copies for) are deleted. A copy that already matches its scene is deleted when the plugin starts.
  • View state is per user, not in the place: zoom, scroll, expanded rows, compact rows, view mode, split height, row heights (the 50 most recent scenes are remembered).
  • Version rules: a scene remembers the version that saved it. A scene saved by a newer AcesMotion opens read-only with a banner; an older scene is migrated on load, with a one-time confirm before its first save in the new format ("Save in the new scene format. Older AcesMotion versions can't open it after.").
  • One error never breaks the whole plugin: each distinct error is reported once (a warning with its stack in Output and one line in the status bar), the failing step is skipped (a failing edit changes nothing, a failing drag always lets go) and the rest keeps working.
