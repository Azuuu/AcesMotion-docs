Viewport ​

Posing in the 3D view: the gizmo, Resize, pins, IK, onion skin, motion trails and cameras.

Viewport: the mouse, picking and what is drawn ​

  • While a scene is open AcesMotion holds the viewport mouse (like Studio's Animation Editor), so Studio never selects or drags your rig. Posing never uses Studio's Move or Rotate tools (they rewrite a dragged part's joint). Picking one of Studio's tools hands the mouse back; choosing the Select tool (or no tool), clicking a joint dot, opening a scene, or finishing a viewport pick takes it back.
  • Click a body part picks its joint, or the joint it is welded to through any chain of rigid welds (accessories, held tools, Weld, WeldConstraint or RigidConstraint), else the actor's root. A part welded to an actor but kept outside it (a sword in Workspace welded to the hand) picks that joint too. Welded parts follow their joint as you pose. Pick welded parts (Pose menu and Settings > Posing, on by default) off: parts that only reach a joint through welds are skipped, so the click goes to what's behind them (a shield never blocks the arm); actors without joints (a Sword model, a Cup) keep every part clickable. Invisible parts (Transparency 0.99 or more) are never hit, so the invisible root part does not steal clicks. Click a joint dot picks that joint; the amber dot on each actor is its root. Click a skinned rig's bone anywhere along it; a click on the mesh away from the bones picks the whole rig (the root).
  • Ctrl+click adds or removes a part from the pick. Click empty space clears the pick. When several things overlap under the pointer, a click goes to motion-trail dots and handles first, then a picked camera's FieldOfView dot, then the gizmo's handles, then the trail line, then joint dots, then camera frustums, then body parts.
  • [ picks the joint above the picked one, ] the first joint below. Esc lets go of the pick (and cancels a gizmo drag first, or drops a picked path point).
  • A click on something outside the scene selects it in Studio the way Studio's own click would: its outermost model; Alt+click selects the part itself; Ctrl+click adds or removes; locked parts are skipped; stand-ins are looked through; parts with CanQuery off (invisible effect holders) are found by their boxes. Empty space clears Studio's selection. Studio's hover outline (in Studio's own hover colour and line thickness) is drawn on what a click would select, except over the cast's own parts, gizmo handles, motion trails, during drags and in pin mode.
  • What is drawn: a dot on every joint pivot (grey), an amber dot on each actor's root, bones joining the dots (View > Bones: Diamonds filled like Blender's octahedral bones and see-through, Wire outlines only, Sticks rounded bars), a blue highlight on picked parts and a white one on the hovered part. A skinned rig's bones follow Blender: one bone to the child in line with the joint's +Y, dashed links to other children, and a tip bone at every chain end. A part rig's bones are only drawn; a click near one is meant for the body part under it. Toggles: View > Joint dots, Bones, Part highlights. Colour, thickness, size and transparency of bones and dots: Settings > Gizmos.
  • One look for what you drag or follow: motion trails, constraint and driver guides, Motion Warp paths, camera frustums, attach lines, a skinned rig's bone links, the IK pull line and pin stems are drawn like the posing gizmo's handles: solid strokes that keep their width on screen as you zoom, drawn again faintly on top so the stretch hidden behind or inside a part still shows. Hovering something you can drag turns it the gizmo's hover yellow and thicker. Lines that only point at something (to a target, a source, a muted camera) stay dashed, at the same width.
  • A hint chip beside the pointer and the status line (left of the Timeline status bar) say what the current mode does ("Click a spot to pin it there", "IK: drag an end part", "Tab · Pivot · Corner"...). While dragging, a readout chip shows the axis (X, Y, Z, YZ, XZ, XY, View, Free) in the axis colour, the angle ("35.0°") or distance ("0.50 studs"), and the space.
  • Scene origin: View > Scene origin (or Anchor preview in the Hub) draws the scene origin in the viewport. Place origin and Pick socket show a banner in the Hub ("Click a spot in the viewport for the scene origin. Esc or right click cancels." / "Click a part of an actor for the spawn socket. Esc or right click cancels.") with a Cancel button; click in the viewport to pick. A quick right click cancels any viewport pick too (a right drag still orbits the camera).
  • A picked joint highlights its Timeline row; K with no row selected keys the picked joints.

Viewport: posing gizmo (move and rotate) ​

  • One handle set at the picked joint (the last picked): X / Y / Z rings (front half bright, back half faint) turn about that axis; the outer view ring turns about the view direction; inside the rings the trackball turns freely (another joint's dot inside them still takes the click, so a nearby joint can be picked); X / Y / Z arrows move along the axis; three plane squares (YZ, XZ, XY) move in a plane (each sits in the quarter facing the camera); the centre dot moves in the view plane. Axis colours: X red, Y green, Z blue; the handle under the pointer and while dragging turns yellow. The pointer turns into a rotate cursor over the rings and the trackball and while turning, and an open hand over the other handles. Where handles sit close, the one nearest the pointer takes the click, and each handle's grab band shrinks with a smaller Gizmo size so the rings, the view ring and the trackball stay apart. A handle turning edge-on to the view (an arrow pointing at you, a plane square or an axis ring lying along your line of sight) fades out over its last few degrees instead of popping, and gets harder to grab as it fades; once hidden it can't be grabbed, so an edge-on ring's line across the middle leaves the clicks to the trackball.
  • Drawn like Studio's own tools (a bright copy in the scene and a faint copy on top, so the part inside the rig still shows), at a constant size on screen: 84 px radius at Medium. Size: Settings > Gizmos > Gizmo size (50 to 200%), View > Gizmo > Small / Medium / Large (80, 100, 125%). Arrow length: Short (the default) keeps the move arrows just outside the rings, 286 px across at Medium; Long reaches further, 336 px across (Settings > Gizmos > Arrow length, View > Gizmo > Long arrows).
  • Everything else you click or follow in the viewport is drawn the same way: joint dots, the motion trail's ticks, key dots and tangent knobs, attach target dots, the scene origin and the Resize outlines are solid where you can see them, with a see-through copy where something covers them. A covered dot stays easy to see (70%) so a joint inside the body can still be picked; lines and outlines show at the gizmo's strength. The scene origin's axes and the Resize outlines keep their width on screen as you zoom.
  • A drag moves every picked joint, each about its own pivot. Axis handles turn or move each joint on its own axes in the chosen space; the view ring, trackball and centre dot apply the same change to all. A root drag moves the whole actor.
  • Space (Local, Parent, World): Settings > Gizmos, the toolbar Space field, View > Space, or R to cycle. Studio's Ctrl+L (local/world) flips it too.
  • Snapping: whenever Studio's Rotate or Move increment is on (Model tab), drags step by it like Studio's tools; hold Ctrl to move freely. When Studio's increment is off, drags are free and Ctrl snaps to 15 degrees or 1 stud. The status line says "Ctrl moves freely" or "Ctrl snaps". Constraint guide handles (a Motion Warp's Arc turn diamond, Limit Rotation's arcs and radii, and the other angle and length handles on the guides) snap the same way, to whole multiples (a limit lands on 45 degrees, not 45 from where you grabbed), and Ctrl flips it.
  • Rotate ruler: while you turn a ring (X, Y, Z, the outer view ring or a Track To's Roll ring), ticks show round it from where the drag started, one per snap step (at least 5 degrees apart; with free rotation only every 45 degrees), longer at every 45 degrees and longest at every 90, like Studio's own rotate tool. Ticks on the ring's far side are faint. The angle turned so far is filled (a translucent pie from where the drag started), a bold pointer shows where it points now, and the readout goes back to 0 after a whole turn (turning 370 degrees reads 10).
  • Keys during a drag: type a number on an arrow or a ring for an exact amount (degrees round a ring, studs along an arrow): it takes over from the mouse at once, - flips its sign, Backspace edits it (empty gives the mouse back), Enter keeps the drag. X, Y or Z puts the drag on that axis from where it began (a move along it, a rotation round it, in the current Space); the same key again goes back to the handle you grabbed; on a square, the centre dot or the trackball, lock an axis first, then type. Hold Shift for a fine drag: each bit of mouse movement turns or moves it a tenth as far (going round a ring still turns it the same way), with snapping off while it's held; let go and it carries on at full speed from there. The status line lists these while you drag.
  • Esc or right click cancels the drag and puts the pose back. On release, with Auto-key on the moved tracks are keyed (IK chains and pinned limbs too; one undo step); with it off the pose stays as a temporary pose (no key). Moving the playhead drops an unkeyed pose; K keys it. Poses are part of undo.
  • View > Gizmo turns handle groups on or off: Rotate rings, Move arrows, Plane handles. Separate move and rotate shows only the rotate handles or only the move handles (arrows move in to the centre); T switches the tool.
  • Keys written by a pose are named after the action in Edit > Undo ("Undo Move", "Undo Rotate").
  • Posing something a constraint drives edits its keys underneath, and the viewport shows the result: dolly a tracking camera and it keeps aiming. What the constraints take over at full strength greys out on the gizmo (the rings under a Track To, the arrows under a Copy Location), with a chip at the top of the viewport ("Rotation driven by Track To"). A Track To adds a blue Roll ring around its aim: drag it to tilt the camera while it tracks (one undo step; keyed at the playhead when Roll has keys).
  • Constraint guides: select a constraint block on the Timeline (several at once show each one's) and its guide draws in the viewport, wherever the playhead is: a dashed line to a ring on its target (in the target's colour) for the Track, Copy and Delay ones; aim and up ticks labelled with their axes ("Aim -Z", "Up +Y") on the Track family and Face Motion; the box a Limit Location keeps it in (its edges behind it dashed, the dots on its far faces dimmer); a filled wedge per axis a Limit Rotation limits, with its range ("Z -45 to 45"); the sphere around a Limit Distance's target; a patch of a Floor's plane with its offset; and, for a distance fade, the Full within ring (filled) and the None beyond ring (dashed) with their values, plus a line to the subject showing how far it is and how strong the constraint is there ("8.9 studs 61%"). The status line names the pick and what its guide does ("Crate picked | Limit Distance: drag the square to set the distance"). Hovering or dragging a handle rings it in yellow. View > Show all constraint guides (or Settings > Posing) also draws every constraint under the playhead. While a selected constraint shows, the move, rotate and resize gizmo hides so only its handles take drags; clicking a part or empty space in the viewport, or Esc, lets the constraint go and brings the gizmo back.
  • Driver guides: select a driver block on the Timeline (or turn on Show all constraint guides for every one under the playhead) and the viewport shows what it reads, in the driver colour: a dot on what it drives, a dashed line to its source with a ringed dot on it, and a chip with the live numbers ("r2 · 23.4 studs > FieldOfView 52"). A Distance with a From draws its line between the two things it measures. An Axis adds an arrow along the axis it reads with its name ("Y") and, for a world position, a dotted bracket from where the axis reads 0 up to the dot, with a tick ("r2 · Position Y 1.6 studs > Rot Z 12°"). A Speed adds an arrow along the way its source is moving. Property and Copy draw the line to their object ("Hero.Power 0.75 > Brightness 1.8", "Copy · Lamp.Brightness > Brightness"); a custom driver a line to each thing it reads. The status line says what the guide means ("Cam picked | Distance: the dashed line is the measure it reads"). Driver guides have no handles: edit in the Inspector. While one shows, the gizmo hides as for constraints.
  • Motion Warp guide: select a Motion Warp on the Timeline and the viewport shows a dashed line from where it starts to its target, a ring on the target, and the path it plays now as a solid orchid tube drawn like the gizmo's handles (faint where something covers it). A white diamond sits at the curve's highest point: click it for its curve gizmo, drawn like the posing gizmo in orchid. Drag its arrow (pointing the way the curve bends off the line) for Arc: out for a taller curve, in for a flatter one, stopping at the line (the chip reads "Arc 1.4 · Turn 15°"). Arc steps by 0.1 while Studio's Move increment is on and moves freely while it's off; Ctrl flips it, as for every move. A thin orchid orbit track appears too: a circle round the line through the curve's top, every place the top can swing to. Drag the diamond along it for Arc turn: the curve's top follows the pointer round the line (the chip reads "Arc turn 95° (+40)", a faint dot marks where it started). Each drag is one undo step and the gizmo stays for the next; hover turns a handle yellow; Ctrl flips snapping as on the posing gizmo; Esc or a click elsewhere lets it go. With Bends Joints the guide follows the reaching joint's path. A straight path has nothing to bend, so no diamond. While you drag that actor's keys or pose it, the path follows with fewer points (a little less smooth) and is drawn exactly again a moment after you stop. An actor that rides something (an Attach key) has its path drawn where it really flies, riding it, and its By speed trip measured from there too.
  • Drag a guide's handle to change its setting live: a Limit Distance's radius (the white square), a Limit Location face, a Limit Rotation arc's ends, a Floor's height, a distance fade's Full within and None beyond rings. Hover a handle and it lights up, with a card saying what it does after the tooltip delay. While dragging, a faint ghost shows where it started and the readout chip shows the value and the change ("Distance 6.5 studs (+2.5)"); one undo step on release, keyed at the playhead when the setting has keys. Esc or right click cancels.
  • Point targets draw a small cross with the point's X Y Z beside it and a dashed line from the subject: click it (or the Inspector's Move in viewport, or the block menu's Move point) and the gizmo's move arrows move the point (one undo step per drag, keyed when it has keys). Esc or a click anywhere else ends it.

Viewport: Resize tool ​

  • Turn it on with the toolbar Resize button, View > Gizmo > Resize handles, or T. It works on Part and Model actors (a rig shows no handles and the status bar says "Resize works on parts and models.").
  • Handles: a coloured shaft with a ball standing off each of the six faces (X red, Y green, Z blue; yellow on hover and while dragging), one white corner cube at the corner nearest the pointer on the side facing the camera (it follows the pointer around that side, hidden under 24 px on screen or with the camera inside), and a blue outline around the part. Settings > Posing > Corner handles (or View > Gizmo > Corner handles) hides the corners.
  • Drag a face handle: that side moves and the opposite side stays; sizes step in Studio's Move increment and never go below 0.05. Drag a corner: the two sides you see change, the opposite corner stays (a ring marks it) and a faint box shows the start size.
  • Ctrl resizes both sides (about the centre). Alt keeps the proportions. For a Model actor every handle keeps proportions, Ctrl does nothing, and Scale is keyed.
  • The readout reads "Size 4 × 2.5 × 2 (+0.5)", or "(+25%)" for a Model or a Group.
  • Several actors: Each grows every picked actor's side facing the handle's way in the world by the same amount (a turned crate does not make an L); Group scales them together as one block, gaps included (always in proportion; Ctrl scales about the block's centre). Set it with the toolbar Resize: Each field, the Resize options chevron, or Settings > Posing > Several actors. With several picked, the handles sit on one blue box around them all and each actor gets a thin outline. While dragging, a dashed grey box shows where it started.
  • On release the change keys Size (parts) or Scale (models) plus the root, frame 0 included, when Auto-key is on; otherwise it is a temporary pose. Esc cancels; one undo step ("Undo Resize"). Space and Pivot are disabled while Resize is the tool.
  • Onion ghosts of a resized actor show the size keyed at their own time.
  • Cameras cannot be resized: while any selected or picked actor is a camera, the Resize button (and View > Gizmo > Resize handles) is greyed with "Cameras can't be resized. Drag the blue dot to change FieldOfView.", T skips Resize, and picking a camera while Resize is the tool goes back to the move or rotate tool used before it (Move if none was).

Viewport: camera actors ​

  • No gizmo on the camera you look through: while the view is locked on a camera (look-through on, not looking away), that camera's gizmo and handles are hidden, like its frustum, so nothing sits in the eye. Look away (move the view while paused) or leave look-through to pose it again.

  • Frustums: every camera actor draws a wire frustum in its actor tint: a small body box behind the lens, four edges to a far rectangle, and a filled up triangle on the far rectangle's top edge so roll reads. The far rectangle sits at the Frustum length (Settings, 1 to 20 studs, default 4), sized by the camera's live FieldOfView and the viewport's aspect. It follows the camera while scrubbing and playing.

  • States: Idle (tint edges), Hover (thicker edges in the gizmo's hover yellow), Selected (thicker accent edges and fills), Live (the camera the Camera track picks at the playhead: thicker and more filled), Muted actor (dashed, 40%). Selected wins over Hover, Hover over Live. The edges are solid strokes like the gizmo's, faint where a part covers them.

  • Show frustums (All cameras, Selected only, Off). The camera the view is locked on while looking through it draws no frustum; while looking away every frustum draws.

  • Name chip: the role and a camera icon in the actor's tint, over the camera body, on hover, when picked and while live (with the Live pill beside it while live). It moves up while the gizmo is on that camera, so it clears the rings. A muted camera shows it only when picked.

  • Picking a camera: click one of its edges (within 6 px), its body or its name chip. The inside of the far rectangle does not pick, so a camera never blocks clicking what is behind it. Ctrl+click adds or removes it, as for any actor. The posing gizmo then moves and rotates the camera's root like any actor's root; every drag is one undo step, keyed with Auto-key.

  • FieldOfView handle: one picked camera shows a blue dot (the Resize tool's Z face ball) at the far rectangle's centre, with a dashed look axis from the lens. It shows with every tool; cameras cannot be resized (see Viewport: Resize tool). Hover reads "FieldOfView 35°. Drag to change." Drag it along the look axis: pull out for a longer lens (narrower view), push in for a wider one; steps of 0.5°, Shift 0.1°, Ctrl whole degrees; clamped to 1 to 120. A readout "FOV 24° was 35°" follows the pointer and the old frustum stays at 35% as a ghost. Esc or right click cancels. Release is one undo step "Set FieldOfView of CloseUp", by the property rule: keyed at the playhead with Auto-key on (a FieldOfView track), a temporary pose with it off, or the camera's own value when it has no FieldOfView track. Hidden with several cameras picked (use the Inspector).

  • Rotation keys keep the horizon level: a new camera root track turns in YXZ order (yaw, then pitch, then roll), so turning a camera between two keys adds no roll. (Tracks made before this used XYZ and roll between keys that change both yaw and pitch: right click the camera's root row > Rotation > Euler YXZ converts one.)

  • Cameras measure from the scene origin: a camera's keys always count from the scene origin, also in a scene whose actors measure from their own root parts (a camera has no root part); games always played them that way.

  • Cameras turn like a camera head (pan, tilt, roll): rotating a camera with the gizmo pans and tilts it without rolling it, whichever ring or space you use; only a turn about the camera's own look axis (the Z ring in Local space, the roll ring) rolls it. Its keys always store the tilt between -90 and 90 degrees, with pan and roll counting on through whole turns, so a pan or a full orbit plays smoothly between keys and never tumbles or flips. (On a YXZ camera track; older XYZ camera tracks convert with right click > Rotation > Euler YXZ.)

  • Focus on the pointer (F): with a camera picked and the Move or Rotate gizmo on it, or a camera actor selected in the Timeline or Hub, point at anything in the viewport and press F: the camera turns to look at the first visible surface under the pointer. It keeps its position and its roll (only yaw and pitch change, in YXZ order); Reset roll when focusing (Settings > Camera and the camera menu, off by default) levels it instead. It is a posing action: one undo step "Focus CloseUp", keyed at the playhead with Auto-key on, else a temporary pose (K keys it; moving the playhead drops it). Camera frustums, onion ghosts and invisible parts are looked through. Refused with a status line reason when nothing is under the pointer, the camera or its root track is locked, or the scene is read-only. With no camera picked F does nothing (Studio's own F zooms as usual). While F acts, Studio's view is held still for half a second, so Studio's own F (Zoom to) cannot also move it. Not while looking through that camera (its gizmo is hidden), except while piloting it: then F turns the piloted camera. Ignored while a mouse button is held; rebindable in Settings > Shortcuts.

  • Focus and level (Ctrl+F): the same, and the roll goes to 0 whatever the "Reset roll when focusing" setting says. While looking through a camera, both keys work only when Pilot camera is on (on the piloted camera); attached without Pilot they do nothing. Rebindable in Settings > Shortcuts (Focus camera and level it); Reset this group on the Camera page puts both back.

  • Status line: "CloseUp picked | move and rotate it like any actor | drag the blue dot to change FieldOfView"; while dragging, "Dragging FieldOfView of CloseUp | Ctrl whole degrees, Shift finer | Esc cancels".

Viewport: pins ​

  • Hold P and click a part to pin it at that spot (a point pin: only that spot is held, the pin marker sits on it). Click a joint dot to pin the whole part. P-click a pinned part to unpin it. Tap P (under 0.35 s, no click), the toolbar Pin button, or Pins menu > "Pin or unpin picked parts" pins the picked parts, or unpins them when all are pinned already.
  • Turn pins into locks (Pins menu, or the viewport's right click on empty space) keeps a pinned pose holding while it plays: each pin becomes a Lock from the playhead to its row's next key, then the pins go.
  • Shift+P (or Pins menu > Clear all pins, or a joint row's pin icon) removes pins. A root cannot be pinned. Pins live in the session (cleared when another scene opens); each pin action is one undo step.
  • A pinned part keeps its place while you pose: the limb above it bends (or aims) to reach it, and a faint line shows any gap it cannot close. A pinned end of a solved limb keeps its world rotation.
  • Anchored pins: with parts picked, hold P and click a floor or prop (or Alt+P-click a body part) to anchor their pins to it. They ride with it: move the prop with Studio's tools or a script and the pinned limb follows (one undo step "Follow anchored pins", keyed with Auto-key); drag a prop actor with the gizmo and the limb follows in the same drag. A pin never anchors to its own body. Scrubbing does not re-solve other frames.
  • Markers: a badge with the pin icon on a stem above a ring (a link icon for an anchored pin); while P is held a ghost marker follows the pointer (red with an x over a pinned part). The toolbar badge counts pins; the status line counts pins and anchored pins.

Viewport: IK and FK ​

  • IK is on when the IK default (toolbar FK | IK switch, Settings > Posing > IK as default posing mode) differs from whether I is held. FK default: hold I to drag with IK. IK default: hold I to drag with FK. The hint chip says "IK: drag an end part" / "FK: drag to rotate one joint".
  • In IK, press a non-root part and drag: it is picked and a view-plane drag bends its chain toward the pointer (a click without a drag just picks it). A soft tube shows the chain and a dashed line the pull.
  • Chain length (Auto: up to the nearest pin, a branch point or 3 joints; 1 to 8 goes on through branch points, never past a pin, and never takes the rig's root joint, so an R6 arm or leg turns on its own at any length instead of swinging the body): toolbar Chain field and menu, Ctrl+[ (shorter), Ctrl+] (longer), Settings > Posing > Default IK chain length. It can change mid-drag.
  • A joint that hangs from the root part (an R15 LowerTorso, an R6 Torso) just moves in IK. A one-joint chain (an R6 limb) can only turn: it aims its part's far end at the pointer.
  • Limbs bend like real elbows and knees: the rig's own HingeConstraint or BallSocketConstraint (cone 30 degrees or less plus twist limits) between a joint's two parts is its limit, so the middle joint bends only about that axis, to the side and within the range its twist limits allow (widened to include the rest pose). Without a constraint it keeps the current bend, or bends like a knee/elbow by name. At a limit the end stops short.
  • Swing: in IK, dragging an elbow or knee swings the limb about its upper-to-end line toward the pointer; the hand or foot stays put and keeps its rotation.
  • Only elbows and knees are limited today; other joints are not clamped. "Pole target" is not available.

Viewport: temporary pivot ​

  • Hold Tab over a part (with a joint picked) to turn about a point other than the joint. The pivot drops at the pointer and locks there while held: a joint dot under the pointer ("Joint"), else the first visible surface under the pointer (any part, the floor included) snapped to the nearest corner, edge midpoint, face centre or centre of that part ("Corner", "Edge", "Face", "Centre"), or the exact surface point with Snap pivot off (a fainter marker and no snap dots). The chip reads "Tab · Pivot · Corner"; the Pivot field reads "Pivot: Custom"; the status line says "Turning about the pivot. Release Tab to go back."
  • While held the gizmo sits on the pivot and every picked target turns about it: FK turns the joint about the point so the joint slides; IK turns the part about the point and bends the chain to keep it joined; a root turns the whole actor; move handles just sit there.
  • Release Tab and the gizmo goes back to the joint. Releasing mid-drag finishes that drag about the pivot, then it goes back. The pivot rides with its part, is not saved and is not its own undo step.
  • Snap pivot: Pivot field menu, View > Gizmo > Snap pivot, Settings > Posing. The key is rebindable (Settings > Posing > Temporary pivot key). If Studio or another plugin owns the key on your machine, rebind it.

Viewport: right-click menu ​

  • Right click in the viewport (a click, not a drag: a right drag still turns the camera) opens AcesMotion's menu at the pointer. On a part it picks the part first, as a left click would, then offers: Key (K), Delete keys here (the picked joints' keys on the playhead), Select keys (at the playhead, or all of its keys), Reset to rest, Pick the joint above ([) / Pick the joint below (]), Copy pose, Paste pose, Paste mirrored, Flip pose, Select mirror, Pin (Unpin when every picked part is pinned), Attach to..., Detach, Back to rig and Show in Inspector. On empty space: Paste pose, Clear pins and Let go of the picked joints. A greyed item's click says why in the viewport's status line ("No key on the playhead for the picked joints."). Esc, arrows and Enter work as in the Timeline's menus.

Viewport: tween slider ​

  • Makes in-between poses: with the playhead between two keys, each picked joint (with none picked, every keyed joint of the selected actors; the root only when it's picked) blends between its own previous and next key, and letting go writes Breakdown keys at the playhead on them, one undo step. A joint with no key on one side is left alone.
  • Three modes: Tween (0% is the previous key's pose, 100% the next key's, from -50% to 150% for overshoot and anticipation; it rests at 50%), Blend to neighbour (from the pose now toward the previous key to the left or the next key to the right, -150% to 150%) and Push / pull (away from the in-between the two keys make to the right, toward it to the left, -150% to 150%).
  • In the Timeline toolbar: the Tween group after Pins and mirror: the mode dropdown and a slider that rests at the mode's middle. Drag it: the pose follows live and a bubble reads the value; let go to key it and the slider springs back. Ctrl snaps to 10%, Esc cancels. It's the third group to go into More when the toolbar is narrow (More > Tween mode).
  • In the viewport: press Shift+T (no need to hold it), then move the mouse left or right: a chip by the pointer reads "Tween 70%" and the two keys ("f10 to f20"). Click or Enter keeps it, Esc or a right click cancels, Shift is finer, Ctrl snaps to 10%, typed digits set the value ("-" flips it, Backspace edits), and Tab changes the mode.
  • The Pose menu > Tween slider picks the mode and says how to start it.

Viewport: mirror posing and pose tools ​

  • Mirror posing (toolbar Mirror button, Shift+M, Pose menu): every joint a drag writes also writes its mirror partner, mirrored (unless you are posing both sides); pins solve after it. The partner of the dragged, hovered or last picked part gets a ring and a "Mirror" chip, and the status line reads "Mirror posing | {partner} follows {joint}, mirrored | {key} turns it off".
  • Partners come from the mirror rules (Settings > Posing): the scene's rules first, then yours. The mirror plane is per scene (X by default; Mirror options menu).
  • Pose tools (the Pose menu; keys work with the viewport focused) act on the picked joints, else the whole selected rig; the root is never copied. A paste or reset is an unkeyed pose (keyed when Auto-key is on) and one undo step: Copy pose (Shift+C), Paste pose (Shift+V, onto the same parts or just the picked ones), Paste mirrored (Shift+N, onto each part's partner, mirrored), Flip pose (Shift+F, also a toolbar button: the pose mirrored onto the other side in place, centre joints onto themselves), Select mirror (menu only: picks each picked part's partner instead), Reset to rest (Shift+R).
  • Over the Timeline the same keys (Shift+C, Shift+V, Shift+N) are the key commands instead.

Viewport: attaching (Attach to, Detach) ​

  • Attach to... (Pose menu, Shift+G, also the actor row menus): pick a part, an actor or a rig joint first or a skinned rig's bone (its child bones follow it) (several at once work), then click what it should follow: another actor, or any part, attachment or bone inside one, including the same rig's own parts (move a sword from the right hand to the left hand). An Explorer selection works as the click too. An Attach key goes in at the playhead, one undo step; the status line says "Sword follows Attacker · LeftHand from f20". Nothing jumps: it follows from where it is at that frame. While you pick, the gizmo hides, the part under the pointer lights up in its actor's colour with a dashed line from the object to it, and the hint names it ("Sword follows r2 · RightHand. Click to attach, Esc or right click cancels."); a part it can't follow shows red with the reason.
  • The grip is saved when you attach: from then on the target carries the object on every frame, the attach frame included, so re-posing or re-keying the hand moves the sword with it. Dragging an Attach key to another frame keeps the grip (the object may jump into the hand there); changing the key's target takes the grip again.
  • What it acts on: what you selected in the Explorer inside the cast comes first (a part: its joint, or its actor; a bone; a stand-in), then the picked joints, then the selected actors. Every click in the viewport clears stand-in parts from the Explorer selection, so the Explorer only counts when it's your latest choice.
  • Moves by (swappable designs): select a part of a Model actor in the Explorer (an invisible HolderPart the other parts are welded to, say) and press Attach to...: the actor now moves by that part ("Sword now moves by HolderPart."). Its keys don't change and nothing moves, but any model that plays the role in a game (or as its stand-in) is placed so its own part of the same name lands where this one's would: animate one basic sword, then swap in other designs with a HolderPart in the same place in their hierarchy. A model without that part moves by its pivot (with a warning in the output). Change it or go back to the pivot in the Inspector's Attached group. A single part actor always moves by its own pivot.
  • Detach (Pose menu, Shift+Alt+G): a Detach key at the playhead for each picked object attached there, or each picked rig joint sitting on its rig (select an R6 arm and Detach: the torso no longer moves it, its Motor6D stays, and Back to rig or Back to rig where it is brings it back). It stays where it was in the scene (a dropped sword stays on the floor while the rig walks away). The spot is saved when the key is made, so posing or keying the hand on the Detach frame (or anywhere after) no longer moves it. Greyed with the reason when nothing picked is attached, or on its rig, at the playhead (an actor that follows nothing has nothing to let go of).
  • Hand-offs: an Attach key while already attached moves it to the new target from where it is, without a jump.
  • Back to rig (rig joints and bones only: a body part, a prop on a Motor6D in the hand, a bone): ends an attach or a detach. Back to rig (Pose menu, Shift+Alt+A) snaps it back into its place on the rig, at once or eased over the key's Blend (a thrown sword flying back into the hand). Back to rig where it is (Pose menu, no default key) rejoins its rig keeping its place in the world and moves with the rig from there. The key's Reattach switch in the Inspector flips between them. A temporary detach is Detach, then Back to rig: while detached the joint keeps its Motor6D or bone but the rig moving no longer moves it. An actor (a part or model, like a Cup or a Sword model) has no rig to go back to: it only attaches and detaches, and Detach leaves it floating where it was.
  • Its own keys still apply while attached, riding with the target: pose it (gizmo, IK, pins) and key it as usual and it stays where you put it, relative to what it follows.
  • Something can't follow itself or anything it carries (the status line says so). A target that is gone (role not cast, part deleted) holds it where it was at the key. Two objects following each other in a loop: that attachment is ignored.
  • IK through held objects: with IK on (the FK | IK switch, or hold I), dragging something a hand holds moves the arm instead: a sword on a Motor6D in the hand, or anything attached with an Attach key to a rig's part or bone (a prop, another rig's joint). The object stays rigid in the hand; the arm bends (Chain field, pins and mirror posing as when you drag the hand) and only the arm is keyed. Move handles and view drags move it, rotate handles turn it about its own pivot (or Tab's temporary pivot). An R6 arm (one joint) turns at the shoulder: a move swings the grabbed point toward the pointer, a rotate gives the sword the exact angle. Past the arm's reach the sword stops short ("Out of reach: Sword stops short."). Several held objects picked: each moves its own arm.
  • FK adjusts the grip: in FK (or hold I to flip) the same drag moves the object within the hand, keyed relative to it.
  • A held object whose hand is pinned doesn't move ("RightHand is pinned. Unpin it, or use FK..."). One held by something that can't bend (an actor's root, a prop, a part no joint moves) moves within it, and the status says so.
  • Which joints are held props: on rigs with a Humanoid, any joint whose part isn't a body part (Roblox's own check, R15 and R6) counts automatically, except what hangs straight from the torso, which is always the body (a custom R6 rig's LeftArm or RightLeg, named without the space, stays a limb); the Inspector's Held object switch on a joint sets it by hand (custom and skinned rigs, or to override). A part held by a Weld already picks its hand, so dragging it moves the arm.
  • The IK chain shows on the arm while you hover or drag the held object, and the hint reads "IK · RightHand, holding Sword". Held objects move their holder (on by default) in the IK menu and Settings > Posing turns it off: then IK drags move the object within its hand.
  • Attach lines: a dashed line in the target actor's colour from each attached object to the point it follows, with a dot on the target. View > Show attach lines (also Settings > Posing) shows them for everything attached; off, only the picked ones show theirs.
  • Roblox animations: an attached joint of the exported rig is baked into the animation where the scene shows it, bones included, and so are its detaches and returns (a prop needs a Motor6D to its hand for that; a Weld can't be animated). A target on another actor bakes from where that actor stands in the scene, and the export notes say so. Attached props (part or model actors) play through the AcesMotion runtime.

Viewport: onion skin ​

  • Ghosts are see-through tinted copies of the selected actors: red before the playhead, blue after (colours and falloffs in Settings > Onion). Each further ghost is fainter by the side's falloff; a lone ghost fades too. They hide during playback and come back on stop.
  • With 2 or more keys selected in the Timeline, a path is drawn from the first selected key to the last and a ghost stands on every selected key. With fewer, ghosts sit at the nearest keys around the playhead (Ghosts at keys, "Before" and "After" counts) or every N frames (Ghosts every N frames, Step), for the selected actors only unless that is off.
  • Limbs only shows just the parts carried by the joints with selected keys (else the picked ones) and everything below them; a root or nothing picked shows the whole body (skinned meshes cannot be split).
  • Past ghosts / Future ghosts can be turned off separately. Ctrl+Shift+O toggles onion skin.

Viewport: motion trails (trajectory) ​

  • While you pose, the stretch of the trail near the playhead follows every frame and the rest of it catches up a few times a second; letting go redraws it exactly. Posing stays smooth with long or sparsely keyed scenes and in IK.

  • With 2 or more keys selected, one path is drawn through them with a tick on every frame and a dot on every selected key (a combined path through the average of several parts when keys are on several parts). With fewer, the last picked part's path over Settings > Trajectory > Range (the Timeline's visible range or the loop region). A root traces its pivot, a part its centre. Up to 600 samples across all paths.

  • Edit with Gizmo (default; Trajectory menu > Edit with): click a key dot to pick it (the playhead goes to its frame, the part is picked) and the pose gizmo there edits it, keyed. Click a tangent handle or the line (it takes a click within 7 px) to pick it and move it with move handles only; a point on the line also shows rings: turning a ring bends the pose in between keys with no key (one undo step on release). Esc drops the pick. Edit with Drag drags dots, handles and the line directly. The picked part's own key dot at the playhead sits under the gizmo's centre: a press there drags the gizmo, so a root on a key frame still moves from its centre. Clicking a key dot also selects that part's keys on that frame, so Del, E and the Inspector act on them; Shift+click a key dot deletes them (one undo step).

  • The picked key dot (the active key) shows two diamond tangent handles, each on its own line from the dot. Dragging a handle reshapes how the path leaves or arrives; both stay smooth unless Alt is held (then one side moves alone).

  • The path is a solid tube in the accent colour, faint where a part covers it. Hovering the line turns that path yellow and thicker, with a marker where it would be grabbed.

  • Drag the line to bend it through the pointer with no new key; double click the line adds a key there (every part of a combined path gets a key).

  • Timing mode (toolbar Timing button, or hold J): dots turn hollow rings; drag a dot along the path to move its key earlier or later (whole frames, never past the neighbouring dots; a chip reads "f 42 · 6 earlier"). Selected keys on that frame move together.

  • Line thickness and Point size scale the path, ticks, dots, handles and their hit reach (Trajectory menu, Settings > Trajectory). Key dots, Frame ticks and the whole trajectory can be turned off. Around the playhead shows the path a set reach either side of the playhead and follows it as you scrub, so a long scene's trail stays readable.

  • A limb keyed only on rotation cannot stretch: its path stays on a sphere round its joint.

Viewport: looking through a camera ​

  • Look-through (Q in the viewport or the Timeline, the toolbar camera button, the camera menu, View > Camera, the "..." menu when Display is in it) shows the scene through a camera actor: Studio's camera (its CFrame, field of view, camera type and focus) is saved, and the camera actor's posed CFrame and FieldOfView are written to the view every frame, so scrubbing and playing film through it. Needs a camera actor that isn't muted.
  • Which camera: with Follow camera track on (the default, Settings > Camera) the camera the Camera track picks at the playhead; cuts at switch keys are instant, and a key's Blend glides between the two cameras as in a game. With Follow off, the chosen camera: picked in the camera menu (which turns Follow off), set by an Inspector Look through button, or the camera actor you last selected while looking through.
  • A chip at the top of the viewport says what is going on, with the camera icon in its key cap: "Looking through CloseUp, Q exits" (with a thin accent frame round the viewport), "Looking away from CloseUp, Scrub or play to return", or for 3 seconds "Look-through ended, You moved the view. Q looks through again".
  • No camera here (gaps): where the Camera track has no live camera (before the first switch key, or after a shot's Until until the next key) look-through stays on but the viewport shows your own view: the view saved when you started looking through, then wherever you left it in the last gap. Nothing is written there and you can move the view freely (it never counts as a view move). The chip reads "No camera here, The next shot starts at f30" (or "No more shots" after the last one) and the accent frame hides. When the playhead enters a shot the view locks onto the camera (your view is remembered first). Leaving look-through in a gap keeps the view. With Follow off the chosen camera always shows (no gaps).
  • A chosen camera lets go outside its shots (Follow camera track off, a camera picked in the camera menu or with the Inspector's Look through): when that camera has shots on the Camera track and the playhead is outside all of them, the view lets go as in a gap (your own view, "No camera here"); back inside one of its shots it locks on again. A camera with no shot of its own is looked through everywhere (to frame a new camera).
  • Stay on a camera between shots (Studio only; Settings > Camera, the camera menu and View > Camera; off by default): look-through never lets go. Where no shot is live it keeps showing the last shot's camera (before the first switch: the first shot's camera), and a chosen camera stays on outside its shots. Games always follow the Camera track.
  • When you move the view (camera menu submenu, Settings > Camera): Look away until scrubbed or played (default): while paused, moving the view (right drag, wheel, fly keys, focus, another plugin) lets go of the camera; look-through stays on (the button stays lit, the frame goes) and scrubbing, playing, stepping a frame or jumping to a key snaps the view straight back. While playing, view moves are ignored. Exit look-through: the first move leaves look-through and keeps the view where you put it. Stay on the camera: moves are ignored.
  • Leaving: Q (from either state), the button, the menu item, closing the scene or opening another, hiding the Timeline. Leaving while locked on the camera puts Studio's view back exactly as it was; leaving while looking away keeps the view you moved to. If the last camera actor is deleted, look-through ends.
  • Studio flies its own view down while Q is held in the viewport, so while the look-through key is held (up to 1 second) the view it left is held still and nothing counts as a view move: leaving with Q lands exactly on your saved view.
  • Align camera to view (Shift+Q in the viewport or the Timeline, the camera menu, the Inspector's Align to view): moves the selected camera actor (else the live one) to your view and keys its root and FieldOfView at the playhead, one undo step. Refused while the view is locked on the camera; the reason shows in the Timeline status bar. In a gap with no camera selected it says "No camera is live here. Select a camera actor first."
  • Camera writes never enter undo; undo and redo keep working while looking through.
  • Pilot camera (Ctrl+Shift+Q in the viewport or the Timeline, "Pilot camera" in the camera menu and View > Camera, the Settings > Camera toggle): fly Studio's view (right drag, wheel, fly keys) while looking through a camera and the camera actor itself follows, root and FieldOfView, so you can animate it by flying. Turning it on while not looking through starts looking through first; turning it off keeps looking through. It turns off when look-through ends or the scene closes, is never saved (off each time the plugin starts), and across cuts it pilots whichever camera is live.
    • Safety: moves count only while playback is stopped and the playhead is still. While playing, scrubbing, stepping or dragging the playhead the view moves are ignored and the camera follows its animation; nothing is written.
    • One undo step per move ("Pilot CloseUp"), committed when the move settles: when the mouse button comes up, or after 0.3 s with no movement (keyboard flying, the wheel). The move shows live in between, as an unkeyed pose.
    • Keys: Pilot keys (camera menu submenu, Settings > Camera): With auto-key (default): with auto-key on a settled move keys root and FieldOfView at the playhead; with auto-key off it stays a temporary pose (K keys it, moving the playhead drops it). Only with K: moves are never keyed by themselves.
    • Refused (the reason shows in the Timeline status bar for 3 s; the Settings toggle's tooltip shows it too): no live camera ("Pilot needs a live camera. Move the playhead into a shot, or add a camera."), a locked camera actor or root track ("Unlock CloseUp to pilot it."), a muted chosen camera ("Unmute CloseUp to pilot it."), a read-only scene. A camera that gets locked while piloting turns pilot off with the same reason. In a gap pilot stays on and does nothing (the view is yours there).
    • While piloting, the chip turns amber: "Piloting CloseUp, Moves key at f40" (or "Moves are not keyed. K keys them"), the frame round the viewport turns amber, and "When you move the view" is greyed in the camera menu (it keeps its value for after). F and Ctrl+F turn the piloted camera. The press of Ctrl+Shift+Q holds the view still like Q, so it never counts as a move.
