Techniques ​

Step-by-step recipes for the things people animate most.

Land a hit on the sound ​

  1. Add the impact sound to the actor and key its Play() call near the hit.
  2. Make the sound row taller (drag the header edge) to see the waveform.
  3. Drag the hit's pose keys near the loud spike. They snap to the waveform peak (an amber line and "snap: Roar peak" show it).
  4. Drop a marker named Hit on the same frame for your damage code.

Plant a foot while the body moves ​

  1. Pick the foot and tap P to pin it.
  2. Move the hips. The leg solves to keep the foot where it is.
  3. With the foot still picked, hold P and click the floor to anchor the pin to it. The pin then rides along if the floor moves.
  4. Shift+P clears every pin.

Check and fix an arc ​

  1. Select two or more keys of a hand or prop. A motion trail runs through them, with a tick on every frame.
  2. Drag a key dot on the trail with the gizmo to reshape the arc.
  3. Hold J and slide dots along the path to retime without changing the shape. Uneven ticks mean uneven speed.

Turn about a heel or a fingertip ​

Hold Tab and hover any of a part's 27 snap points. The gizmo turns about that point until you let go, which is what you want for heel rolls and sword pivots.

Mirror half the work ​

  • Mirror posing (Shift+M) poses both sides at once.
  • Flip pose (Shift+F) swaps the pose to the other side in place.
  • Paste mirrored (Shift+N) pastes copied keys onto each joint's partner. Animate a walk's left step, then paste it mirrored half a cycle later.

Cut between cameras ​

  1. Use Add camera at view in the Cast tab for each shot (or press Shift+Q to move the selected camera to your view and key it).
  2. Press Q to look through the live camera, and drag its field of view handle to zoom.
  3. On the Camera track, add switch keys where the shot changes.
  4. To glide between shots, select a switch key and set its Blend in the Inspector (0.5 is a soft move). In the game, CameraBlendIn and CameraBlendOut glide from the player's camera into the scene and back out.

Time a VFX burst ​

  1. Add the emitter (or the model holding it) as an actor.
  2. Key its Emit() call. The burst's particles preview as you scrub.
  3. Drag the bar's grips to set that burst's particle life. It doesn't depend on the emitter's Lifetime.

Spawn a slash effect at the hand ​

  1. Select the slash model and Add selected template as a spawned actor.
  2. Set Spawn at and Lifetime, and pick the hand for Socket (or Parent to, so it follows the arm).
  3. Animate it like any actor. In a game, the runtime clones it at that frame and cleans it up after its lifetime.

A grab and slam ​

  1. Put both characters in one scene as roles, Attacker and Target, with Align set to Blend so the target slides into place.
  2. Animate both together and add a Hit marker at the impact.
  3. On the server, play it for everyone:
luau
local track = AcesMotion.PlayForAll("GrabSlam", { Attacker = attacker, Target = target })
track.MarkerReached:Connect(function(marker)
	if marker == "Hit" then
		target.Humanoid:TakeDamage(25)
	end
end)

FreezeActors (on by default for PlayForAll) holds moved NPCs on the server while the scene plays (player characters are never frozen), and CommitRoot puts them where the clients left them at the end.

Layer a scene over idle and walk ​

Play with a Weight below 1 and the scene blends over whatever the Animator is playing:

luau
AcesMotion.Play("ArmWave", { Rig = character }, { Weight = 0.6, Looped = true })

Shape many keys at once ​

Select keys across any channels and press E. The ease popover sets one progress curve for all of them. To reuse a shape, save it as a preset.
