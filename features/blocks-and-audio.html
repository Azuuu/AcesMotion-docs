Action blocks, events and audio ​

Some things in a scene happen at a moment rather than change over time: a sound plays, an emitter bursts, a trail turns on, your game code hears "Hit". AcesMotion puts these on the Timeline as calls, action blocks and audio clips, so you time them against the animation and the runtime fires them in games.

Calls: sounds and emitters ​

An object's methods can be keyed like properties. In the Inspector's Calls section, click the diamond beside a sound's Play() or Stop(), an emitter's Emit() or Clear(), a Trail's Clear(), or a VideoFrame's Play() or Pause(). Calls run while the Timeline plays (not while you scrub) and in games.

Sound bars ​

Each Play() key draws as a bar from its flag to where the sound ends, with the sound's name, a waveform and a speed chip when the speed isn't 1. A later Stop() key cuts the bar.

Right click a fade for its shape: Linear, Equal power, Slow start or S-curve. Dragging an edge, a fade or the playhead over a bar plays short audio snippets. Keys, the playhead and markers snap to a sound's loud hits ("snap: Roar peak"). View > Sound rows switches between Compact and Expanded rows.

Effect bars ​

Each Emit() key shows a lime burst bar, solid up to the particles' minimum life and fading out to their maximum, with "×20" on it. Drag its end grip or its knob to set that burst's life, with the viewport updating as you drag. An emitter that's Enabled with a Rate above 0 shows a thinner stream band; its grips set the emitter's Lifetime.

Overlapping sound and burst bars stack in up to 4 sub-lanes. Drag a bar up or down to choose its lane; right click > Reset lane lets it stack on its own again.

Action tracks and blocks ​

An actor can have any number of action tracks: right click the actor row > Add action track. Right click an empty spot of the lane > Add block to add an Event or one of your custom actions.

A block is either an instant, a chip centred on its frame, or a bar with a duration. Blocks draw in the actor's colour.

A block whose module is missing or failed to load draws dashed red with a warning icon and says why in its tooltip. It keeps its place and data, and still moves, trims and deletes. A muted track draws at 50% and its blocks don't run.

In Studio, duration blocks run as the playhead enters them and clean up as it leaves, playing or scrubbing. Instants fire only while playing, unless Settings > Actions > Fire instant actions while scrubbing is on.

Event blocks ​

An Event block is an instant your game code listens for. It reads "Event: Hit" on the Timeline. In the Inspector it has a Name and a Payload: a table of named values, each a Number, String, Boolean, Vector3 or Color3. In a game, AcesMotion.OnEvent("Hit", callback) calls your function with the track and the payload whenever an Event block named Hit fires.

Custom actions ​

Custom actions are ModuleScripts tagged AcesMotionAction, kept anywhere the server and clients can require them, such as ReplicatedStorage. The module's Params become fields in the block's Inspector panel. See Custom actions for a walkthrough and the API reference for the module contract.

The block panel shows Timing (Start, Length and Ends at, in the ruler's units), then one row per parameter. Right click a parameter row for Reset to default. With several blocks of one action selected, edits apply to all of them.

Audio lanes ​

Audio lanes hold the scene's own sounds: music, voice-over, ambience, anything that belongs to no actor. They sit under an Audio row after the last actor. Each lane has its own Mute, Lock and volume.

Add a lane with + Add > Add audio lane. Add selected sound puts the Sound or AudioPlayer selected in the Explorer on a lane at the playhead. It asks each time whether to Use as template (play that Sound itself, with its own effects) or Copy its id (take its id, volume, speed and loop, so the Sound isn't needed afterwards).

Clips work exactly like sound bars: trims, fades, loops, Ctrl speed stretch, sub-lanes, audio scrubbing and peak snapping. They select like keys, so one box can take keys and clips together and move them as one.

The clip panel ​

Select a clip for its panel: Timing, Sound (an asset id or a template), Playback (Volume as a share of the lane's, Speed, Start at and Stop at, Loop, fades) and Position (play as 2D, or 3D from a part in the cast). Select a lane's header for the lane panel: Name, Volume, Mute and Lock.

Tips ​

  • Right click a sound bar > Fit scene length to this sound to make the scene end where the sound does.
  • Turn waveforms off in View > Waveforms; fades, loops and peak snapping keep working.
  • A 2D clip plays flat. A 3D clip plays from its part, though Settings > Audio > Play sounds in 2D while animating flattens it in the editor.

Compared to Roblox's Animation Editor ​

Roblox's Animation Editor has animation events, named markers your scripts listen for, but no audio on its timeline. A sound or a particle burst has to be started from your own code at the right moment. In AcesMotion sounds, bursts, Event blocks and your own action modules sit on the same Timeline as the keys, and the runtime plays them in sync in games.
