AcesFX effects ​

AcesFX is a plugin for building particle effects: bursts, held auras, trails, light flashes, lightning, sounds and camera shake, set up with attributes on the effect's own parts. When an AcesFX effect sits inside an actor, AcesMotion can play it from the Timeline, scrub it frame by frame, and play it in your game at the same moments.

What you need ​

You need the AcesFX runtime in your place. AcesFX's Import runtime puts it in ReplicatedStorage and tags it AcesFXRuntime; AcesMotion finds it by that tag wherever it lives, and picks the newest when there are several. Keep it where clients can reach it (ReplicatedStorage, for example): in ServerStorage, players won't see the effects, and the Inspector warns you.

Without a runtime the AcesFX blocks draw dashed red with the reason in their tooltip. They keep their settings and work again as soon as the runtime is back.

The four blocks ​

BlockKindWhat it does
EmitinstantFires every part's one-time version: bursts, counted streams, trails and beams for the length of the Emit, flashes, one-shot sounds. It never switches anything on or off.
EnableinstantSwitches the held parts on: plain emitters stream at their Rate, trails and beams with no duration draw, looped sounds loop.
DisableinstantWinds the held parts down the way AcesFX does: the stop delay, the release burst, then the fade.
EnabledbarEnable at its start and Disable at its end. Trim it by its edges.

Before the first Enable or Disable, every part starts as it was authored, so an aura that's already on stays on. A key on a part's Enabled counts as a change at its own frame, and the last change wins.

Adding one ​

Right click an empty spot on an actor's action lane and choose Add block > AcesFX. The group shows only while an AcesFX runtime is in the place. If you select a part of the actor in the Explorer first, the new block targets it; otherwise it targets the whole actor. The command palette also has Add AcesFX Emit, Enable, Disable and Enabled at playhead.

The effect can live in the actor itself (a sword with a slash effect, a rig with an aura on its hands) or be a spawned actor whose template is the effect, which then plays where the spawned copy is.

In the Inspector ​

Select a block to edit it:

SettingWhat it does
TargetThe group or part of the actor the effect lives on. Blank uses the whole actor. On a whole rig, only Sounds that carry AcesFX settings play, so a character's own footsteps stay quiet.
ScaleScales the effect's sizes, speeds and values. 1 plays it as authored.
Keep playing if stoppedWhen the scene is stopped early, this block is left to finish instead of being cancelled. Off by default.
TargetsOne row per lightning target the effect declares. Pick the actor part each bolt should reach; left blank, the effect aims where it was authored.

Under them a summary reads what the block fires and for how long ("3 emitters, 1 trail · 0.9 s"), and Select in AcesFX selects the effect so AcesFX's own window shows it. When nothing in the target fires, the summary turns yellow and tells you to pick another part.

Scrubbing and playback in Studio ​

Scrub onto a block and the viewport shows the effect as it plays at that frame, forward or back:

  • bursts and streams as frozen particles, and riders (Curve, Orbit, Wander) on their paths;
  • light flashes and flicker, texture swaps, beams switching on and off;
  • lightning bolts re-striking, and sub-effects at the spot they spawn;
  • trails as ribbons drawn from where their attachments were;
  • camera shake, while you look through a camera;
  • sounds as playback passes them, and short snippets while you scrub.

AcesMotion asks the AcesFX runtime in the place how the effect plays, so the preview follows the effect's own settings. Edit the effect in AcesFX or the Properties window and the preview and the tails update. Nothing the preview draws is keyed or saved.

Playing forward at 1x, plain bursts and streams switch to the real emitters, as AcesMotion's own particles do. While AcesFX's own window previews an effect, AcesMotion leaves those parts alone, with a note in the Inspector.

Timing tails ​

Each AcesFX block draws a tail behind it in the effects' lime, so you can time the effect against the animation without scrubbing:

PieceMeans
BodySomething of the effect is alive. Solid while every particle lives, then fading as the shorter lives end.
End capWhere the last particle is gone.
TickA burst at that moment (a burst on the chip's own frame is the chip).
StripeA part keeps emitting: a stream, a trail or beam for the Emit's length, a held part at its Rate.
Held bandAn Enable's held parts are on, up to the change that ends them.
Wind-downAfter a Disable or an Enabled bar's end: the stop delay, the release burst and the fade, paler.

Tails can't be dragged; a click goes to the block or the lane under them. Their ends and bursts are snap targets for keys, markers and other blocks ("Emit: Sword tail end", "Emit: Sword burst").

In your game ​

The runtime plays AcesFX blocks through your game's AcesFX runtime, on each client. Server plays skip them, since the server's writes would replicate over every client's own.

  • The held state follows the scene: a jump, a loop or a late join lands on the state at the new time, and Emits jumped over don't fire.
  • Effects follow the scene's SetSpeed and Pause.
  • When the scene ends, every part goes back to how it was. A natural end lets fired Emits play out, and a spawned effect actor stays until its last particle is gone. Stop cancels what hasn't fired yet, except blocks with Keep playing if stopped.
  • Effects = false skips every AcesFX block, and EffectFired fires for each Emit, Enable and Disable played.

Everything in one list ​

AcesFX blocks ​

  • Four blocks on an actor's action rows play the AcesFX effects inside that actor, or inside a spawned actor whose template is an effect, right where they are: Emit fires every part's one-time version (bursts, counted streams, trail windows, flashes, one-shot sounds) and never turns anything on or off; Enable and Disable switch the held parts (plain emitters, trails and beams with no duration, looped sounds) on and off, winding them down AcesFX's way; Enabled is a span, on at its start and off at its end. Each block's Target is the whole actor or any group or part inside it; Scale sizes the effect; Targets aims lightning at actor parts; Keep playing if stopped lets it finish when the scene is stopped.
  • In games the runtime plays them through the game's AcesFX runtime (a ModuleScript tagged AcesFXRuntime, anywhere clients can require it), on clients only. Without one the blocks do nothing (warned once).
  • The held state follows the scene: the last change before the playhead wins, whether it's an Enable, a Disable, an Enabled span or a key on a part's Enabled; before the first change, parts already switched on are taken over so riders and flicker run on them. A jump, a loop or a late join lands on the state at the new time; Emits jumped over don't fire.
  • Speed, Pause and time travel reach the effects the scene started, so a 2x attack plays its slash twice as fast.
  • At the end parts go back to how they were before the scene: held parts wind down, parts a Disable turned off come back. A natural end lets every fired Emit play out (a spawned explosion stays until its last particle is gone); Stop() cancels what hasn't fired yet, except blocks with Keep playing if stopped.
  • A whole rig as the Target fires only Sounds that carry AcesFX settings, so a character's footsteps never play.
  • PlayOption Effects = false skips every AcesFX block. Signal EffectFired(blockId, kind, handle) fires for each Emit, Enable and Disable played (kind is "Emit", "Enable" or "Disable").
  • A part's Enabled keys under a held block's target are read as held-state changes, never written as a property.

In Studio ​

  • Add block > AcesFX lists the four blocks while an AcesFX runtime is in the place (tagged AcesFXRuntime, or the older untagged ReplicatedStorage.AcesFX). A new block targets the part selected in the Explorer when it's inside the actor, else the whole actor. The command palette has Add AcesFX Emit / Enable / Disable / Enabled at playhead (no keys bound).
  • Scrubbing shows the effect as it plays at the frame you're on, forward or back. AcesMotion asks the AcesFX runtime in the place how the effect plays, then draws it itself: bursts and streams as frozen particles, riders (Curve, Orbit, Wander) on their paths, light flashes and flicker, texture swaps, beams switching on and off, lightning bolts re-striking, trails as stand-in ribbons, and sub-effects at the spot they spawn. While looking through a camera, AcesFX camera shake plays on the view. Sounds play as the playhead passes them and as short snippets while you scrub. Nothing AcesMotion draws is saved or keyed.
  • Playing forward at 1x, plain bursts and streams hand off to the real emitter, as AcesMotion's own particles do. Riders and everything else stay drawn by AcesMotion.
  • Timing tails draw behind each AcesFX block in the effects' lime: an Emit's body runs from the chip to its last particle (solid while every particle lives, then fading to a faint lime, never to nothing), a solid end cap marks where the last particle is gone, ticks mark later bursts and a stripe under it marks parts that keep emitting. An Enable draws the held band to the change that ends it; a Disable, and an Enabled bar's end, draw the wind-down (stop delay, release burst, fade) at 70%. Tails follow edits to the effect, the block or AcesFX itself, can't be dragged, and their ends and bursts are snap targets ("Emit: Sword tail end", "Emit: Sword burst").
  • The Inspector shows Target (the actor part picker, blank for the whole actor), Scale, Keep playing if stopped, a Targets row per lightning target the effect declares ("Effect's own" when left blank), a summary of what fires ("3 emitters, 1 trail · 0.9 s"; "Nothing in Rock fires with Emit" when nothing does) and Select in AcesFX, which selects the effect so AcesFX's window shows it.
  • When the runtime is missing, too old or too new, the blocks draw in the failed look with the reason in their tooltip and Inspector card, keep their settings, and work again once it's back. A runtime clients can't reach (in ServerStorage, say) or one without the tag gets a note.
  • While AcesFX's own window previews an effect in place, AcesMotion leaves those parts alone: it draws no particles there, holds and writes nothing, and doesn't record AcesFX's changes as keys. The Inspector says so.
  • View > Trails while scrubbing and Settings > Effects turn the trail stand-ins off; the AcesFX drawing follows View > Effect preview.

Linked settings ​

  • An AcesFX setting can follow another one (AcesFX's links: a Duration following a Ride time, say). When a scene keys a setting that others follow, AcesMotion writes the followers too, the way AcesFX does: a number follows as the source times the link's Scale plus its Offset, anything else is copied, and a chain of links settles. This works while scrubbing and playing in Studio and in games. A follower whose source is gone keeps its own value.
  • In Studio the followers come back to their own values with the source when the preview ends, and the effect isn't marked changed.
  • You can key a follower too: its own keys win over the link while the scene plays, and what follows it follows those keys.
  • A linked setting's Timeline row shows a link icon, and its tooltip and the Inspector row's tooltip say what it follows ("AcesFX links this to Core's RideTime (times 2 plus 1)").
