The Library ​

The Library holds ready-made settings you can drop into a scene: eases, generators, constraints, drivers, Motion Warps and poses. Each tab has built-in presets plus the ones you save yourself, so a camera shake or a look-at-player aim you tuned once is a double click away in every scene.

Opening it ​

The Library is a tab in the Hub, next to the Inspector. Like any Hub tab it can pop out into its own window (right click the tab > Pop out). Inside it, the tabs are Easing, Generators, Constraints, Drivers, Warps and Poses, with a Search box that filters the open tab. When the tabs don't all fit, the strip scrolls sideways with the mouse wheel.

Adding a preset ​

Every tab except Easing and Poses adds blocks to Timeline rows, and they all work the same way:

ToDo
Pick a presetClick it
Add it to the selected Timeline rows at the playheadDouble click it, or press Add to selected rows
Add it to one row at a frameDrag it from the Library onto a row in the Timeline
Rename or delete one of your ownRight click it

Adding keeps you on the Library tab so the next add is one click. The new blocks are selected, the Inspector tab gets its dot, and the status bar says "Added 2 blocks. The Inspector tab shows them." Turn on Settings > Timeline > Show added blocks in the Inspector to jump to the Inspector instead.

Rows a preset can't drive are skipped, and an actor row takes the preset on its root. A preset that aims at or reads a player, the player's camera or a tagged object uses the scene's actor of that kind. When the scene has none, a card offers to add one first.

Easing ​

The ease presets from the Ease view, under Built in and Saved. Double click a card or press Apply to selection to put it on the selected keys' segments. See Easing and curves.

Generators ​

Built in lists the Screen effects group, then each generator with its description: Shake, Wiggle, Oscillate, Flicker / Strobe / Pulse, Spin, Recoil, Overshoot / Settle, Stepped, Hold / Fade, Cycle / Flipbook and Typewriter. Click one to open its presets, each with its settings in a line ("Amplitude 0.40, 6 Hz, no decay"). The Screen effects group (Fade to black, Fade from black, Flash white, Letterbox, Vignette pulse) always goes on the scene's screen effects at the playhead, whatever is selected, and adds the screen effects first when the scene has none. See Generators.

Constraints ​

Built in lists Damped Track, Locked Track, Horizon Lock, Face Motion, Delay and Floor. The built-in presets include Look at player (aims at the local player's head, fading out between 5 and 15 studs), Turret, Level horizon, Bank into turns, Echo and Feet on the floor (Floor on the feet, resolved by bending the limbs). They go on the selected root and joint rows; property and channel rows are skipped. See Constraints, drivers and Motion Warp.

Drivers ​

PresetWhat it does
Dolly zoomA camera's FieldOfView goes from 70 to 30 as a target moves 5 to 40 studs away
Proximity glowBrightness goes from 3 to 0 as the nearest player comes within 20 studs
Speed trailA Trail's width goes from 0 to 2 with speed
Engine pitchA sound's PlaybackSpeed goes from 0.8 to 2 with speed, smoothed over 4 frames
Match colourCopies Color from another Color

Drivers added from the Library cover the whole scene.

Warps ​

Projectiles fly an actor to its target: Fireball lob (a Lob at 30 studs a second), Homing missile (follows the target) and Coin magnet (straight, at 40 studs a second). Reaching bends a joint chain toward it: Crane claw, Dragon bite and Tentacle. A warp preset goes on the first selected row and picks its target the way a new warp does. A reaching preset on an actor or root row reaches with the rig's right hand.

Poses ​

Your pose library lives in your plugin settings, so each animator has their own. Poses that fit the selected rig are listed by group, each card drawn as a stick figure. Poses made on a rig that doesn't share at least half their joints sit dimmed under Other rigs.

ToDo
Preview a poseHover its card (moving off puts the rig back)
Apply itClick (Shift mirrored, Ctrl to the whole actor)
Blend toward itAlt-drag across the card, left edge 0%, right edge 100%
Save the current poseSave pose, then type its name
Assign a slotRight click > Assign to slot (1 to 9)
Apply a slotAlt+1 to Alt+9 over the viewport or the Timeline
Find a pose by nameCtrl+Alt+P opens the quick pose picker at the pointer

Applying a pose works like posing by hand: it keys with Auto-key on, goes on the active layer, and is one undo step. It sets the picked joints, else every selected rig, else the scene's only rig. Deleting a pose or group shows an Undo button in the status line for 6 seconds, since plugin settings aren't in Studio's undo.

Sharing presets ​

Export presets... opens a window where you tick what to share: ease, generator, constraint and driver presets, poses, and the open scene's saved colours, gradients and sequences. Export puts them in a folder in ServerStorage and selects it. Right click it > Save to File... to send it as a .rbxm or .rbxmx.

To bring presets in, insert the file into the place, select it in the Explorer and press Import presets. Taken names get a number. Nothing in a preset file is ever run.

Tips ​

  • Deleting a saved preset asks first: presets live in your plugin settings, outside Ctrl+Z.
  • Save as preset... in any generator, constraint, driver or warp block menu opens the new preset in the Library with its name ready to type.

Compared to Roblox's Animation Editor ​

Roblox's Animation Editor has no generators, constraints, drivers or Motion Warp, so it has no presets for them either. The Library's Generators, Constraints, Drivers and Warps tabs have no counterpart there.
