Constraints, drivers and Motion Warp ​

These three kinds of block set a value from something else in the scene instead of from keys alone. A constraint moves an actor's root or a joint: aim a camera at a character, keep a head inside its limits, plant a foot. A driver sets any value from a measurement, such as a camera's FieldOfView from how far a character is. A Motion Warp bends an actor's path so it lands on its real target, wherever a game puts it.

All three sit on a row's Stack lane with generators. A row's blocks apply top to bottom, each on the result of the ones above.

Constraints ​

Right click a root or joint row's header, or its lane's empty space, and choose Add constraint:

GroupConstraints
TrackTrack To, Damped Track, Locked Track
CopyCopy Location, Copy Rotation, Copy Transforms
LimitLimit Location, Limit Rotation, Limit Distance
RelationshipFloor, Lock (on a joint row), Child Of
MotionDelay, Face Motion, Horizon Lock
CustomYour own ModuleScripts tagged AcesMotionConstraint (see Custom constraints)

A new constraint spans the keys selected on that row. With none selected it spans a camera's current shot, or runs from where you clicked to the scene end. Its target is the Explorer selection when that's part of the cast; otherwise you click the target in the viewport, or press Esc to pick one later in the Inspector. Constraints can't go on property rows, since they drive positions and rotations.

Blocks draw in mint with their target in the label ("Track To · r2 · Head"). A block with no target reads "No target" and does nothing. A missing target draws as failed ("Head missing"), and a target that depends on what it drives draws "Loop, ignored".

Lock holds a hand or foot where it was at the lock's first frame while the limb above bends to keep it there: a planted foot while the hips move, a hand on a rail. Give it a target and the part sticks to that target instead. Select keys on a hand or foot row and right click > Lock, or pick a part in the viewport and press Shift+L. Posing a locked part keys the lock's Spot, so one lock can cover the whole timeline.

The constraint panel ​

Select a constraint block for its panel: Timing, Target (the part list, a viewport pick button and Use selection), Settings for its kind, Blend and Preset.

  • -Z Front in the axis dropdowns is Roblox's front.
  • A target can be a Point instead of a part: a spot with X, Y and Z key diamonds that you move with the gizmo.
  • Distance fade (in Blend) keeps the constraint at full strength within Full within studs of what it measures to and fades it out by None beyond.
  • Influence, Roll, Delay and Bank have key diamonds.

The Stack section, at the end of an actor's or joint's properties, lists every block on the row in the order it runs. Drag an entry up or down to reorder it, or click its eye to mute it.

Drivers ​

Right click a row's header or its lane's empty space and choose Add driver:

DriverReads
CopyOne property's whole value, or a joint's pose or placement
DistanceHow far apart two things are
AxisOne axis of an object's position or rotation
SpeedHow fast something moves or turns
PropertyA property or attribute

Drivers go on any row a generator takes, including one channel of a joint or vector. A new driver covers the whole scene. When you add it nothing changes yet: Out Low and Out High both start at the row's current value. Set them to the range it should drive between.

Driver blocks draw in tangerine, labelled with what they read and what they drive ("Distance · r2 > FieldOfView").

The driver panel ​

The Source section picks what it reads, with Now showing the source at the playhead ("23.4 studs"). Map sets In Low and In High (each with a Capture button: scrub to a frame and capture the source there), Out Low and Out High, a Shape from the ease presets, and Clamp and Invert. On a whole Color3, a colour ramp edited in the gradient editor takes the place of Out Low and Out High.

The Mapping graph plots source against output. Drag a dashed range line to change that range; Shift drags both ends. Smooth averages the source over a number of frames.

Motion Warp ​

You animate the trip once against the stand-ins. In a game the path is re-fitted between where it really starts and where the target really is, so a fireball still lands on a unit three times further away and an arc stays an arc at any range.

Add it from an actor's or root row's menu: Add Motion Warp. It covers the selected root keys, else the next half second; with no root keys yet it keys both ends so it flies at once. On a joint row it bends a chain so that joint reaches the target, for a claw, a bite or a tentacle.

The panel's Path section sets Starts from and Lands on, and whether it follows a moving target or aims where it was at launch. Path shape switches on a live shape (Straight, Lob, High arc, Spiral, Zig-zag) in place of your keyed path; Bake shape to keys writes it into the root keys. Arc and Arc turn are keyable. Travel time keeps the keyed length or times the trip By speed, within a Min and Max.

To ease the trip, select the warp bar with no keys selected and press E.

Tips ​

  • Bake to keys in a block's menu writes the result as keys and removes the block, along with every block above it on the row that overlaps it. The row header's Bake stack bakes the whole stack.
  • Baking a block that follows something the game fills in (the local player, a tagged object) asks first, since baking freezes it to the stand-in.
  • To write your own constraint, choose Add constraint > New custom constraint... for a typed, commented template. Custom constraints walks through it.
  • A native export bakes a rig's constrained joints and root into its animation, and plays a Motion Warp as it is for the stand-ins. Constraints on other actors play with the runtime.

Compared to Roblox's Animation Editor ​

Roblox's Animation Editor has IK for posing, but no constraints, drivers or Motion Warp on its timeline: everything it plays comes from keys on one rig. In AcesMotion a head can keep aiming at another actor, a camera's lens can respond to distance, and a projectile's path can re-fit to wherever its target stands when the game plays the scene.
